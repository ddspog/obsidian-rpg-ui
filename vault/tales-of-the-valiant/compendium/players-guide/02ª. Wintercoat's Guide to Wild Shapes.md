# Wintercoat's Guide to Wild Shapes
_Complete transcription of the "**D&D** Wintercoat's Guide to Wild Shapes" book by **Two Dicey Bards**._

![[wintercoats-guide-to-wild-shape-preface.webp|right|384]] _The group of druids gathers in a circle as the wolf pack surrounds them. A massive winter wolf, the pack leader, moves towards the center of the gathering. He grins menacingly before a deep howl, revealing his huge frosted fangs. As he sits, he reverts to his true form: the ancient druid, the master of this grove, Aela Wintercoat._

Druids are powerful spellcasters, deeply connected to the natural world. They possess the ability to shape the land and themselves, making them one of the most sought out classes in all of D&D. A classic ability that druids have shared throughout the ages called Wild Shape. Wild Shape allows a creature controlled by the player to transform into a beast, gaining their strengths and becoming a force to be reckoned with.

Many players choose to have a personal theme for the wild shapes their druid possesses. They can choose beasts from their homeland, beasts they have seen in adventures, or stick with a specific kind of beasts such as insects, reptiles, felines, etc. However, when leveling up, especially when you get access to higher CR beasts, the options decrease drastically either because the campaign doesn’t have higher level beasts in it or there simply aren't enough beasts in the game. For example, the *D&D* **Monster Manual** only has three Challenge Rate 3 beasts: [[Allosaurus]], [[Giant Scorpion]], and [[Killer Whale]]. These aren't the sort of beast an adventurer would encounter on a daily basis.

If you have a [[Druid]] character or a player in your group and you're looking for more options, this is for you. As a DM you'll save a lot of time in preparing specific beast encounters, and as a player, you'll have the resources to build the beast you always wanted.
# New Class Feature
This homebrew offers a new class feature you can use as a druid. Although it may be more powerful for [[Shifter]] druids, it may be used by any druid that possesses the Wild Shape feature. Consulting with your DM is essential to make the most out of this new wild shape system.
## Amalgam
Whenever you choose a new Known Form, you can choose a [[beast]] with a CR (Challenge Rating) at least one level lower than the maximum available to you and increase its CR to the highest available to you.

For each level up in CR level, do the following: 
- Increase the total amount of hit dice by 1.
- Gain one Amalgam Trait. Also, gain an additional ability score improvement at CR 1/8, 1/4, 1, and 2.

At the end of leveling up, calculate your hit points with the formula below. You may use the hit die average instead of rolling. 
- **(Hit Die + Constitution modifier) x Number of Hit Dice**

```rpg table.traits-learned-by-cr-level-table
|CR|Features|
|---|---|
|1/8 and 1/4|Gain 1 trait and 1 ability score improvement|
|1/2|Gain 1 trait|
|1|Gain 1 trait and 1 ability score improvement|
|2|Gain 1 trait|
|3|Gain 1 trait and 1 ability score improvement|
|4 to 6|Gain 1 trait|

```
# Step-by-Step Guide
![[wintercoats-guide-to-wild-shapes-step-by-step.webp|right|256]] This module represents a new set of rules and it can be challenging to make a beast of your own for the first time. You should follow these suggestions for an easy and fun time leveling up your beasts:
- Begin with the beast's original stat block and always keep it as is by your side. As you edit it, keep the changes separate from the original stats (for example, keep the ability score improvements separate from the base stats).
- The first step in your level-up should always be adding the extra hit die. Then move on to the ability score improvements.
- Pick the trait you want and follow its indications. Do it step by step whenever the trait increases or affects more than one thing. For example, the Growth trait is powerful but affects multiple things:
	- Increasing all your hit dice.
	- Your base Strength score can be affected if it's under the minimum to the respective new size, so add any past ability score improvements in Strength to the new minimum score.
	- Lastly, increase the damage dice of all your natural weapons at the moment you choose this trait.
- Lastly, calculate your hit points using the formula in page 5. For example, if a beast has 4d6 hit points and a +1 Constitution modifier, it would have (1d6+1) x 4 total hit points, 16 on average.
# Trait List
Amalgam Traits are abilities, actions, and aspects a beast can have. Many traits have prerequisites to be available as an option. The prerequisites might limit you from choosing a trait more than once, exclude it if you already have another trait, require another trait or require the beast to be a specific CR.

In this section, you will find a small abbreviation of each type of trait. Check the following pages for detailed descriptions and more options:
- **Ability Score Improvement.** Increase your Dexterity, Constitution, and Strength scores.
- **Environmental Adaptation.** Gain a special movement or adaptation from a list, like flying speed and underwater breathing.
- **Growth.** Grow the beast by one size, increasing its hit points and damage potential.
- **Heightened Senses.** Gain one special sense from the list, like Tremorsense or Darkvision.
- **Precision.** Substitute the beast's proficiency bonus for your own, improving attacks, skill checks, and saving throws.
- **Skilled.** Gain proficiency in two skills of your choice from a list.
- **New Natural Weapon.** Gain a new natural weapon.
- **Beast Styles.** Choose between multiple attacks, one powerful attack or defense. At CR 1, gain the Multiattack or the Hardhitter feature. At higher CRs, you can choose an extra feature or improve an existing one.
- **Fighting Trait.** When you choose this, you gain or change a basic or advanced trait according to your CR. These can be a poisonous bite, a charge attack, or the flyby ability.

````rpg rule.tab
### Ability Score Improvement
![[tiger-with-constitution-improvement.webp|right|384]] Increase the beast’s Strength, Dexterity, or Constitution scores by 2, or increase two of these scores by 1, to a maximum of 20. This affects their skills, attacks, saving throws, and DCs as normal. An increase in the beast’s Constitution modifier increases its maximum hit points by 1 per hit dice it has.

In case the rules or stat blocks aren't clear, consider the following lines as true:
- An increase in the beast’s Strength modifier increases the beast’s natural weapon attacks and damage by 1.
- An increase in the beast’s Dexterity modifier increases its AC by 1.
- If the creature has an ability that forces a target to make a saving throw, such as a poison sting or a pounce attack, the DC also increases by 1. Work with your DM to decide if it increases with your Strength, Dexterity, or Constitution modifier.
````

````rpg rule.tab
### Growth
![[giant-toad-grown-to-gargatuan-size.webp|right|384]] *Prerequisite: A creature can only increase in size if it has the appropriate CR, according to the table below.*

As your druidic powers grow, so do your beasts. When you choose this feature, a beast of your choice grows by 1 size.

```rpg table.growth
|CR|Maximum Size|Minimum Strength|
|---|---|---|
|0|Tiny|1|
|1/8|Small|4|
|1/4|Medium|7|
|1|Large|10|
|3|Huge|13|
|6|Gargantuan|16|

```
- The beast's hit dice increase by one step: 1d4 to 1d6, 1d6 to 1d8, and so on to a maximum of 1d20. Whenever you increase your hit dice step, your hit point maximum increases by 1 hit point per hit dice the beast has at the current CR level. If increasing to 1d20, gain 4 hit points per hit dice instead.
- If your beast's current Strength score is lower than the one on the table, it is instead the value listed above for the respective size. Treat this as the base score for any ability score improvements to Strength.
- Your natural weapons' damage dice increase by one step: 1 damage increases to 1d4, 1d4 to 1d6, and so on to a maximum of 1d12. The same applies to natural weapons that deal more than one damage dice; 2d4 increases to 2d6, up to a maximum of 2d12. For example, the wolf’s bite weapon deals 2d4+2 piercing damage and by choosing this feature it becomes 2d6+2 piercing damage.
````

```rpg rule.tab
### Precision
You substitute the beast's proficiency bonus for your own. Most beasts have a bonus of 2, and at CR 5 or more have a bonus of 3. This change affects the beast’s attacks, proficient saving throws, proficient skills, and natural DCs, increasing whenever your proficiency bonus does.
### Skilled
Gain proficiency in 2 skills of your choice from [[Acrobatics]], [[Athletics]], [[Intimidation]], [[Perception]], [[Stealth]], or [[Survival]]. You can select this feature multiple times.
```

## Beast Styles
*Prerequisites: Up to CR 1 you can only have one basic style, at CR 2 onwards you can have both options. You only gain access to one advanced style at CR3.*

When you choose this trait, you gain a specialization for your natural weapons. Choose one of the basic, advanced or defensive styles according to their prerequisites.

In this section, you can find choices for your beast’s natural weapons and defenses. You can focus on getting more attacks, making a single attack stronger, hardening your hide, or improving your reflexes. At higher CR you get access to advanced traits that strengthen your beasts even further.

```rpg rule.tab
*Prerequisite: You can only have one basic style option. At CR 2 or more, you can have both.*
#### Multiattack
![[hawk-with-multiattack-basic-style.webp|right|284]] *Prerequisite: the beast must have more than one natural weapon and can't have Charge.*

As an action on your turn, you can attack with one of your natural weapons and make another attack with a different natural weapon.

*Prerequisite: The beast must have a natural weapon with only one damage dice.*

Choose one of your natural weapons that only has one damage dice. Add one weapon dice to it, increasing its damage roll to two damage dice. For example, choosing this trait for a panther increases the bite attack that deals 1d6+2 piercing damage to 2d6+2 piercing damage.
```

In this section, you can see the main fighting traits most beasts have. Fighting traits are the signature abilities beasts use during fights. A beast can only have fighting traits from the same line from the given table. They are divided into basic and advanced fighting traits and are only accessible by beasts with a minimum CR of 1/8 and 1, respectively. When you choose this trait:
- If your beast has none of the listed traits, it gains the basic trait of a line of your choice.
- If the beast already has a line's basic trait, you gain one of the advanced traits if it's at least CR1.

```rpg rule.tab
name: Basic
icon: footprints
color: var(--color-green)
---
### Basic Fighting Traits
![[hawk-with-charge-feature.webp|right|384]] This is the first fighting style your beast wild shapes can get. This allows for your giant beetle to charge and your wolf to bite with venomous fangs. Choose one of the following:
#### Achilles Strike
*Prerequisite: The beast must have a natural weapon that deals piercing or slashing damage.*

Choose one of your natural weapons. When you hit a creature with an attack using this weapon, it must succeed on a Strength saving throw (10 + beast's Strength modifier) or be knocked prone. Alternatively, if you have the Precision trait, the DC is 8 + your proficiency bonus + the beast’s Strength modifier.
#### Charge
*Prerequisite: The beast can't have the Multiattack feature.*

Choose one natural weapon. If the beast moves at least 20 feet straight toward a target and then hits with the chosen weapon’s attack in the same turn, the target takes extra bludgeoning damage equal to the weapon's damage in dice. If the target is a creature, it must succeed on a Strength saving throw (DC equals 10 + the beast's Strength modifier) or be knocked prone. Alternatively, if you have the Precision trait, the DC is equal to 8 + your proficiency bonus + the beast’s Strength modifier. For example, if your beast's tail attacks deal 2d8+3 bludgeoning damage, when you use the Charge feature you deal an extra 2d8 bludgeoning damage if you hit with the attack.
#### Flyby
*Prerequisite: Must have a flying speed or the high jump feature.*

You don’t provoke opportunity attacks when flying or jumping out of an enemy’s reach.
#### Grapple
*Prerequisite: Must have a claw, bite or constrict attack.*

Choose one of the beast’s natural weapons according to the prerequisite. On a hit, if the target is of the same size or smaller than the beast, it becomes grappled. The escape DC is equal to 10 + the beast’s Strength modifier. Alternatively, if you have the Precision trait, the DC is equal to 8 + your proficiency bonus + the beast’s Strength modifier.
#### Venomous
*Prerequisite: Must have a natural weapon that deals piercing or slashing damage.*

One of your natural weapons is now a source of venom. When you hit with an attack using this weapon, the target must make a Constitution saving throw (DC equals 10 + the beast’s Constitution modifier), taking a d6 in poison damage a number of times equal to 2 + the beast's Constitution modifier. The target takes full damage on a failed save, and half as much on a successful one. Alternatively, if you have the Precision trait, the DC is 8 + your proficiency bonus + the beast’s Constitution modifier and you roll a d6 a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus + the beast’s Constitution modifier.
```
```rpg rule.tab
name: Advanced
icon: building
color: var(--color-red)
---
### Advanced Fighting Traits
![[pouncing-bear.webp|right|384]] This is the last step of your fighting style, as you hone your abilities. Give your giant wasp a dive attack, or your panther pack tactics to fight in a group.

If you have a basic fighting trait and are at least CR 1, you can choose one of the following advanced fighting traits. In some cases, beasts have abilities similar to basic fighting traits in their stat blocks. You may count those as prerequisites for the purpose of choosing an advanced fighting trait.
#### Constrict
*Prerequisite: The beast must have a weapon attack that, on a hit, grapples the target.*

A creature grappled by one of your natural weapon attacks becomes restrained. You can’t use the same weapon to attack or grapple a different target. The escape DC is 10 + the beast’s Strength modifier. Alternatively, if you have the Precision trait, the DC is 8 + your proficiency bonus + the beast’s Strength modifier.
#### Dive Attack
*Prerequisite: The beast must have the Flyby feature.*

If the beast is flying and dives at least 30 feet toward a target and then hits with a melee weapon attack, the attack deals extra damage equal to twice the weapon's damage in dice. For example, if your beast's beak attacks deal 3d6+3 piercing damage, when you use the Dive Attack feature you deal an extra 6d6 piercing damage if you hit with the attack.
#### Pack Tactics
*Prerequisite: The beast must have an attack that can prone its target.*

The beast has advantage on an attack roll against a creature if at least one ally is within 5 feet of the creature and the ally isn't incapacitated.
#### Pounce
*Prerequisite: The beast must have an attack that can prone its target.*

If the beast moves at least 20 feet straight toward a creature and then hits it with an attack on the same turn, that target must succeed on a Strength saving throw (DC equals 10 + beast’s Strength modifier) or be knocked prone. If the target is prone, you can make one weapon attack with a different natural weapon against the target as a bonus action. Alternatively, if you have the Precision trait, the DC is 8 + your proficiency bonus + the beast’s Strength modifier.
#### Deadly Poison
*Prerequisite: The beast must have a weapon attack that deals poison damage.*

Your poison is now incredibly potent, and few creatures can endure it. When rolling poison damage for an effect that forces a saving throw, like in the Venomous feature, increase the dice from d6 to d10.
#### Swallow
*Prerequisite: The beast must have a bite attack that, on a hit, grapples the target.*

When a beast makes a bite attack against a creature one size smaller than it that it is already grappling and the attack hits, the target is swallowed, and the grapple ends. The swallowed target is blinded and restrained, it has total cover against attacks and other effects outside the beast, and it takes a d6 in acid damage a number of times equal to 2 + the beast's Constitution modifier at the start of each of your turns. You can have only one target swallowed at a time. If you die, a swallowed creature is no longer restrained by you and can escape from the corpse using 5 feet of movement, exiting prone. Alternatively, if you have the Precision trait, instead roll 1d6 of acid damage a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus + the beast's Constitution modifier.
#### Trampling Charge
*Prerequisite: The beast must have the Charge Trait and be huge or larger in size.*

Your massive size gives you a tremendous advantage against fallen foes. If a target is already prone, the beast can make one melee weapon attack against it as a bonus action.
````
