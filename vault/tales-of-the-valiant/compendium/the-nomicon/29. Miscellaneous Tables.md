# Taverns
When naming taverns, it is important to vary the formula, or the names will sound repetitious.
- **Formula 1**: The \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-3 Short Tavern Items 1|Table 29-3]] or [[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-4 Short Tavern Items 2|29-4]]: Short Tavern Items] or the \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-5: Long Tavern Items|Table 29-5: Long Tavern Items]]\]. Examples: “The Chime” or “The Medallion.”
- **Formula 2**: The \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-8: Short Tavern Item Adjective|Table 29-8: Short Tavern Item Adjective]]\] \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-3 Short Tavern Items 1|Table 29-3]] or [[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-4 Short Tavern Items 2|29-4]]: Short Tavern Items]. Examples: “The Bronze Heart,” “The Fragrant Cask”.
- **Formula 3**: The \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-7 Short Tavern Living Adjective|Table 29-7: Short Tavern Living Adjective]]\] \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-9: Short Tavern Animal|Table 29-9: Short Tavern Animal]]\]. Examples: “The Climbing Cat,” “The Stone Camel.”
- **Formula 4**: The \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-11: Tavern Person|Table 29-11: Tavern Person]]\]’s \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-3 Short Tavern Items 1|Table 29-3]] or [[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-4 Short Tavern Items 2|29-4]] (Short Tavern Items 1 or 2), or [[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-5 Long Tavern Items|Table 29-5]] (Long Tavern Items)]. Examples: “The Baron’s Medallion,” “The Herdsman’s Doublet.”
- **Formula 5**: The \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-11 Tavern Person|Table 29-11: Tavern Person]]\]’s \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-9 Short Tavern Animal|Table 29-9: Short Tavern Animal]]\]. Examples: “The Druid’s Griffon,” “The Warrior’s Hound.”
- **Formula 6**: The \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-11 Tavern Person|Table 29-11: Tavern Person]]\]’s \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-12 Tavern Abstract Item|Table 29-12: Tavern Abstract Item]]\]. Examples: “The Knight’s Riddle,” “The Weaver’s Long Walk.”
- **Formula 7**: The \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-9 Short Tavern Animal|Table 29-9: Short Tavern Animal]]\]’s \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-12 Tavern Abstract Item|Table 29-12: Tavern Abstract Item]]\]. Examples: “The Cat’s Riddle,” “the Peacock’s Shadow.”
- **Formula 8**: The [Table [[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-3 Short Tavern Items 1|Table 29-3]] or [[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-4 Short Tavern Items 2|29-4]] (Short Tavern Items)] and \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-9 Short Tavern Animal|Table 29-9: Short Tavern Animal]]\]. Examples: “The Map and Minnow,” “The Broom and Badger.” These are usually alliterations. They can also be reversed in order, if that sounds better.
- **Formula 9**: The \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-11 Tavern Person|Table 29-11: Tavern Person]]\] and \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-11 Tavern Person|Table 29-11: Tavern Person]]\]. Examples: “The Knight and Queen,” “The King and Plowman.”
- **Formula 10**: The \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-11 Tavern Person|Table 29-11: Tavern Person]]\] and \[[[29. Miscellaneous Tables#Table 29-9 Short Tavern Animal|Table 29-9: Short Tavern Animal]]\]. Examples: “The King and Cat,” “The Plowman and Peacock.” Reverse the order if it sounds better. These are often alliterated, but not always. The Baron and Cat isn’t great, but The Cat and Baron works fine.
