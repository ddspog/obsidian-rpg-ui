# Monarchs
First of all, let’s sweep through the actual historical titles of monarchs in the real world. Keep in mind that a “monarch” is any person with sovereign power who rules until their death, so kings and queens aren’t the only sort.
#### Emperors and Empresses
**Title**: His/Her Imperial Majesty, your Imperial Majesty.
**Other terms**: Imperator, Imperatrice (Latin); Maharaja, Maharini (Indian); Padishah (Arabic and Persian) along with Padeshah, Patshah, Padshah and Badshah; Kha-Khan and Khagan (Mongolic and Turkic respectively). Caliph, although it carries a religious connotation as well as a secular one, probably fits within this category.
#### Kings and Queens
**Title**: His/Her Majesty, your Majesty.
**Other terms**: Roi, Reine (French); Rex, Regina (Latin); König, Königen (German); Rey, Reyna (Spanish), Cyne, Cwen (Saxon); Konge, Kvaen (Norse); Tsar, Tsarina (Russian) (and those might really be equivalent to Emperors rather than kings); Rajah, Rani (Indian); Shah (Arabic and Persian); Malik (Phoenician, Hebrew, Arabic); Khan (Mongolic and Turkic). Sultan might also fit in this context, but it has a religious as well as a secular connotation: in cultures using both “sultan” and “malik” there is a clear distinction between the two words.
#### Archdukes and Archduchesses
**Title**: His/Her Highness, your Highness.
**Other Terms**: Erzherzog (German), Archidux (Latin).
#### Grand Dukes and Grand Duchesses
**Title**: His/Her Serene Grace, your Grace.
**Other Terms**: Magnus Dux (Latin), Grossherzog (German), Groothertog (Dutch).
#### Prince (Sovereign)
**Title**: His/Her Highness, your Highness.
**Other Terms**: Princeps (Latin). Atheling is Old English for “prince,” but does not refer to a sovereign prince. Emir, Emira (Arabic) can be used for the child of a monarch, but is also applicable to a sovereign prince who rules a principality.

Note: Sheikh (Arabic) has a similar meaning, but is specifically for a tribal leader, literally meaning “elder.”
#### Duke (Sovereign)
**Title**: His/Her Grace, your Grace).
**Other Terms**: Dux (Latin); Duc, Duchesse (French); Herzog, Herzogin (German); Bey, Begum (Bey is also rendered as Baig, Bayg, Beigh, Beig, Bek, Baeg or Beg) (Turkic).

# Modes of Address
Now we’re going to depart from the historical world and move into fantasy.

A monarch or other noble has titles such as “His Dread Majesty, Lord of This and That.” You can vary these with the tables below, generating such titles as “His Cherished Majesty,” or switching out both “Dread” and “Majesty” to get a title such as His Beloved Luminsescence.”

There are three categories of monarchs; normal monarchs may still have rather aggressive sounds to their titles (after all, “Dread Majesty” is from real-world Europe). Tyrants, and monarchs in war-torn areas, may have even more barbaric- sounding titles, so there is a separate category for tyrants. Thirdly, there are some leaders (demonic, fiendish, and leaders of the totally-evil) whose titles actually reverse the normal standards (“Your Wickedness”). This last category can become humorous very quickly, which might be good or might be a problem, depending on what you’re trying to achieve.
