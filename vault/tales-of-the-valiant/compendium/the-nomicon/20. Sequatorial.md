![[sequatorial.webp|right|384]]This is a selection of phonemes from an enormous number of African languages, mostly excluding the Arabic-influenced countries in Northern Africa, which are generally covered in the [[05. Arabish|Arabish]] naming group. These languages have a variety of different pronunciation methods. I have left out “clicks” entirely, since they create problems both for pronunciation and spelling.

Usually, the “Mb” is pronounced so that the sound “Mba” would sound like “mm-bah,” not “muh-bah” and definitely not “Em-bah.” The same for Ndo (nn-doh). “Ngo,” also starts with the “nnn,” but it’s a dipthong, so it slides into a sound a bit like “Ingo.”

As most fantasy gamers know, being at least occasional students of history, Northern Africa was more highly civilized than Europe for most of the Medieval period, although it was part of an Arabic/Persian controlled Muslim civilization that swept across Northern Africa, beginning with the conquest of Egypt in 642 C.E. and eventually extending all the way into most of Spain .

The rest of Africa during the Middle Ages was a patchwork of independent kingdoms, tribes, and empires, some of them quite large, with developed trade routes that were linked into the same Arabic networks as Europe. These trade routes had Africa and Europe as different end points of the Arab-controlled Silk Road, so there wasn’t much direct trade between Medieval Africa and Medieval Europe (other than along the South coast of the Mediterranean Sea) Anyone interested in the civilizations and cultures of Medieval Africa might start looking at the Kingdom of Mapungubwe, the Kingdom of Mutapa, the Swahili City-States, the Ghana Empire, the Bunyoro Kingdom, and the Mali Empire.
