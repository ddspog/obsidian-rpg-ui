![[celtigaulic.webp|right|364]] Celtigaulic mimics the linguistic sounds of a vast culture (the Celts or Gauls) that spread across France, Spain, the British Isles, and across the Alps until it was conquered by the Roman Empire. Later, most of this area was also invaded by Germanic tribes, and eventually all of the continental Celtic languages died out in the early Middle Ages. Celtic languages survived in the British Isles, but these “Insular Celtic” language groups are treated separately in this book under “[[06. Brittonnian|Brittonnian]].”

Most records of the continental Celts are from Roman historians. You’ll see a lot more of the letter “w” in these names that you would expect, if you’re familiar with the Roman history. This is because I have replaced a lot of the Roman “v” letters with a “w,” based on the reconstructed Gaulish language. I have, however, left some “v” sounds: there may have been a “v” sound in Gaulish since it appears in Irish, and I’ve taken advantage of that thin reed when making these tables.
