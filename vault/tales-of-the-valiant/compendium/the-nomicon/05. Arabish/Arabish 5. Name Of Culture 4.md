## More of a Persian style than Arabic-style
To get more of a Persian feel to the name, do the following. Roll a name on the Arabish Culture Name tables. Eliminate all but the first two syllables, with a vowel ending. So, you would change “Halawara” to just “Hala.” You don’t actually need to do this, but the names can get very long if you don’t. You can try it both ways.

If you have a double vowel, you can decide whether to use the double vowel or cut it back to the first of the vowels. Thus, you might change Haraatha to either Haraa or Hara.
#### Table 5-16: Persian-Styled Culture Name
**Sample Results**: Haraaman, Osumenid, Sabrimite, Yeghamnid

```rpg table.persian-styled-culture-name
|d6|Ending|Examples|
|---|---|---|
|1|-man|Add “-man,” with “-manic” or “-manid” to get the adjective for the culture. So, starting with Haraatha gives you results of Haraaman Empire, Haraamanid Empire, Haraamanic Culture, or the Haraamanid Culture, whatever sounds the best. Osupha would result in an Osuman or Osumanid Empire, Osumanic Culture, or an Osumanic Empire. Again, whichever one seems to ring the bell properly.|
|2|-menid|Add “menid” to get the adjective for the culture. So, starting with Haraatha, you get results like the Haraamenid Empire or Culture or People. Starting with Osupha generates the Osumenid Empire, Culture, or People.|
|3|-mid|Add “mid” or “midian” to get the adjective for the culture. So, starting with Haraatha, you get results like the Haraamid or Haramidian Empire or Culture or People. Starting with Osupha generates the Osumid or Osumidian Empire, Culture, or People.|
|4|-mite|Add “mite” or “mitic” to get the adjective for the culture. So, starting with Haraatha, you get results like the Haraamite or Haramitic Empire or Culture or People. Starting with Osupha generates the Osumite or Osumitic Empire, Culture, or People.|
|5|-mnid|Add “mnid” or “mnidian” to get the adjective for the culture. So, starting with Haraatha, you get results like the Haraamnid or Haramnidian Empire or Culture or People. Starting with Osupha generates the Osumnid or Osumnidian Empire, Culture, or People.|
|6|-ssid|Add “ssid” or “ssidic” to get the adjective for the culture. So, starting with Haraatha, you get results like the Haraassid or Haraassidic Empire or Culture or People. Starting with Osupha generates the Osussid or Osussidic Empire, Culture, or People.|
```

# Terrain feature names
Note: if you are looking for a broader range of terrain names than the method below, you can also roll for a culture name. The culture names all end with -i, a suffix which works like -ish in the English language. Remove that final -i from the culture name, and you have a workable base word for a terrain feature.
## The Terrain name table
This table is the base for any kind of terrain feature; hills, mountains, rivers, forests, deserts, etc.
- **Formula 1**: Generate a name by rolling on Column 1, 2, or 3 on the [[05. Arabish#Table 5-17 Arabish Terrain Feature Name|Arabish-Persi Terrain Name Table]]. This will generate names such as “The Marshes of Aafa.”
- **Formula 2**: As above, but add an ending to the name using [[05. Arabish#Table 5-18 Arabish Optional Terrain Feature Suffix 1|Table 5-18: Optional Terrain Feature Suffix 1]]. This can give you names such as “The Marshes of Aafadaz.”
- **Formula 3**: For a format such as “The \_\_\_\_\_ Marshes,” add a suffix from [[05. Arabish#Table 5-19 Arabish Optional Terrain Feature Suffix 2|Table 5-19: Optional Terrain Feature Suffix 2]]. This generates names such as “The Aafan Marshes.” There are some dialect options in the Suffix 2 Table.
- **Formula 4**: For a hyphenated name such as “The Marshes of Aafa-Bai,” “The Aafa-Bai,” or the “Marshes of the Aafa-Bai,” roll for the Terrain Name (alone) and then add a hyphenated suffix from [[05. Arabish#Table 5-20 Arabish Optional Hyphenated Suffix|Table 5-20: Optional Hyphenated Suffix]]. This can also be used to distinguish a dialect.
