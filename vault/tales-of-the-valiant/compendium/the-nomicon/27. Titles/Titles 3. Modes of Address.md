## Tyrants
The format is a Tyrannical Title Adjective ([[Titles 3. Modes of Address#Table 27-10 Tyrannical Title Adjective|Table 27-10]]) plus a Noun of Majesty. I recommend just sticking with Majesty, Highness, or Grace (the normal nouns for monarchs), but you can add weirdness to the title using a generated Noun of Majesty ([[Titles 2. Monarchs + Noble Titles + Modes of Address#Table 27-8 Nouns of Majesty, Table 1|Tables 27-8]] and [[Titles 2. Monarchs + Noble Titles + Modes of Address#Table 27-9 Nouns of Majesty, Table 2|27-9]]).

**Instructions**: Roll on either column for an adjective to use with “Majesty,” or with a Noun of Majesty.

## Yes, your Wickedness
The format is a Title Adjective ([[Titles 2. Monarchs + Noble Titles + Modes of Address#Table 27-2 Title Adjective|Table 27-2]]) or a Tyrannical Title Adjective ([[Titles 3. Modes of Address#Table 27-10 Tyrannical Title Adjective|Table 27-10]]) plus a Noun of Wickedness. Keep in mind that villains with these titles just aren’t going to be taken seriously by players, with the possible exception of fey villains. This table generates such titles as “His Inexorable Severity,” or “Her Ferocious Harmfulness,” so this only works for absolutely over-the-top villains (fey villains can fit this category) or unusual situations such as a title for a demonic entity that has gotten into the feudal system somehow.
#### Table 27-11: Noun of Wickedness
**Instructions**: Roll on either column for a substitute for the word “Majesty” in a ruler’s title. And keep in mind that addressing a tyrant as “Your Ferocious Profanity” is going to cause giggles in all but the most serious of situations.
**Sample Results**: His Inexorable Severity, Her Ferocious Harmfulness, Your Ferocious Profanity.
