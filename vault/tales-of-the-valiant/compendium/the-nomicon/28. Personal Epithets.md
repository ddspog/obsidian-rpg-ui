# Surnames
A surname is usually a family name, although there are also “habitational bynames” which are derived from a person’s place of origin, such as “Chretien de Troyes” or “John of Gaunt.” A third type of surname is a patronymic like “Smithson” or “Smithsdottir,” which only carries for one generation. A fourth type is the occupational surname, or aptonym, which identifies the person by profession. For the most part, family names were used more by the nobility than by commoners, who identified by nicknames, habitational bynames, and epithets.
# Habitational Bynames
These can be generated using any of the place name tables in any of the naming groups. The formula is usually John of [Place Name]. Any place names from the book will work.
# Patronymic/Matronymic
A patronymic is identifying a person as the “son of ” or “daughter of ” the father, and a matronymic references the mother. For these, generate another name in the naming group. The patronymic/matronymic might be formulated in various ways, usually depending on the culture.
- **Formula 1**: Son/Daughter of [Parent Name]. In the Robin Hood stories, we have “Much the Miller’s Son” as an example.
- **Formula 2**: [Parent Name] + a suffix such as -son or -ssen. An example would be “Thor Odinson.”

# Occupational Surname
Avoid using these for important NPCs or if the NPC’s profession is clearly something different. These can either be used as [Name] the [Occupational Surname] (“Ben the Baker”) or just [Name] [Occupational Surname] (“Ben Baker”). Many of these don’t work well in the second format if we’re not used to seeing them as surnames. “Gandolfo the Alchemist” sounds fine, but “Gandolfo Alchemist” doesn’t have a ring to it.

# Arcane Epithets
If you have a lot of wizardly NPCs in your world, varying the formula for their names is important to avoid a repetitive sound.

Powerful spellcasters might have:
- An Arcane Epithet Type 1 ([[28. Personal Epithets#Table 28-4 Arcane Epithet Type 1|Table 28-4]]), such as Gandolfo the Esoteric.
- An Arcane Epithet Type 2 (a or b) ([[28. Personal Epithets#Table 28-5 Arcane Epithet Type 2a (Evil Type)|Table 28-5]] or [[28. Personal Epithets#Table 28-6 Arcane Epithet Type 2b (Good Type)|28-6]]) such as Gandolfo, the Hawk of Tzul-Tesh.
- A Physical Epithet ([[28. Personal Epithets#Table 28-9 Descriptive Epithet|Table 28-9]]) such as Gandolfo the Gaunt.
- A Demonic Epithet (if evil) ([[28. Personal Epithets#Table 28-12 Demonic Epithets|Table 28-12: Demonic Epithets]]) such as Gandolfo the Entangler.
- An Evil Dominion Epithet ([[28. Personal Epithets#Table 28-15 Evil Dominion Epithets|Table 28-15]]) such as Gandolfo, Master of Crows.
- An Animal Epithet (with negative or positive connotation, depending on the nature of the spellcaster) ([[28. Personal Epithets#Table 28-16 Animal Epithet - Negative Connotation|Table 28-16]] or [[28. Personal Epithets#Table 28-17 Animal Epithet Broad Possibilities- Generally Positive Connotation|28-17]]) such as Gandolfo the Leopard.
- An Arcane Title ([[27. Titles#Table 27-1 Arcane Titles|Table 27-1: Arcane Titles]]) such as Gandolfo the Ombrathar.
- An Alternative Title ([[27. Titles#Table 27-12 General-Use Titles Part 1|Table 27-12]] and [[27. Titles#Table 27-13 General-Use Titles Part 2|27-13]]: General-Use Titles Part 1 and 2) such as Gandolfo the Lutriasept.
