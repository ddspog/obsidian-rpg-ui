Elderweirdish has three branches: Colossoponderous, Dreamlandish, and Cthonic.

Colossoponderous is for ancient, lost cultures that need to sound like they are old or primitive. The focus is a little more on Elder and a little less on Weird.

Dreamlandish is for weird locations (including planar ones) that have high cultures, even if the culture is sinister. The overall model for this naming group is H.P. Lovecraft’s Dreamlands stories, but the focus is the feeling of high civilization and delicacy.

Cthonic is a bit like the opposite of Dreamlandish. The phonemes are, in general, more threatening or evil than the ones used in Dreamlandish. This naming group could be used for civilizations with brutal gods or cultures, or just for civilizations that are so ancient that they need an alien sound.
# Colossoponderous
These aren’t divided into cultures, countries, and terrain features because there isn’t really a referent to make any of these names sound more like one than another. So they are just divided by their three structures: a hyphenated name like Ko-Atun, a two-word name like Jal Yor, and a longer single-word name like Anhubiri.

# Dreamlandish
## Dreamlandish Personal names
![[dreamlandish-personal-names.webp|right]]For personal names in a Dreamlandish area, most people should have names from the [[15. Italican|Italican]]], [[12. Espannic|Espannic]], and [[23. Trobadoric|Trobadoric]] naming groups. The [[19. Russlavik|Russlavik]] and [[22. Teutonnic|Teutonnic]] groups are also workable. As you go up the chain of wealth and power, names in the Dreamlandish-type fiction (Lovecraft, Vance, CA Smith) tend to get longer and more unusual, so names for the nobility can be pulled from [[16. Metallik#High Metallik|High Metallik]], [[16. Metallik#Dharc-metallik|Dharc Metallik]], [[17. Nanskrit|Nanskrit]], and [[14. Hellenica|Hellenic]]. Names for spellcasters, who will be the most commonly encountered types of people from places like this, are treated separately.
