## Nörslik Feminine Personal names
I’ve done a fair amount of violence to this list, to get names that sound good for an English-speaking audience. Feminine Norse names often don’t sound like feminine names in English. So I have added some endings from Finnish into this mix. I have also dropped the ending “r,” which I kept for the masculine names so it can be modified into other dialects. It was probably pronounced a long time ago, but even Icelandic, which is believed to be very close to Norse, the ending “r” is only pronounced (sometimes) when it follows a vowel. So I’ve taken it out of the feminine name endings to differentiate them more from the masculine endings. In other words, this table is even more “imagined” than the rest.

# Nörslik Culture and Country names
The first table, part one, sets the overall name of the people who live in the culture (a nation-level name), or the people who live in the country (a tribal-level name). The second table simply adds a suffix for -land, -home, etc.

On Part One, roll on either column 1 or 2 (First Part) and then on Column 3 (Ending) for an ending. The Part One table generates names such as Uldyr, Fig, and Hysyrd.

#### Table 18-5: Culture or Country: Nörslik Part Two
This table lets you modify Part One names into countries and cultures, by adding the result from this table to the end of the word generated with Part One. This changes Part One results such as Uldyr, Fig, and Hysyrd into country or culture names such as Uldyrheim (Uldirheim), Figlund, and Hysyrdnyr (Hisirdnir).

**Instructions**: Roll for a Standard ending or an Alternate ending (these can be used to differentiate two cultures) and add the result to the end of the result from the Part One Table.

## Dialectal changes to Nörslik Country or Culture names
You can reflect a dialect in any of the following ways for Nörslik:
- Change all (or most) of the “y” letters to “i.” Geadsyr becomes Geadsir.
- Change all (or most) of the “y” letters to “a.” Geadsyr becomes Geadsar.
- Change a “dr” in the middle or end of the word to “dir” “dar” “dra” or dyr” Gnadrstadr (an unfortunate result) would become Gnadirstadir, Gnadrastadra, etc. You could even (almost) fix that result by changing it to Gnadirstadra.”
