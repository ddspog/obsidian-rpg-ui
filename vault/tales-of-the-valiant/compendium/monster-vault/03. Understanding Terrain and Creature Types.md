# 03. Understanding Terrain and Creature Types
![[terrain-tools.webp|right|384]]*Complete transcription of the "**Tales of the Valiant** Monster Vault" book by **Kobold Press**.*

This chapter describes where creatures might be encountered, and it gives guidance on typical allies, traits, and encounter locations for each creature type. If you are looking for inspiration for an adventure site or for the kinds of allies your favorite monster might have, you’ll find it here. 
# Terrain 
Because most creatures can inhabit multiple types of terrain, the creatures in this book have been divided into the terrains they inhabit in the Creatures by Terrain appendix rather than duplicated in the various terrains listed here. See that appendix for the broadest lists showing which creatures would best fit an encounter and its terrain.

# Creature Types 
A creature’s type describes its fundamental nature. Certain spells, magic items, class features, and other effects interact in special ways with creatures of a particular type. For example, an [[arrow of dragon slaying]] deals extra damage to creatures of the [[Dragon]] type, such as red dragons, dragon turtles, and wyverns. This section includes definitions, allies, common traits and themes, and typical encounter locations for each of the creature types in this book. Each type section also includes a table of each creature of that type in this book along with its CR, its common allies, where you can find it in this book, and how to pronounce its name. 

**_Pronunciation Key._** Each creature includes guidance on how to pronounce its name. The guidance is written using phonetic spelling with no special characters for easier pronunciation.

Beasts are living creatures of the natural world, fearsome, wild, and often deadly. Beasts include wild animals, domesticated animals, and enormous behemoths, such as whales and dinosaurs. ### Allies
Because most Beasts aren’t overly intelligent, their allies tend to be others of their own kind, such as a pack of wolves. In other cases, they ally themselves with Humanoids or other creatures that use the Beasts, for instance as mounts or guardian animals. Because of the sheer variety of Beasts, they have the widest range of allies, making such allies too numerous to include in the table in this section. 
### Traits and Themes
The Beast category includes almost all real-world animals, in their full variety of shapes, sizes, and appearances, and each adapted to its environment and niche. As natural creatures, Beasts never cast spells or use magic, though some have unique adaptations to magical environments, such as being resistant to specific environmental damage or effects. Beasts also typically have less intelligence than many other creatures, operating almost entirely on instincts when hunting, eating, and sleeping. 
### Encounter Locations 
Beasts are found anywhere on the Material Plane where a natural order of plants, animals, and climate exists. Beasts are rarely found near sites of unnatural magic or places where nature is destroyed, corrupted, or unwelcome. Similarly, Undead and sites imbued with necromantic magic unnerve or frighten most Beasts. Few Beasts are adapted to urban environments or life alongside Humanoids, and most avoid the noise of Humanoid cities. 
## Celestials
Celestials dwell in higher realms outside of measurable or material reality, often called heavens or paradise worlds. Many are servants of divine powers, serving as messengers or agents in the mortal realm and throughout the planes. Most of them lean innately toward forces of good and order. All of them strive to oppose or destroy Fiends. Celestials include beings like angels, couatls, and unicorns. 

**_Angel Tag_.** Angels are Outsider Celestials that are direct agents of good deities or operate on behalf of such deities. Some are even created by deities for specific tasks.

### Allies
As agents of deities, goodness, or some other higher power, Celestials ally with similarly motivated creatures. A Celestial might aid a paladin on a mission from their shared deity, might form a temporary alliance with a creature seeking assistance to correct a great injustice, or might arrive at a pivotal moment in a battle to help a creature with a greater destiny only the Celestial understands. In many cases, the recipient of the Celestial’s aid must prove worthiness—whether or not that recipient is even aware of the Celestial’s tests. As otherworldly creatures, Celestials have their own opinions on what might be worth their aid, and they view the mortal realm with an immortal gaze. Their aid, whether earned or withheld, often has some greater purpose unfathomable to the mortal mind. 

Whatever the case for their aid, Celestials that seek non‑Celestial allies typically align themselves with Humanoids. Celestials are almost never found allied with Fiends, malicious creatures, or agents of foul deities.  

When a Celestial aids such a creature, it is either at the behest of some unknowable greater plan, or, more often, the Celestial was manipulated into believing the malicious creature was just and upstanding. 
### Traits and Themes
Many Celestials are creatures of light and goodness, both in ideals and in physicality. They often shed light, create radiance, promote life and growth, and similarly brighten or empower the world and creatures around them. Even Celestials that aren’t angels often have angelic qualities, such as melodic voices, feathered wings, shining fur, glowing eyes, metallic skin, or similar. Though many Celestials have human-like forms and mannerisms, just as many are completely alien in appearance with bodies of holy fire, bodies covered in dozens of eyes in exacting formations, bodies made of concentric rings that emit an otherworldly melody, or bodies made up of other variations that seem bizarre to Humanoids, such as pure light or feathered serpents.  
### Encounter Locations
Many Celestials aren’t naturally from the Material Plane and visit only on specific missions with particular goals in mind. Because of this, Celestials encountered on the Material Plane are usually protecting or watching over sites dedicated to deities of light and goodness, quietly observing locales where events of cosmic significance are taking place, or encouraging mortals to rise up against tyranny and oppression. 
## Constructs
Constructs are made, not born. Often built of metal, stone, or wood, some are charged by their creators to follow a simple set of instructions, while others are given sentience and think independently. Golems, mechadrons, and clockwork creatures are iconic Constructs. 

**_Golem Tag._** Golems are constructs that are almost always humanoid in shape, are typically crafted wholly out of a specific material, and have some magical connection to their creators. This connection might be that the golem was made from the remains of the creator’s loved one, made with some of the creator’s blood, made from an object with great sentimental significance to the creator, or made with some other object or material personal to the creator.

### Allies
A Construct is a created being often with specific instructions imprinted into its consciousness during creation. As such, Constructs rarely choose their allies, at least initially. Constructs that still serve their creators are typically allied with those creators, their creator’s allies and servitors, and other like-minded creatures. Constructs that no longer serve their creators—often due to the creator’s death or imprisonment—might ally with creatures similar to their creators or with creatures serving the creator’s goals. Constructs created for a specific purpose then left to it by their creators, such as a Construct instructed to guard a location, ally with other creatures that align with that purpose, such as aiding and defending creatures in the guarded location. 
### Traits and Themes
Constructs can come in all shapes, sizes, and body styles as suited for the Constructs’ primary purposes. Most Constructs have limited intelligence and devote themselves to endlessly carrying out their original directives—often over decades or even centuries—even if the current situation makes such directives out-of-date, irrelevant, or even dangerous. Constructs whose creators have long since perished might develop different quirks over the years, such as a fondness for a particular flower that grows in the locale the Construct inhabits or a protectiveness toward creatures that wear their creator’s favorite color. 
### Encounter Locations
Constructs serve, aid, or protect specific people or locations, especially people and locations of magical significance, like an archmage or a temple. Because Constructs can often outlive their creators by centuries, Constructs are typically found in ancient ruins, hidden treasuries, forgotten wizard towers, in service to a monarch’s bloodline, or even as seemingly decorative statues that remain motionless along a city’s walls for centuries. Such inactive Constructs might awaken only when the city is in grave danger—or to clean the palace once a decade. 
## Dragons
As large, reptilian creatures of ancient origin and tremendous power, Dragons are both legendary and feared. True dragons are highly intelligent, have innate magic, and grow in power as they increase in age, called age categories. This creature type also includes creatures distantly related to true dragons, but less powerful, less magical, and often less intelligent, such as wyverns and pseudodragons. 

**_Drakes and Dragonettes._** Drakes are a bit more “primitive” than their cousins—less evolved physically, intellectually, or magically as true dragons. Dragonettes are also not quite as evolved as their true dragon cousins, and they usually have a connection with Humanoids, often through bonding with spellcasters. Dragonettes are sometimes even magically bred by Humanoids for specific purposes or roles in Humanoid societies. 

In a pale mirror of their true dragon cousins’ affinity for specific treasures, drakes, dragonettes, and other dragons tend to have some type of theme or area of interest that defines them, such as the scorch drake’s passion for collecting charred bones or the pseudodragon’s curiosity about magical Humanoids.

### Allies
As apex predators and powerful beings in general, Dragons, especially true dragons, view most other creatures as beneath them, leading alliances to be based on subjugation, willing service to the dragon, worship of the dragon, or out of the dragon’s desire to help or protect the “lesser” creatures. Even the most benevolent true dragons see themselves as separate from those they aid. Most other types of dragons tend to be solitary, content with hunting prey and collecting the target of their obsession. Dragonettes are one of the few exceptions, preferring to ally with—and even serve—Humanoids, especially Humanoids with similar interests to the dragonette’s obsession. 
### Traits and Themes
Dragons are scaly beasts often with breath weapons or similarly powerful exhalations, but they are otherwise as varied as the environments in which they are found. Most Dragons are uniquely adapted to their home environments, and dragons have lasting and often subtle impacts on their surroundings. The Dragon’s chosen prey or its obsession shapes the territory around it, because the Dragon maintains that territory to expand its prey or to produce more of its object. In some cases, a Dragon strips territory of everything and moves on, but many prefer to tend their territory over many years. 

**_Draconic Hoards._** All Dragons create hoards or are keenly interested in a particular thing. True dragons have preferences based on the type of dragon they are, and their hoards contain a wide variety of similar items (all golden, all iron, or all magical, for instance). Drakes and dragonettes are much more focused in their obsessions, collecting only very specific things. A silver dragon might collect histories of various peoples, including texts, coins, statues, and similar aspects of those peoples, while a pseudodragon might covet just the hair ties used by a particular people or even treasure the people themselves, seeing their hoard as the number of friendships they can acquire and maintain. 
### Encounter Locations
True dragons are most often encountered in their lairs, though true dragons that enjoy meeting Humanoids or meddling in Humanoid affairs might be found disguised in the courts of kings, hidden among villagers at a festival, or excitedly observing a bustling city from a cliff above it. Drakes and other more instinct-driven Dragons are usually encountered while hunting prey or indulging in their chosen obsession. Because dragonettes are very attached to Humanoids and Humanoid societies, they can be found perched on the shoulders of Humanoids, delivering messages across a city, or sleeping on the counter of a village shop. 
## Elementals
These creatures born of raw elemental chaos can be humanoid or bestial in shape, although true elementals, including the creatures simply called elementals, have no corporeal body beyond the elements from which they formed. Elementals include beings like azers, genies, invisible stalkers, and salamanders. 

**_True Elementals._** A “true” elemental is an animate mass of its origin element, such as an air elemental whose form is howling dust-filled wind or an earth elemental comprised completely of mud and stone. True elementals dissipate into unbound elemental magic when destroyed.

### Allies
Elementals are most comfortable in areas that match their element and typically ally with creatures also comfortable in those environments. This means most Elementals ally with other Elementals, though many Elementals still find allies elsewhere, such as the azer’s kinship with dwarves through a mutual love of crafting. Regardless of common ties, allies of Elementals almost always include creatures accustomed to exposure to the elements of their allied Elementals, preventing pain or even lasting harm to the ally. While humanoid and bestial elementals like genies or salamanders may create sprawling communities or have ties to various allies, true elementals are almost always found inexorably tied to the will of more intelligent Elementals or to spellcasters that have summoned them or otherwise bound them to service. 
### Traits and Themes
Elementals are infused or made of an element, and so their physiology is impervious to that element. Similarly, their bodies are adapted to harsh environments where their element is prevalent. For example, fire-based Elementals often have hair and hide incapable of catching fire, water-based Elementals often have thick hide that retains moisture, and earth-based elementals often have iron-hard bones or sandy or tar-like blood. 
### Encounter Locations 
Elementals rarely appear on the Material Plane, though powerful elemental upheavals may create areas that spontaneously give rise to them (a tsunami might spawn a water elemental, for instance). Elementals gravitate to natural locations where their element is most common and use their affinity for that environment to defend themselves, inhabiting the darkest depths of the ocean, swimming through pools of lava, or racing the winds through a mountainous ravine.  
## Fey

Many Fey are enigmatic, ageless, and consumed with the need to collect and exchange secrets, though not all of them; some more harmless Fey spend their time playing pranks on Humanoids or tending to plants in blighted regions. In some worlds, Fey are closely tied to other planes of existence, such as the Plane of Fey or the Plane of Shadow, where they may pursue darker flights of fancy beyond the watchful eyes of powerful archfey. Fey include dryads, hags, and satyrs.

### Allies
Most Fey, even the unassuming blink dog, possess human‑like intelligence. These clever beings often form close-knit symbiotic bonds with other local Fey and Beasts, particularly those who safeguard the natural world, to protect one another. Many benevolent Fey are treated as minor gods by other creatures and sometimes even worshiped or served by cults of nature-focused Humanoids. Powerful malicious Fey, like hags and selangs, often ensnare desperate Humanoids into service with ironclad contracts or mind-wrenching enchantments, while lesser Fey may serve such powerful creatures through fear alone. 
### Traits and Themes
Fey are irrevocably tied to the magic inherent in nature, and most have a bit of innate magic. Regardless of a Fey’s plane of origin, they share common characteristics with Humanoids and Beasts from the Material Plane, though these characteristics are often magnified or blended in unnatural ways. Some fey might be humanoid in every way except for their insectoid wings or flowering hair, some may have hooves or furred ears, and others still might appear completely human but can magically disguise or change their forms. 

**_Bountiful Lives_.** Many Fey have extremely long lifespans, due to their connection to nature. The more powerful a Fey is, the longer its life may become. Some archfey, considered the most powerful of all Fey beings, have lived for millennia and may live until the end of time. Even death itself is considered a season to most Fey, and should a Fey become powerful enough, death may pose little obstacle to their goals or ambitions. 
### Encounter Locations
Fey are almost always encountered in a natural setting. Misty forests, overgrown bogs, ancient ruins reclaimed by desert sands, or the island-strewn ocean beneath an endless downpour are all hospitable homes to many Fey. Some Fey are adapted to urban environments and can take Humanoid forms, hide in darkness, or beguile Humanoids to keep their presence secret. Incredibly powerful Fey may even rule their own safeguarded demiplanes, where they command lesser Fey and shape their surroundings with a thought. 
## Fiends
Fiends dwell in lower realms outside of measurable or material reality. A few are servants of deities, but many more labor under the leadership of archdevils and demon princes. Priests and mages sometimes summon Fiends to the material world and bind them into service. Most Fiends lean innately toward forces of evil and chaos, and all true Fiends strive to oppose or destroy Celestials. Fiends include demons, devils, hell hounds, and rakshasas. 

**_Demon Tag._** Fiends from the chaotic areas of the Lower Planes, demons are typically more chaotic. They are destructive and volatile and rarely stick to plans or goals for long, because they easily become bored without the constant guidance from a superior demon. Because they tend to recognize and respect only power, Demon Lords rule weaker demons, and these lesser demons constantly squabble among themselves for supremacy and rule over demons even weaker than themselves. 

**_Devil Tag._** Fiends from the orderly areas of the Lower Planes, devils are more organized and lawful than demons. They are corruptive and malicious, endlessly toiling to amass power, prestige, and mortal souls. They work in strict hierarchies with Arch Devils at the top and other devils ruling each other in perfectly-structured tiers, often based on each devil’s individual accomplishments or influence.

### Allies
Fiends seldom trust anyone, mortal or immortal, but they often strike temporary alliances to accomplish specific tasks or goals. A fiend might aid a mortal creature with a similar goal or seek to coerce a mortal into helping it with its current task. As mortal souls, particularly Humanoid souls, are currency and even food in some parts of the Lower Planes, most fiends helping or working with mortals have ulterior motives. Indeed, they seek to capture that soul, either by killing the mortal once the initial task is accomplished or otherwise by corrupting the mortal, so that its soul goes to the Lower Planes when it dies. Otherworldly and immortal, Fiends are rarely obvious about such motives, and many seem quite helpful, offering assistance again and again but always with the end goal of gaining that mortal’s soul when it dies. 

Because of the value of Humanoid souls, Fiends ally themselves with Humanoids, even if the task on the Material Plane is trivial for the Fiend. On occasion, a Fiend allies with other Fiends or non-Humanoids, but this is typically done only at the behest of a superior. 
### Traits and Themes
Fiends of all types are native to harsh, inhospitable planes filled with creatures, hazards, and threats that many mortals only experience in nightmares. Most Fiends are comfortable amid rot and disease, and some even produce deadly poisons from their fangs, tails, or natural gases. Fiends often have spines, horns, leathery skin, sharp teeth, vicious claws, or spear-like tails, and some have a natural resistance to magic. Though many Fiends have human-like or animal-like forms, just as many are completely alien in appearance with forms and features as nightmarish and terrifying as the places they call home. 
### Encounter Locations
Many Fiends arrive on the Material Plane because they are summoned or magically bound to a specific location or person. As a result, Fiends on the Material Plane are often protecting the temples of evil deities, patrolling the holdings of spellcasters, or directly serving powerful Humanoids as guards or assassins. Regardless of their reason for being on the Material Plane, Fiends try to subvert any service they are bound to provide or try to slowly corrupt any mortals they encounter. They often interpret the terms of their service literally and stretch the limits of their bindings as far as they possibly can.

Giants have long memories and have existed in one form or another since the formation of the material realm. ### Allies
Giants mostly associate with other Giants of similar power as well as Humanoids who might worship or revere them. Giants tend to nurture local Beasts and Monstrosities as pets, livestock, or beasts of burden. Occasionally, Giants seek companionship from such creatures or use them in hunting. For example, some frost giant families work with packs of winter wolves in ways that are mutually beneficial for the growth of both family and pack. Giants in particularly harsh or inhospitable environments, such as cloud or fire giants, ally with the few creatures found in those environments, such as air elementals, salamanders, or giant eagles.  
### Traits and Themes
Giants are all far larger than Humanoids and possess enormous strength. While true giants resemble humans, some giants, such as ettins and trolls, grow elongated limbs, additional heads, or excess appendages. The most powerful giants are so enormous that they can hold an adult human in their outstretched hand. Despite these differences, most Giants have societies, villages, family units, and cities just like many Humanoids, and like many Humanoids, they have their own laws and customs unique to each settlement or society. 
### Encounter Locations
True giants are reclusive and tend to be found around homelands matching their name. Giants with an elemental affinity, such as fire or frost giants, are typically found in environments that strongly feature that element. Giants that regularly trade with Humanoids can often be found near Humanoid settlements. Due to their size, all Giants are rarely willing to stay in areas where they can’t freely move, and elementally aligned Giants are particularly hesitant or even hostile when asked to enter a smaller area devoid of their element.

### Allies
![[drow-mage.webp|right|384]] Humanoids understand their limitations and try to shore them through alliances, which could mean building guilds of like‑minded Humanoids, hiring a legion of mercenary fiends, magically summoning fey, raising undead, or investing countless hours training Beasts or building Constructs. Humanoids—whether driven by love, honor, greed, vengeance—can be a staunch ally with any other creature, making them a universal and powerful threat. 

Humanoid stat blocks in the NPC Appendix can be applied to any other type of Humanoid (for instance, the mage stats could be used for a human mage or lizardfolk mage). As such, those Humanoids don’t list allies in the table included here. Allies of Humanoids with NPC stat blocks are assumed to be the same allies of that base Humanoid, or NPCs are assumed to be allied with others of the same or similar type of NPC. For example, a lizardfolk mage is likely to be allied with other lizardfolk, black dragons, crocodiles, and flinderbeasts, just like any other lizardfolk. Similarly, a priest is likely to be allied with acolytes, cultists, and fanatic cultists, and they often match the Humanoid lineage chosen for that priest. 
### Traits and Themes
Humanoids share a typical appearance: two arms, two legs, a torso, and upright posture. While not as innately magical as Fey or as mundane as Beasts, Humanoids are intelligent, capable of speech, and sometimes wield magic, and they gain that access through study, magic-infused blood, or by gift of some powerful being. Humanoids almost always live in groups or societies, creating their own cultures, laws, and traditions wherever they are found. Curiosity is a major driving factor behind the survival, tenacity, and growth of many Humanoid individuals and as whole societies. 
### Encounter Locations
Humanoids are highly adaptable and able to use all types of equipment, magic, and weapons to defend themselves. They can prosper almost anywhere, even hostile caverns, arctic wastelands, or the high seas. Some Humanoids possess specific traits that help them survive in these harsh conditions, such as the ability to see in the dark or breathe underwater. Humanoids tend to live near sources of water and food, in cities or frontier camps, and surrounded by others of their kind.  
## Monstrosities
![[advanced-grick.webp|right|384]] These frightening, abnormal, unnatural creatures are almost never benign. Some monstrosities are the result of magical experimentation gone awry (such as owlbears), others are the product of terrible curses (such as chimeras), and many are monstrous animals that dominate their natural habitats (such as ankhegs). Creatures that defy exact categorization and don’t fit within another type are ideal examples of the [[Monstrosity]] creature type. 

**_Animal Tag._** Animals are Monstrosities (and sometimes other creatures) that share enough characteristics with Beasts that they can be affected by spells, features, and other abilities that specifically target Beasts, such as the _speak with animals_ spell. Ankhegs and owlbears are Monstrosities with the Animal tag.

### Allies
Monstrosities often share characteristics with Beasts or Humanoids, and many Monstrosities ally with such creatures. For example, the spider-like ettercap frequently allies with spiders. However, the oddness of Monstrosities makes many Beasts afraid of them and Humanoids wary of them, leading most Monstrosities to ally primarily with others of their kind. Intelligent Monstrosities often use their natural or magical abilities to influence or manipulate other creatures into serving them, while less intelligent monstrosities are frequently tamed or trained by Humanoids for use as mounts or guardians.  
### Traits and Themes
Monstrosities are known for having frightening, unnatural, or unsettling characteristics that other creatures, especially Beasts or Humanoids, find disturbing. Some blend humanoid and animal traits, others blend multiple animals, and a few possess a magical, particularly dangerous characteristic that clearly sets them apart from other similar animals. Because many Monstrosities are the results of magical experimentation or curses, they often possess magical features, such as breath weapons, magical stares, or even the ability to cast spells, and their bestial forms often belie a shrewd intelligence. 
### Encounter Locations
Monstrosities’ unique bodies make them tougher than most other creatures, and many Monstrosities inhabit less‑hospitable environments because of this. Most Monstrosities favor remote, secluded, or well‑sheltered locations like caves, mines, or forlorn sea shores, but a Monstrosity claims territory or makes a home anywhere it can find food, shelter, and safety. While the small cockatrice might claim a copse of trees as its territory, the titanic kraken may lay claim to an area larger than most Humanoid kingdoms.  
## Oozes
Oozes are gelatinous predators without fixed forms, and they are eating machines. Often completely mindless, they consume anything they can dissolve with their bodies’ natural acids, living or otherwise. Mostly subterranean, scouring caves and dungeons for refuse, Oozes are happy to devour any living creatures unlucky enough to get in their way. Black puddings and gelatinous cubes are among the most recognizable Oozes.

### Allies
Oozes largely lack the sapience required to forge lasting bonds. Content to squeeze through tiny gaps and feed on moss, detritus, and corpses, Oozes are considered vermin by some and share a vermin’s sensibilities. If coaxed into a trap via a trail of food or similar guile, Oozes might willingly remain inside the trap for days, weeks, or years, waiting for their next meal to happen by. Many clever creatures use captured Oozes this way for waste disposal, as mindless guards, and as living traps. Though not allies in the strictest sense, Oozes are occasionally accompanied by ghosts, specters, or skeletons, as the remnants of Oozes’ victims linger near them after death.  
### Traits and Themes
Oozes are semisolid at best and often amorphous. Though some rare Oozes may possess near-human or higher intelligence, the majority of Oozes lack any type of mental ability beyond what is required to move toward their next meal. Many Oozes can remain perfectly still for unnaturally long periods and protrude parts of their body, called pseudopods, to attack and draw in prey. Other Oozes simply move inexorably toward something they believe they might consume with reckless abandon. Most Oozes naturally dissolve any creature they touch, making them dangerous to those they ambush or that can’t outpace them. 
### Encounter Locations
Most Oozes can be found below ground and in fetid, inhospitable places. Crumbling corridors of ancient cities may be swarmed with Oozes feasting on rotting tapestries and wood, while lightless tunnels of sprawling dungeons may contain numerous Oozes that devour fresh corpses. In extremely rare cases, Oozes can be found in cities or towns, particularly in basements, catacombs, or sewers, where tiny vermin and hapless citizens might feed an Ooze indefinitely. 
## Plants
![[shrieker.webp|right|384]] Plants are vegetable creatures, not ordinary flora. Most are ambulatory, some are carnivorous, and many are sentient. Most Plants are naturally occurring creatures on the Material Plane, while some gain sentience through druidic magic or planar influence in parts of the world where the veil between planes is thin. The quintessential Plants are the shambling mound and the treant. Fungal creatures, such as shriekers and mycolids, also fall into this category.

### Allies
Due to Fey’s innate connection with the natural world, Plants often ally with Fey that inhabit the same areas, defending or working alongside those Fey to protect their homes. Plants also tend to ally with Beasts, friendly Monstrosities, and other Plants, creating symbiotic relationships or communities with those other creatures. Rarely, Plants can be cultivated as engines of war by Humanoids, tasked with collapsing walls and boring through defenders with their gnarled roots. 
### Traits and Themes
Creatures of the Plant type tend to be primarily comprised of vegetation or share numerous traits with mundane plants. Some Plants have characteristics in common with Beasts or Humanoids or even have bestial or human‑like shapes, but these are still comprised primarily of vegetation like other Plants. However, most Plants look identical or very similar to mundane plants of a similar type when they remain completely motionless, allowing them to hide from predators and ambush prey. Plant physiology and their mobility also make them hardier and less susceptible to changes in their environments when compared to mundane plants. 
### Encounter Locations
Plants are found in thickly overgrown areas above and below ground, such as glades, forests, grasslands, and fungal-infested caverns. Only rarely are Plants found in urban regions unless they are laying siege to entrenched enemies on behalf of Fey or Humanoids. Fungal Plants tend to prefer dim, dark, or damp areas, like caves, sewers, and the deepest forests and rainforests. 
## Undead
![[undead-vampire.webp|right|384]] Undead are creatures that died and were brought to a state of nightmarish undeath through necromantic magic, curses, or divine intervention. Without concern for food or shelter, Undead are driven by darker urges: to avenge their deaths, to feed an insatiable hunger for flesh, to share their pain with the living, or to obey the magical commands of other creatures. This creature type includes mindless animated corpses like zombies and skeletons, potent predators like vampires and ghouls, bodiless tormented souls like ghosts and specters, and powerful beings who sought undeath for power like liches and mummy lords.

### Allies
Most Undead are created using magic or are born from a particularly traumatic death. As a result, most of an Undead’s allies are its creator or its creator’s subordinates. Intelligent Undead without creators operate on their own or subjugate or convert their own, typically Humanoid, allies when needed. Mindless or low-intelligence Undead without creators often wander aimlessly, seeking living creatures to harm, especially living creatures that resemble their killers or the creatures they were in life. Such Undead are often easily controlled by other more powerful Undead or necromancers, who can direct the Undead’s desire for violence.  
### Traits and Themes
Most Undead were mortals who died and were reanimated to serve some malevolent purpose by a greater undead, spellcaster, or deity. In general, they can work tirelessly, without fear of exhaustion, hunger, or thirst. Most Undead can survive timeless epochs confined in airless, lightless spaces without ever rotting or expiring, sustained by the magic that created them. Though they come in many forms, all undead detest the living, can’t be restored by healing magic, and have perfectly functioning senses (sometimes despite the lack of sensory organs). 
### Encounter Locations
Undead are unnatural creatures, and most exist to serve the beings that created them. Such Undead can often be found attacking the living, digging trenches, building keeps, pillaging outposts, or even safeguarding their creators. Because Undead were often originally living Humanoids, they can be found anywhere Humanoids live. Less intelligent Undead might shuffle around an old graveyard, while an intelligent Undead might lead a city’s guild from the shadows, keeping its deathly nature hidden from all but its closest associates. Powerful, intelligent Undead are bound only by their desires and can be found nearly anywhere.
