This chapter is full of both rules options that expand on the basics presented in the _Player’s Guide_ and _Monster Vault_ and variant rules that are modifications you can make to existing mechanics to customize them. Use these rules to bring something new and fun to your game, to tailor play to your game world and players, or simply to streamline your session prep. 

# Curses, Diseases, Hazards, and Traps 

Curses, diseases, hazards, and traps are excellent tools to increase encounter difficulty or toss in a plot twist. All of these share a format to make them easy to run. The similarities and differences of each are described here. A few sample curses, diseases, hazards, and traps in this section include preset DCs, damage, and mechanical elements ready to use without modification. However, any of these encounter elements can (and should) be adjusted for PCs of various levels and skill sets. 

## Elements of a Curse, Disease, Hazard or Trap
Along with basic information about its nature, each curse, disease, hazard, and trap description includes a trigger, the effects once triggered, and the resolution necessary to remove, prevent, mitigate, or otherwise end the effects: 
- **Trigger:** This describes the condition that must happen to activate the curse, disease, hazard, or trap’s effects. For example, a disease trigger might specify what types of creatures can contract and spread it. A trap trigger might describe the size and placement of the pressure plate that activates its mechanisms.
- **Effects:** This describes what happens after the trigger condition is met. It includes ability checks, saves, and other details required to help the GM determine what happens to targets. For example, a curse’s effects might detail ongoing penalties while a creature suffers its effects. A hazard’s effects might detail what happens each round while targets are exposed to it. 
- **Resolution:** This describes how a trigger can be avoided (if possible) and ways to stop any ongoing effects. For example, a disease’s resolution might include a list of spells that must be cast to cure its victims. A trap’s resolution might include the DC of checks to disarm it if detected before it is triggered. 
### Effect Severity
Curses, diseases, hazards, and traps have varying levels of severity, which detail the degree of danger the effect poses: setback, dangerous, or deadly. An effect intended to be a setback is unlikely to kill or seriously harm characters, a dangerous effect is likely to seriously injure (and potentially kill) characters, and a deadly effect is very likely to kill at least one character. The attack bonus of an effect, the save DC to resist its effects, and the damage it deals can vary depending on its severity. Use the **Effects Save DCs and Attack Bonuses** and **Damage Severity by Level** tables for suggestions based on the three levels of severity. When using these tables, disregard any information not applicable to the effect of the curse, disease, hazard, or trap you are creating. For example, a poisonous gas hazard likely requires only a save while an arrow-shooting trap would require an attack roll but not a save. 
## Curses
Curses are most commonly encountered when a PC attunes to a cursed magic item—a process described in more detail in the **Special Magic Items** section in **Chapter 5**. A few monsters, such as the werewolf, can inflict curses as part of their attacks. Curses can also be tied to locations, creatures, or objects. 

Whatever the source, curses are most effective when their triggers or effects are foreshadowed in a way that gives PCs the opportunity to ignore a warning or decide that the risk is worth it. For example, a crypt might bear a curse that afflicts anyone who takes treasure from it. If the PCs have no notice about the curse and loot the crypt, then getting hit with it can feel unfair. However, if the PCs read a cryptic warning carved into the crypt door before they even step inside, the decision to pocket treasures becomes a risk-versus-reward decision with possible unknown consequences.

### Curse Descriptions
This section features a collection of ready-to-use curses, or you can use this section as inspiration when crafting your own. 

#### Babbling Hex #css/h/feature
_Curse_ 
This curse is a favored faerie punishment that robs a promise‑breaker of their power of speech and understanding, rendering all that they say into incoherent babble. 

**Trigger:** When a creature breaks a promise or fails to hold up a bargain made with a Fey, it must succeed on a DC 15 INT save or become cursed. The GM decides what actions constitute breaking the promise or deal. A creature must be able to speak at least one language to be targeted by this curse. 

**Effects:** While cursed, the creature loses proficiency in all languages, can’t read, write, or speak and fails to understand when other creatures attempt to communicate with it. In addition, whenever the cursed creature attempts to cast a spell with a verbal component, it must succeed on a DC 15 INT save or fail to cast the spell, expending the spell slot in the attempt. 

**Resolution:** The curse can be lifted with a _remove curse_ spell or similar magic. Alternatively, the curse can be ended by convincing or tricking the Fey who placed the curse into eating an intact frog. This alternative resolution might be available only to creatures that discover it by succeeding on a DC 15 INT (History) check while using the Researching downtime option detailed in the _Player’s Guide._

#### BURNING APPARITIONS #css/h/feature 
_Curse_ 
Long ago, a large group of creatures was burned alive at the stake, on a dais or altar, in a pit, or some other concentrated location. The pain and torment of those creatures’ last moments places a curse on the area. Living creatures that enter the area are assaulted with searing sensations and visions of the event where the creatures were burned alive. 

**Trigger:** This curse takes effect when a creature that isn’t a Construct or Undead touches or moves to within 5 feet of the location where the creatures were burned alive. 

**Effects:** When triggered, there is a whoosh of air as burning ghostly figures briefly fill the area. Each creature within 10 feet of the location where the creatures were burned must succeed on a DC 14 DEX save or take 10 (3d6) fire damage and catch fire for 1d6 rounds. Until a creature takes an action to douse the fire, the burning creature takes 3 (1d6) fire damage at the start of each of its turns. The curse then goes dormant for 10 minutes and can’t be triggered again in that time. 

**Resolution:** A creature that casts _detect evil and good_ within 30 feet of the cursed area learns it is a magically desecrated area but doesn’t learn the specific nature of the curse. Celestials, creatures with celestial blood, and spellcasters with access to Divine source spells can sense a nearby malign presence when within 20 feet of the cursed area. The curse can be permanently ended by consecrating the area with a _hallow_ spell cast on the cursed area. Alternatively, the curse permanently ends if three vials of holy water (see _Player’s Guide_) are sprinkled over the affected area. If the area is targeted by a _magic circle_ spell that affects Undead, the curse is suppressed for the duration of the spell. 

#### COVETOUS DEAD #css/h/feature 
_Curse_ 
This curse is often placed on wealth and treasure within tombs, mausoleums, and other burial sites. When creatures take such cursed treasure from its resting place, it brands them and slowly drains their vitality until the stolen treasure is returned. 

**Trigger:** This curse takes effect when a creature leaves the cursed tomb, sepulcher, or gravesite holding or carrying one of its treasures (any item valued at 1 gp or more). 

**Effects:** The next dawn, a creature that isn’t a Construct or Undead and that touched the removed treasure—even if the creature no longer possess it—manifests scarlet marks on its hands or other grasping limb, spreading up the limb over the course of a few hours. In an ancient language, these marks proclaim the creature a thief and defiler. Every 24 hours, the cursed point maximum is reduced by 2 (1d4) as the body wastes away. This reduction lasts until the creature finishes a long rest after the curse has been removed. The creature dies if this effect reduces its hit point maximum to 0. 

The curse persists even if the creature gets rid of the treasure. In addition, any other creature that isn’t a Construct or Undead and that touches the stolen treasure must succeed on a DC 15 WIS save or also be cursed. 

**Resolution:** A _remove curse_ spell cast on the cursed creature provides only temporary relief: the cursed creature automatically succeeds on its next save against the curse’s hit point maximum reduction. Lifting the curse requires the stolen treasure be returned to the tomb, but it doesn’t matter who returns it. Returning the treasure lifts the curse for all affected, immediately ending the pain and causing the marks to fade. 

#### CURSE OF LYCANTHROPY  #css/h/feature 
_Curse_ 
This curse permanently transforms a Humanoid into a werecreature of the same kind that turned it. The curse of lycanthropy can be spread only by those already changed by it. ![[curse-of-lycanthropy.webp|right|384]]

**Trigger:** When a creature with the Lycanthrope tag bites a Humanoid or otherwise passes its saliva into a Humanoid’s wound, it can spread the curse.

**Effects:** For the first 30 days after becoming infected, the Humanoid experiences dreams and nightmares related to the Beast associated with its curse, such as bear or wolf. After 30 days, the Humanoid undergoes its first transformation. While sleeping, it loses control of its body and acts as a member of its type of lycanthropy, as defined in the Lycanthrope’s lore (see _Monster Vault_). 

A newly cursed Humanoid continues transforming each night, remembering only fragments of these events. Every 7 days, it can repeat the save it made when it was first cursed. When it succeeds on this save three times, it gains full control of its transformation, retains its memories while transformed, and no longer has to make this save, becoming a true Lycanthrope. 

**Resolution:** For the first 30 days after becoming infected, the curse can be cured with the _remove curse_ spell or similar magic. Once a creature’s first transformation occurs after these first 30 days, the creature can no longer be cured of lycanthropy, except by a _wish_ spell or similar magic. 

#### ETERNAL GUARDIAN #css/h/feature 
_Curse_ 
A grouping of foreboding, monolithic stones stands above an underground barrow once belonging to a tribe of witches and shamans. Creatures that die within its confines are doomed to remain, forced into eternal service as Undead guardians. 

**Trigger:** This curse takes effect when a creature dies within or beneath the circle of stones. This curse has no effect on Constructs or Undead. 

**Effects:** A creature that dies in the cursed area can’t be restored to life by any magical means while its body remains within the cursed area. After 1 hour in the area, a dead creature’s remains reanimate, becoming an Undead creature. The type of Undead is determined by the approximate level or CR of the creature. Less powerful creatures return as skeletons or zombies, while more powerful creatures turn into ghouls or wights. Once reanimated, an Undead creature can’t willingly leave the area and is compelled to hunt and kill living creatures inside the area. 

**Resolution:** Remains removed from the cursed area before an hour has passed can be magically restored to life. Once a creature reanimates as an Undead, only a _wish_ spell or similar magic cast upon it after it is removed from the area can restore its living form. This curse can be permanently ended by consecrating the area with a _hallow_ spell cast at the center of the cursed area. When the curse is ended, any Undead created by the curse are instantly destroyed. 

#### FALLING FORTUNES  #css/h/feature 
_Curse_ 
A curse that punishes the self-serving and robs people of their fortunes—in fate if not in purse. It typically protects gold or other treasure in the possession of Fey, but powerful or influential individuals might hire or convince Fey to protect their treasure with the curse. 

**Trigger:** This curse takes effect when a creature steals the cursed gold pieces, trinkets, or other symbols of wealth. 

**Effects:** The creature cursed is plagued with bad luck for 24 hours. While cursed, the creature can’t use Luck points, can’t gain Luck points, and can’t add its PB to attack rolls or ability checks. If the cursed creature rolls a 20 on an attack roll or an ability check, the curse ends. If the cursed creature rolls a 1 on an attack roll or an ability check, the duration of the curse is extended for another 24 hours. 

**Resolution:** The curse can be lifted with a _remove curse_ spell or similar magic. Alternatively, the curse can be ended by giving away all fortune gained within the last week to someone in honest need. This alternative resolution might be available only to creatures that discover it by succeeding on a DC 13 INT (History) check while using the researching downtime option detailed in the _Player’s Guide_. 

#### FLESH BANE  #css/h/feature 
_Curse_ 
First created by a hag fulfilling a petitioner’s wish to be immortalized, this curse slowly turns the afflicted into stone, preserving them forever. It now clings to statues, archways, and fences made of stone in areas infused with faerie magic. 

**Trigger:** When a creature touches a piece of stone enchanted with this curse, it must succeed on a DC 14 CON save or be afflicted with this curse. 

**Effects:** This curse progresses in four swift stages, escalating the affliction before fully petrifying the cursed creature. At the end of each of its turns, the cursed creature must succeed on a DC 14 CON save or progress to the next stage of the petrification. Success only delays the curse until the end of the next turn, when the creature must repeat the save, progressing to the next stage on a failure. At the first stage, the cursed creature’s speed is reduced to 0, and it is restrained as its body grows heavy and sluggish. At the second stage, the creature’s limbs begin to petrify, and it can’t take actions, bonus actions, or reactions that require precise movements, such as spellcasting, or that require the creature to manipulate objects, such as weapons. At the third stage, the creature’s body begins to petrify, and it has disadvantage on STR, DEX, and CON saves. At the fourth stage, the creature becomes fully petrified. 

**Resolution:** If the cursed creature hasn’t yet been fully petrified, the curse can be lifted with the _remove curse_ spell. If the cursed creature is fully petrified, the curse can be lifted and the creature returned to normal with a _greater restoration_ spell or similar magic. Alternatively, the curse can be ended and the creature returned to normal by washing the petrified creature with a medusa’s blood or sewing a trinket crafted from snakeskin into the petrified creature’s hair with a golden needle and thread. This alternative resolution might be available only to creatures that discover it by succeeding on a DC 14 INT (History) check while using the researching downtime option detailed in the _Player’s Guide_. 

#### FRENZIED DANCING #css/h/feature 
_Curse_ 
The soul of a murdered musician haunts an instrument, inflicting a curse on any who dares play it. 

**Trigger:** This curse takes effect when a creature plays a cursed musical instrument. 

**Effects:** The creature that triggers the curse must succeed on a DC 14 CHA save or take 5 (1d10) psychic damage, be unable to let go of the instrument, and begin to play a haunting tune on the cursed instrument. Each Humanoid that can hear the tune must make a DC 14 WIS save or suffer one level of exhaustion and begin a frenetic, furious dance until the cursed creature can let go of the instrument and stop playing or until the Humanoid can no longer hear the music from the cursed instrument. At the start of each of the cursed creature’s turns, it must repeat the CHA save. On a success, it overcomes the curse’s effects and can let go of the instrument. On a failure, it takes 5 (1d10) psychic damage and continues to play, forcing each Humanoid that can hear the music to make a WIS save, as before. 

A dancing creature can’t willingly leave its space and must use its action to dance, but it can repeat the WIS save at the end of each of its turns. On a success, it can move out of its space, potentially moving into an area where it can’t hear the music. On a failure, it continues dancing and suffers another level of exhaustion. 

**Resolution:** The curse lasts for 1d6 rounds. A creature in the area of a _magic circle_ spell that is warded against Undead or a creature under the effects of the _protection from evil and good_ spell is immune to the effects of this curse. Burning or melting the musical instrument in an area consecrated by the _hallow_ spell permanently destroys this curse. 

#### GILDING TOUCH #css/h/feature 
_Curse_ 
A legend of antiquity, this curse causes a creature to render whatever it touches into solid gold, whether breakfast or belongings, friend or foe. The greedy covet this curse, only afterward realizing their hubris when they can’t eat their solid gold supper. Though this curse can be caused by Fey or genies fulfilling mortal wishes for endless wealth, it most often is imbued on items made of gold or encrusted with gemstones. 

**Trigger:** When a creature touches an item imbued with this curse, it must succeed on a DC 14 WIS save or be afflicted with this curse. 

**Effects:** When a cursed creature touches an item no larger than a 5-foot cube of material with its grasping limb, such as a hand or tentacle, that item turns to gold instantly. If the touched item is larger than a 5-foot cube, such as a wall or floor, only a 5-foot cube or 5-foot section of the item turns to gold. The touched item must be a single, solid item to become gold. A pond, cloud, air, rain, or similar substance that isn’t a solid, contiguous object doesn’t become gold when touched by the cursed creature. 

The weight of an item turned into gold is multiplied by 20, its AC (if it has one) is reduced by 2, its damage threshold (if it has one) becomes 0, and any damage it deals is reduced by 2. 

If the cursed creature touches another creature, the target must succeed on a DC 14 CON save or be petrified at the end of its next turn as it turns into solid gold. The cursed creature can’t turn itself into gold, but it can turn anything it wears or carries into gold by touching that item with its grasping limb. 

**Resolution:** Only a _wish_ spell can end the curse, but casting _remove curse_ on an item or creature changed into gold reverts the item or creature back to its original form. Alternatively, the curse can be ended by the cursed creature throwing its most coveted treasure in a river, which ends the curse on the cursed creature and changes all items and creatures turned into gold back into their original forms. This alternative resolution might be available only to creatures that discover it by succeeding on a DC 14 INT (History) check while using the researching downtime option detailed in the _Player’s Guide_. 

#### MAYFLY AFFLICTION #css/h/feature 
_Curse_ 
A favorite of covens who delight in punishing the long lived, the curse ages the afflicted over just a period of hours, kills it, and brings it back to life only to start the entire cycle of life over and over again. 

**Trigger:** When a creature offends a hag coven or disrupts an item or area infused with time magic, it must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or be afflicted with this curse. 

**Effects:** The cursed creature ages at a rate of 1d10 years each hour. After 24 hours or 100 years pass, whichever occurs first, the cursed creature dies, then it returns to life at initiative count 20 of the next turn. If the cursed creature dies from some other effect than this curse, the curse still returns it to life. When brought back to life by this curse, the target assumes the form it had in infancy, typically swaddled in or surrounded by whatever clothing and equipment it was wearing or carrying before it was brought back to life. The cursed creature then continues to age at the same rate, repeating the cycle over and again.

Regardless of how many times the cursed creature dies and is brought back, it is the same individual with all its memories intact. 

**Resolution:** Only a _wish_ spell can end the curse, but casting _remove curse_ on the cursed creature halts the cycle for 24 hours, preventing the creature from aging but not preventing the creature from returning to life as an infant if killed. Alternatively, the curse can be ended by the cursed creature convincing a phoenix to take on the burden of the curse instead. The phoenix then dissipates the curse’s power, ending it permanently, the next time the phoenix is reborn. This alternative resolution might be available only to creatures that discover it by succeeding on a DC 15 INT (History) check while using the researching downtime option detailed in the _Player’s Guide_. 

#### MUMMY ROT #css/h/feature 
_Curse_ 
This disease-like curse slowly rots a victim’s flesh. 

**Trigger:** A creature has a 50 percent chance to become cursed with mummy rot for each minute it spends exposed to the remains of a mummy or to the remains of a creature that died while cursed by mummy rot. Alternatively, a creature can become cursed with mummy rot during a combat encounter with a mummy or mummy lord. 

**Effects:** The cursed creature’s flesh slowly deteriorates, rotting off in flakes at first, then whole chunks as the curse progresses. While cursed, the creature can’t regain hit points. At the end of each long rest, it must succeed on a DC 13 CON save or its hit point maximum is reduced by 7 (2d6). If the curse reduces the creature’s hit point maximum to 0, the creature dies, and its body turns to dust. 

**Resolution:** The curse lasts until removed by the _remove curse_ spell or similar magic. A creature normally immune to disease is immune to mummy rot, but spells and effects that combat diseases, such as the _restoration_ spell, have no effect on this curse. 

#### SURFACE CURSE  #css/h/feature 
_Curse_ 
Long ago, a circle of moon-revering druids sought to aid nearby communities plagued by nightly raids from subterranean creatures. They cast this curse on the raiders, who found themselves suddenly fearful of the surface and pained by moonlight and sunlight alike. Now, this curse is typically found on items with lunar motifs or in the repertoire of Fey associated with the moon and stars. 

**Trigger:** When a creature touches an item enchanted with this curse, it must succeed on a DC 15 CHA save or be afflicted with this curse. 

**Effects:** While the cursed creature is on the surface of the world and not underground, it is vulnerable to the frightened condition. While the cursed creature is in moonlight or sunlight, it has disadvantage on attack rolls and on WIS (Perception) checks. When the cursed creature finishes a long rest on the surface of the world, it must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or have its hit point maximum reduced by 5 (2d4). This reduction lasts until the creature finishes a long rest after the curse has been removed. The creature dies if this effect reduces its hit point maximum to 0. 

**Resolution:** The curse can be lifted with a _remove curse_ spell or similar magic. Alternatively, the curse can be ended by spending 7 days underground. This alternative resolution might be available only to creatures that discover it by succeeding on a DC 15 INT (History) check while using the researching downtime option detailed in the _Player’s Guide_. 
#### THROTTLER  #css/h/feature 
_Curse_ 
The despair of a Humanoid who was murdered by strangulation manifests as a pair of spectral hands that squeeze inexorably around the throats of creatures near where the creature was murdered. 

**Trigger:** This curse takes effect when a Humanoid moves to within 5 feet of the location where the creature was murdered or touches the item used to commit the murder, such as a rope, cloth gag, or similar item. 

**Effects:** When triggered, each creature within 10 feet of the location where the creature was murdered or within 10 feet of the cursed item must succeed on a DC 15 STR save or take 11 (2d10) bludgeoning damage and begin suffocating as a pair of strong, spectral hands wrap around its throat. 

**Resolution:** The curse’s strangulation effect lasts for 1d6 rounds. The strangulation effect ends early if the _protection from good and evil_ spell is cast on the cursed creature. The curse can be permanently ended by sprinkling three vials of holy water across the cursed area or item. 
## Diseases
A disease is usually contracted by exposure to other creatures carrying it. In addition, some monsters, such as ghouls, can inflict diseases as part of their attacks, necromantic spells like _contagion_ can inflict diseases upon their targets, and exposure to certain hazards or poisons can result in contracting a disease. 

Common diseases are easily cured by PCs with mundane or magical healing. Exposure to rarer diseases with highly specific triggers, effects, or resolutions can present novel challenges. Discovering a cure for a mysterious plague that only affects Constructs or racing to collect the sole ingredient capable of producing a cure are examples of impactful ways to utilize a disease in your games.
### Disease Descriptions
This section features a collection of ready-to-use diseases, or you can use this section as inspiration when crafting your own. 
#### APOCALYPTIC FUNGUS #css/h/feature 
_Disease_ 
This infectious disease is transmitted via fungal spores. Infected creatures become irritable and sensitive to noise. In the final stages, fibrous fungal growth sprouts from the skin—particularly around the cranium. If left untreated, infected creatures turn into sporeborn (see _Monster Vault)_. 

**Trigger:** When a Humanoid ingests food or water contaminated with the spores of the apocalyptic fungus or breathes in the spores, it must succeed on a DC 12 CON save or become infected. Some creatures can also spread this disease. If a creature carries this disease, its statistics detail how it can spread the infection. 

**Effects:** An infected creature can’t regain hit points except by magical means, and when it finishes a long rest, it must succeed on a DC 13 CON save or suffer two levels of exhaustion. If an infected creature has at least one level of exhaustion because of this disease, small fungal growths sprout from its skin, especially around its head. If an infected creature is slain, or if it suffers six levels of exhaustion, it doesn’t die but instead transforms into a skullboom sporeborn 1 hour after it would have died. 

**Resolution:** If the infected creature hasn’t started sprouting fungal growths from its skin, the disease can be detected only with magic or a successful DC 15 WIS (Medicine) check. If an infected creature hasn’t yet been transformed, the infection can be cured by the _restoration_ spell or similar magic. Once transformed, only a _wish_ spell or similarly powerful magic can reverse the transformation. 
#### CACKLE FEVER #css/h/feature 
_Disease_ 
Victims in the grips of this disease succumb to random fits of manic laughter, giving the disease its common name and its morbid nickname, “the shrieks.” 

**Trigger:** When a Humanoid starts its turn within 10 feet of an infected creature, it must succeed on a DC 10 CON save or become infected. Once a Humanoid succeeds on this save, it is immune to infection from that creature for 24 hours. 

**Effects:** Fever, disorientation, and mild giggles manifest 1d4 hours after infection. In addition, the infected creature gains one level of exhaustion that can’t be removed until the disease is cured. Any event that causes stress— including entering combat, taking damage, experiencing fear, or having a nightmare—forces the creature to make a DC 13 CON save. On a failure, the creature takes 5 (1d10) psychic damage and becomes incapacitated with a fit of laughter for 1 minute. The creature can repeat the save at the end of each of its turns, ending the fit and the incapacitated condition on a success. 

**Resolution:** At the end of each long rest, an infected creature can make a DC 13 CON save. On a success, the DC for this save and for the save to avoid a fit of laughter is reduced by 1d6. When the save DC drops to 0, the disease is cured. A creature that fails three of these saves gains a random form of indefinite dread (see **Dread** later in this chapter). 

#### CARRION CURSE #css/h/feature 
_Disease_ 
Within a day of contracting this abyssal disease, a dark discoloration appears around the infected wound. Over time, the wound festers, causing it to smell like putrefying flesh that attracts carrion-craving scavengers. 

**Trigger:** When a creature comes into contact with infected carrion, it must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or become infected. Some creatures, especially carrion-eating Fiends or Monstrosities, can also spread this disease. If a creature carries this disease, its statistics detail how it can spread the infection. 

**Effects:** Until the disease is cured, the infected creature can’t regain hit points magically and regains hit points only through resting or spending Hit Dice. At the end of each long rest, an infected creature must succeed on a DC 13 CON save or suffer one level of exhaustion. If the infected creature succeeds on this save, it remains infected, but it no longer has to make this save. If the infected creature has at least one level of exhaustion because of this disease, its wounds smell like putrefying flesh, and carrion-eating creatures have advantage on WIS (Perception) checks to find or track the infected creature by smell. 

**Resolution:** If the infected creature’s wounds don’t smell like putrefying flesh, the disease can be detected only with magic or a successful DC 15 WIS (Medicine) check. This abyssal disease resists many efforts at treatment and can be cured only with a _greater restoration_ spell or similar magic. 
#### GHOUL HUNGER #css/h/feature 
_Disease_ 
Ghoul hunger instills in its victim a despicable appetite and craving for Humanoid flesh. 

**Trigger:** A Humanoid can contract this disease after being attacked by a ghoul. A ghoul’s statistics detail how it can spread the infection. 

**Effects:** The disease takes 1d4 hours to manifest, after which the infected creature is poisoned until the disease is cured. The poisoned condition can be suppressed for 1 hour if the infected creature consumes any amount of Humanoid flesh. Every 12 hours that elapse after, the infected creature must succeed on a DC 13 CON save or its hit point maximum is reduced by 7 (2d6). The creature has disadvantage on this save if it consumed any Humanoid flesh while infected. This reduction lasts until the creature finishes a long rest after the disease is cured. The infected creature dies if this effect reduces its hit point maximum to 0. 

**Resolution:** A creature that succeeds on two of its CON saves is cured. If an infected Humanoid fails two of these saves or dies while infected, it becomes a ghoul, or a ghast if its PB is +4 or higher, in a flesh-rending transformation. If the Humanoid is a spellcaster with a PB +4 or higher, it has a 25 percent chance of transforming into a necrophage ghast instead. Only a _wish_ spell or similarly powerful magic can reverse the transformation. 
#### NULLIFYING BLIGHT #css/h/feature 
_Disease_ 
This disease is spread by corrupted unicorns and pockets of antimagic, plaguing creatures with fatigue, light-headedness, blurry vision, and a rash that spreads when magic is used. 

**Trigger:** For each hour a creature spends exposed to antimagic—whether via the _antimagic field_ spell or an area of antimagic—it must succeed on a DC 13 CON save or contract this disease. Some creatures can also spread this disease. If a creature carries this disease, its statistics detail how it can spread the infection. 

**Effects:** Until this disease is cured, when the infected creature starts its turn wielding or wearing a magic item and each time it casts a spell or activates a magic item, it loses 5 (2d4) hit points, and an itchy rash appears on its body, spreading each time it uses magic in this way. At the end of each long rest, the infected creature must make a DC 13 CON save. On a failure, the hit points the infected creature loses each time it uses magic in this way increases by 2 (1d4). 

**Resolution:** If the infected creature doesn’t have the itchy rash, the disease can be detected only with magic or a successful DC 17 WIS (Medicine) check. An infected creature that succeeds on three saves recovers from the disease. 
#### RAVENING #css/h/feature 
_Disease_ 
This disease causes uncontrollable fury and is highly contagious, infecting Giants, Humanoids, and Monstrosities in equal measure.

![[ravening.webp|right|384]]**Trigger:** When a Giant, Humanoid, or [[Monstrosity]] comes into contact with the saliva or blood of an infected creature, it must succeed on a DC 13 CON save or become infected. Some creatures can also spread this disease. If a creature carries this disease, its statistics detail how it can spread the infection. Minotaurs and beastkin have disadvantage on the save to avoid infection. 

**Effects:** After 24 hours, the infected creature becomes bloodthirsty and dull witted, and it regards all other creatures with hostility and hunger. While bloodthirsty, the infected creature has disadvantage on INT checks and saves, and whenever it reduces a creature to 0 HP, it is compelled to spend a bonus action to consume the slain creature’s heart, provided the creature has a heart that can be consumed. The infected creature gains temporary hit points equal to twice its PB each time it consumes a heart in this way. Temporary hit points gained in this way can be added to temporary hit points the creature previously gained in this way, and these temporary hit points last until the infected creature finishes a long rest. 

**Resolution:** An infected creature can be cured by the _restoration_ spell or similar magic. After an infected minotaur or beastkin becomes bloodthirsty, it can be cured only by the _greater restoration_ spell or similar magic.
#### SCALEMOLDER #css/h/feature 
_Disease_ 
This disease causes the scales of draconic creatures to develop a green coating and become intolerably itchy. As the disease progresses, the coating covers the entirety of the creature’s body and causes the victim to scratch itself raw. In its final stages, the creature’s scales fall off and slowly turn to dust, revealing the raw flesh below. 

**Trigger:** When an adult or older draconic creature, such as a Dragon or kobold, comes into physical contact with an infected creature or interacts with an infected creature’s blood or saliva, it must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or become infected. Some creatures can also spread this disease. If a creature carries this disease, its statistics detail how it can spread the infection. 

**Effects:** When an infected creature finishes a long rest, it must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or suffer one level of exhaustion. A creature that fails the save by 5 or more suffers two levels of exhaustion. For each level of exhaustion the creature suffers, the green coating spreads further, and after it reaches four levels of exhaustion, its scales slowly start flaking off. The exhaustion lasts until the creature finishes a long rest after the disease is cured. 

**Resolution:** An infected creature can be cured by applying a special poultice made from treant sap to the affected area for 1 hour or by the _greater restoration_ spell or similar magic. A creature that has proficiency with herbalist tools can create the poultice from treant sap with 1 hour of work. 
#### SEWER PLAGUE #css/h/feature 
_Disease_ 
This disease is a catchall name for a swath of illnesses that incubate in sewers, refuse heaps, and stagnant waters. It can be also transmitted by creatures that dwell in those areas, such as rats and otyughs. 

**Trigger:** When a Humanoid comes into contact with filth or offal contaminated by sewer plague, it must succeed on a DC 11 CON save or become infected. Every hour a creature spends wading through or otherwise in contact with sewage, refuse, or stagnant water, it has a 50 percent chance of becoming infected, without requiring a save. At the GM’s discretion, creatures native to sewers or swamps might also transmit this disease through Bite or Claw attacks. 

**Effects:** Fatigue and muscle cramps manifest 1d4 days after infection. When the disease manifests, the infected creature suffers one level of exhaustion. After the disease manifests and until it is cured, the infected creature regains only half the normal number of hit points from spending Hit Dice and regains no hit points after finishing a long rest. After finishing a long rest, an infected creature must make a DC 11 CON save. On a failure, the creature suffers one level of exhaustion. On a success, its exhaustion decreases by one level. 

**Resolution:** If a successful save reduces an infected creature’s level of exhaustion to zero, it recovers from the disease. Alternatively, an infected creature can be cured by the _restoration_ spell or similar magic. 
#### SIGHT ROT #css/h/feature 
_Disease_ 
This painful infection causes bleeding from the eyes and eventually blinds the victim. 

**Trigger:** When a Beast or Humanoid drinks water contaminated with sight rot, it must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or become infected. 

**Effects:** One day after infection, the creature’s vision starts to become blurry. The creature takes a −1 penalty to attack rolls and ability checks that rely on sight. At the end of each long rest after the symptoms appear, the penalty worsens by 1. When it reaches −5, the victim is permanently blinded until its sight is restored. 

**Resolution:** An infected creature can be cured with an ointment made from the eyebright flower, which grows in swamps. Applied to the eyes before a long rest, one dose of the ointment prevents the disease from worsening after that rest. After three doses, the ointment cures the disease entirely. A creature that has proficiency with herbalist tools can turn one eyebright flower into one dose of ointment with 1 hour of work. A creature blinded by sight rot can have its sight restored by the _restoration_ spell or similar magic. 
#### SLIME POX #css/h/feature 
_Disease_ 
This disease is characterized by the slimy pustules that erupt across the victim’s body. 

**Trigger:** When a creature comes into contact with an aboleth’s slime, it must succeed on a DC 16 CON save or become infected. Some creatures, typically aboleths or their servitors, can also spread this disease. If a creature carries this disease, its statistics detail how it can spread the infection. 

**Effects:** Until the disease is cured, the infected creature can breathe air and water, can’t regain hit points except when underwater, and takes 6 (1d12) acid damage for every 1 hour it remains outside of water or without moisture applied to its skin. Every 24 hours that elapse, the infected creature must make a DC 16 CHA save. Each time a creature fails this save, it views the infecting aboleth more and more favorably, becoming the aboleth’s doting and protective thrall after three failed saves. The successes and failures don’t need to be consecutive, but once a creature has acquired three of a kind, it no longer has to make this save, resulting in either the creature becoming a thrall of the aboleth or an autonomous—though infected— creature. If the aboleth that created the slime is alive, the infected thrall can innately sense the presence of that aboleth, knowing its general direction, distance from the thrall, and needs from its thralls. If the aboleth that created the slime is no longer alive, the infected thrall is doting and protective of aboleths in general but is otherwise an autonomous, infected creature. 

**Resolution:** An infected creature can be cured only by the _heal_ spell or similar magic. 
#### URUSHI BLIGHT #css/h/feature 
_Disease_ 
An uncomfortable and distracting disease contracted by prolonged exposure to poisonous plants. This disease causes a painfully itchy rash to spread across the victim’s body, making any activity that requires focused attention difficult. As the disease progresses, the rash develops into inflamed blisters that cover the victim. 

**Trigger:** When a creature that isn’t immune to poison and that has skin spends at least 1 hour in a patch of urushi plants (see **Hazards** later in this chapter), it must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or become infected. Some creatures can also spread this disease. If a creature carries this disease, its statistics detail how it can spread the infection. 

**Effects:** The persistent, itchy rash makes an infected creature irritable and constantly distracted. An infected creature has disadvantage on CON saves to maintain concentration on spells, and it has disadvantage on INT, WIS, and CHA ability checks. At the end of each long rest, the infected creature takes 2 (1d4) slashing damage from indiscriminately scratching itself during its rest. 

**Resolution:** At the end of each long rest, the infected creature must make a DC 15 CON save. If it succeeds on two of these saves, it recovers from the disease. An anti-itch salve applied to the rash during a long rest gives the infected creature advantage on its next save and prevents the creature from taking slashing damage from scratching itself during the rest. A creature that has proficiency with herbalist tools and access to a natural forest can find the necessary plants and create the salve with 1d4 hours of work. 
## Hazards
Hazards are commonly encountered when PCs explore wilderness or dungeon environments. Hazards include extreme weather conditions like blistering heat or subzero temperatures, natural features like quicksand or lava pits, and residual magic in the environment such as magical mists or overgrown briars. 

Hazards are flexible in scale, allowing them to affect part of an area (such as one pool in a cave complex) or an entire terrain feature (such as a 3-mile stretch of forest). Hazards impose an array of challenges on their own but shine brightest when paired with other kinds of threats. For example, the effects of extreme cold are a minor challenge to PCs all alone but become more meaningful when those same PCs contend with the possibility of freezing to death while racing across a snowy mountain range ahead of a white dragon. 
### Hazards Descriptions
This section features a collection of ready-to-use hazards, or you can use this section as inspiration when crafting your own hazards. These hazards don’t include exact sizes, as many of them can cover a wide range of areas. For example, the briar growth hazard can grow along the back of a noble’s garden or fill a forest’s floor. Similarly, extreme cold can be localized to the summit of a mountain or fill an entire geographical region. 

Many hazards are obvious, such as extreme cold in an arctic region or thorny vines covering the ground, but some might not be as obvious. For the less-obvious hazards that can be detected before creatures stumble into them, the information to detect or recognize the hazard is noted in its details. 
#### ACIDIC RAIN #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
Depending on the severity, acidic rain might be either rainwater infused with acid or showers of pure acid. 

**_Trigger:_** When a creature travels through an area affected by acidic rain, it suffers the hazard’s effects. Areas regularly affected by acidic rain feature pock marks, claw-like streaks, and other signs of erosion on stone and metal surfaces. These features can be recognized as signs of acidic rain with a successful DC 13 INT (Investigation or Nature) check. 

**_Effects:_** For every 10 minutes a creature spends in acidic rain, it must succeed on a DC 13 CON save or take 1d4 acid damage. If the acidic rain is not diluted by rainwater and is pure acid, the DC increases to 15, and the creature must make the save every 1 minute it spends in the acidic rain. If a creature fails the save by 5 or more, its armor also corrodes, taking a permanent and cumulative −1 penalty to the AC it offers. Armor reduced to an AC of 10 is destroyed. 

**_Resolution:_** An affected creature must find shelter or leave the area.
#### ALCHEMICAL FALLOUT #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
Alchemical fallout is the combination of sludge and fluid that results as the by-product of alchemical development. It is most commonly found in large urban areas, especially in high-magic settings. 

**_Trigger:_** When a creature enters an area of alchemical fallout for the first time on a turn, starts its turn there, or comes into physical contact with the fallout, it suffers the hazard’s effects.    

**_Effects:_** A creature exposed to this hazard must make a DC 15 CON save. On a failure, the creature takes 11 (2d10) poison damage and is poisoned for 1 minute. On a success, the creature takes half the damage and isn’t poisoned. Alternatively, a creature that fails this save takes the damage but rolls a d8 and consults the **Alchemical Fallout Effects** table instead of being poisoned. 

**_Resolution:_** An affected creature must leave the area or otherwise stop being in contact with the fallout.

#### ALKALINE TERRAIN #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
Alkaline crystals in the soil irritate bleeding injuries when the soil is stirred up. This hazard is typically encountered in the desert and in salt flats, and it is especially dangerous when stirred into a sandstorm. 

**Trigger:** When a creature takes slashing damage in alkaline terrain, it suffers the hazard’s effects. Alkaline crystals can be detected in soil with a successful DC 10 INT (Nature) check while inspecting the soil. 

**Effects:** A creature that takes slashing damage while in this hazard takes an extra 2 (1d4) slashing damage from the stinging crystals in the air. This damage increases to 5 (2d4) if the soil is stirred up into a sandstorm. 

**Resolution:** An affected creature must find shelter, if the soil is stirred up into a sandstorm, or leave the area.

#### ANTIMAGIC TERRAIN #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
Antimagic terrain is any area where magic fails to function. It occurs most often in locations that have been devastated by magic, the resulting magical backlash creating the hazard. A location can’t be affected by both antimagic terrain and arcane instability at the same time. 

**Trigger:** When a creature enters antimagic terrain, it suffers the effects of the hazard. 

**Effects:** This hazard functions like the _antimagic field_ spell, except it can be of any size, is permanent, and can either be a naturally occurring part of another terrain feature, such as a crater or outcropping, or can float along the ground like a heavy gas, slowly moving from place to place as if on unseen winds. Creatures that stay in antimagic terrain run the risk of contracting the nullifying blight disease. 

**Resolution:** An affected creature must leave the area. 

#### ARCANE INSTABILITY #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
Like antimagic terrain, an area of arcane instability occurs most often in locations that have been devastated by magic, which infuses the surrounding terrain. This infused magic leaps chaotically around the area, alternatively helping and harming those within it. A location can’t be affected by both antimagic terrain and arcane instability at the same time. 

**Trigger:** When a creature enters an area of arcane stability for the first time on a turn or starts its turn there, it suffers the effects of the hazard. An area of arcane instability might occasionally display brief sparks of magic but is otherwise undetectable from a distance. 

**Effects:** A creature exposed to this hazard must roll a die. On an odd result, the creature takes 5 (1d10) damage of a type determined by rolling a d8 and consulting the **Arcane Instability Damage** table. On an even result, the next attack roll the creature makes before the start of its next turn deals an extra 5 (1d10) damage of a type determined by rolling a d8 and consulting the **Arcane Instability Damage** table. 

**Resolution:** An affected creature must leave the area. Alternatively, a creature protected by the _protection from energy_ spell or the _antimagic field_ spell is immune to the effects of this hazard.

#### BLIZZARD #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
A blizzard is a mix of heavy snowfall and high wind, chilling those within to the bone. 

**Trigger:** When a creature enters the area of a blizzard, it suffers the effects of the hazard. 

**Effects:** The ground in the blizzard’s area is difficult terrain, and the blizzard’s winds impose disadvantage on ranged attacks. A creature in the area can’t see beyond 10 feet and has disadvantage on WIS (Perception) checks to smell or hear beyond that range. A Small or Tiny creature must succeed on a DC 13 STR save or fall prone at the start of each of its turns while in the area, and a flying creature must land at the end of each turn spent in the area or make a DC 13 STR save. On a failure, the flying creature falls to the ground, taking falling damage, as normal. In addition, every minute a creature is exposed to the blizzard, it must succeed on a DC 10 CON save or suffer one level of exhaustion. The DC increases by 1 for each minute of continued exposure beyond the first. A creature automatically succeeds on these saves if it is immune to cold damage or has natural adaptation to cold climates. A creature has advantage on these saves if it is resistant to cold damage or is wearing dry winter clothing. A creature has disadvantage on these saves if it is vulnerable to cold damage, wearing metal armor, wearing clothing not designed for winter conditions, or is wet. 

**Resolution:** An affected creature must find shelter or leave the area. 

#### BLOOD BELLS #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
These plants feature gracefully arching stems that end in bell-shaped flowers ranging in color from magenta to crimson. When disturbed, the bells release a pollen that clings to nearby creatures and attracts animals and insects with signals of food. 

**Trigger:** When a creature enters a patch of blood bells, it suffers the effects of the hazard. A patch of blood bells can be recognized with a successful DC 13 INT (Nature) or WIS (Survival) check. 

**Effects:** Blood bells deposit sticky, red pollen on the clothing, fur, and skin of creatures that move through them, coating the creatures. Beasts have advantage on attack rolls against a creature coated in blood bell pollen, and they can pinpoint the location of any creature coated in the pollen within the Beast’s normal range of senses, automatically detecting the creature if it hides. 

**Resolution:** The pollen loses its potency after 8 hours. Alternatively, it can be removed by spending 5 minutes washing it off with water. 

#### BRIAR GROWTH #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
Briar growth is brush rife with thorns that impedes movement and scratches those that travel through it. 

**Trigger:** When a creature enters an area of briar growth, it suffers the effects of the hazard. 

**Effects:** An area covered in briar growth is difficult terrain. For every 5 feet a creature moves within briar growth, it takes 5 (1d10) piercing damage. A creature that moves more than 5 feet within briar growth must succeed on a DC 13 DEX save or be restrained by the thorns. A creature, including the restrained creature, can take its action to free the restrained creature by succeeding on a DC 13 STR check. 

**Resolution:** Each 10-foot cube patch of briar growth has 20 hit points and AC 13, and it is vulnerable to slashing damage and immune to poison and psychic damage. If a patch of briar growth takes at least 5 slashing or fire damage, each creature that moves through that patch has advantage on the DEX save. If a patch of briar growth is reduced to 0 hit points, it is destroyed and no longer affects that area. Alternatively, the _speak with plants_ spell suppresses the effects of briar growth in an area, provided that area of briar growth is nonmagical. 

#### BURNING RAIN #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
Burning rain is a catchall term for any fiery substance falling like precipitation from the sky. Such rain is typically droplets of liquid fire called down by powerful magic or divine whim, though it can also be used for burning ash that falls during a volcanic eruption or similar natural occurrences. Storms of burning rain typically last only a few minutes. 

**Trigger:** When a creature enters burning rain for the first time on a turn or starts its turn there, it suffers the effects of the hazard. Areas regularly affected by burning rain tend to be devoid of all but the hardiest plants and show signs of frequent fires, such as swaths of burnt plants and scorched rock. These features can be recognized as signs of burning rain with a successful DC 15 INT (Arcana or Nature) check. 

**Effects:** The creature must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or take 5 (1d10) fire damage. In addition, on initiative count 20 (losing initiative ties) each round, the burning rain has a 25 percent chance of igniting a random space no larger than a 5-foot cube in the burning rain’s area. If the space doesn’t contain flammable material, the fire dies out on initiative count 20 of the next round. Otherwise, each flammable item or plant in that space that isn’t being worn or carried catches fire and remains on fire until extinguished (requiring an action or a suitable natural occurrence, such as water-based rain). An item on fire takes 5 (1d10) fire damage on initiative count 20 of each round. At the GM’s discretion, items on fire in a space might cause the fire to spread to other spaces when the items take that fire damage, provided the conditions are appropriate for spreading fire, such as if a nearby space has dry flora. 

**Resolution:** An affected creature must find shelter or leave the area. 

#### CRUMBLING MONUMENT #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
Monuments are typically statues, altars, murals, or other large objects that depict heroes, gods, ancient stories, or something similar. They are most often made of stone, glass, or wood, and monuments left without care and upkeep, such as those in long-abandoned ruins, eventually decay, crack, and fall apart. This can be a hazard to anyone nearby, especially if combat or other action causes collateral damage to the teetering monument. 

**Trigger:** When a creature leans or pushes against the monument or the monument takes any damage, the monument crumbles. A monument on the verge of collapsing can be recognized with a successful DC 15 INT (Investigation) check. 

**Effects:** When the monument crumbles, each creature within 10 feet of it must make a DC 15 DEX save. On a failure, a creature takes 11 (2d10) bludgeoning damage and is knocked prone and buried in rubble. On a success, a creature takes half the damage and isn’t knocked prone or buried. A buried creature is restrained and unable to breathe or stand up. 

A particularly large monument might deal more damage, or a monument made of a material other than stone might deal a different type of damage, such as slashing for a crumbling glass or crystalline monument. 

**Resolution:** If a creature is buried in rubble, a creature, including the buried creature, can take its action to free the buried creature by succeeding on a DC 13 STR check. Monuments typically crumble only once, destroying the hazard once triggered. If a creature knows the monument is on the verge of collapse, it can work to shore up the monument and prevent the collapse with 10 minutes of work and the appropriate materials. 

#### DESECRATED GROUND #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
Cemeteries, catacombs, fiendish battlefields, and unholy sepulchers are often tainted with unseen traces of a malevolence that empowers Undead. Desecrated ground might be created if the site is used for necromantic rituals, is where an archfiend died, or is where a sudden and great loss of life occurred. 

**Trigger:** When a creature enters desecrated ground, it suffers the effects of the hazard. 

**Effects:** While in the area, a creature that isn’t a Construct or Undead has disadvantage on death saves and on saves against effects that deal necrotic damage. A creature that deals radiant damage while in the area deals only half the normal radiant damage, and a creature that regains hit points magically, such as from the _cure wounds_ spell, regains only half the hit points. Each Fiend and Undead in the area has advantage on saves against effects that turn Fiends or Undead, such as Turn the Profane. 

**Resolution:** Sprinkling three vials of holy water on a space of desecrated ground suppresses the effects of the hazard within 10 feet of that space for 10 minutes. Desecrated ground can be permanently cleansed and turned into mundane, nonmagical ground by consecrating the area with a _hallow_ spell cast at the center of the area. 

#### EXTREME COLD #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
Exposure to temperatures below 0 degrees Fahrenheit threatens creatures not adapted to the cold. The hazard is generally continual. 

**Trigger:** This hazard begins when a creature is exposed to extreme cold for at least 1 hour. A creature avoids exposure if it is resistant or immune to cold damage, if it has natural adaptation to cold climates, or it is wearing dry winter clothing such as a heavy coat and furs, gloves, and a hat. 

**Effects:** After the first hour, an exposed creature must succeed on a DC 10 CON save or suffer one level of exhaustion. The creature must repeat this save at the end of each hour of continued exposure, increasing the DC by 1 for each hour of exposure beyond the first (DC 11 at 2 hours, DC 12 at 3 hours, etc.). Creatures have disadvantage on these saves if they are vulnerable to cold damage, wearing metal armor, wearing clothing not designed for winter conditions, or are wet. 

**Resolution:** If an exposed creature is removed from the affected area or otherwise avoids exposure for at least 1 hour, the CON save made against this hazard resets to DC 10. Otherwise, this hazard ends once a creature leaves the affected area or environmental conditions significantly change.

#### EXTREME HEAT #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
Exposure to temperatures above 100 degrees Fahrenheit threatens creatures not adapted to the heat. The hazard is generally continual. 

**Trigger:** This hazard begins when a creature is exposed to extreme heat for at least 1 hour. A creature avoids exposure if it is resistant or immune to fire damage, if it has natural adaptation to hot climates, or if it has access to twice its daily amount of required drinking water. 

**Effects:** After the first hour, an exposed creature must succeed on a DC 10 CON save or suffer one level of exhaustion. The creature must repeat this save at the end of each hour of continued exposure, increasing the DC by 1 for each hour of exposure beyond the first (DC 11 at 2 hours, DC 12 at 3 hours, etc.). Creatures have disadvantage on these saves if they are vulnerable to fire damage, wearing metal armor or Heavy armor, or wearing clothing not designed for hot climates. 

**Resolution:** If an exposed creature is removed from the affected area or otherwise avoids exposure for at least 1 hour, the CON save made against this hazard resets to DC 10. Otherwise, this hazard ends once a creature leaves the affected area or environmental conditions significantly change. 

#### FREEZING WATERS #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
Icy waters can sap the strength of hearty barbarians and ill-adapted creatures alike, exhausting muscles and spirits and threatening a cold end from the exposure. Immersion in exceptionally cold water causes creatures to lose heat faster than their bodies generate it. 

**Trigger:** When at least half a creature’s body is immersed in water that is 40 degrees Fahrenheit or colder, it suffers the effects of the hazard. A creature that is resistant or immune to cold damage or that has natural adaptation to cold climates is immune to the effects of the hazard. 

**Effects:** A creature can be immersed in frigid water for a number of minutes equal to its CON modifier before suffering its effects. At the end of each minute after that, the creature must succeed on a DC 10 CON save or suffer one level of exhaustion. A creature that is vulnerable to cold damage has disadvantage on this save. 

**Resolution:** An affected creature must leave the area. 

#### GALE WINDS #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
Heavy gusting winds can appear with little warning, battering the weak and impairing many activities. Gale winds disperse fog, extinguish open flames, and make movement difficult for small and flying creatures. As a weather hazard, it is usually only minutes long, though in canyons or mountainous regions, it can be continual. 

**Trigger:** When a creature enters an area affected by gale winds, it suffers the effects of the hazard. 

**Effects:** Gale winds impose disadvantage on ranged attacks and on WIS (Perception) checks that rely on hearing. A Small or Tiny creature must succeed on a DC 11 STR save or be knocked prone at the start of each of its turns while in the area. A flying creature must land at the end of each turn spent in the area or make a DC 11 STR save. On a failure, the creature falls to the ground, taking falling damage, as normal. 

**Resolution:** An affected creature must find shelter or leave the area. 

#### GHASTLY SWARD #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
The plants that make up this hazard have broad, scalloped leaves that curl out from a pulpy, central mass. The central mass exudes a foul smell reminiscent of rotting flesh, which fills the nostrils of all who smell it. 

**Trigger:** When a creature enters a patch of ghastly sward, it suffers the effects of the hazard. A patch of ghastly sward can be recognized with a successful DC 15 INT (Nature) or WIS (Survival) check. A creature with a heightened sense of smell has advantage on this check, as its fine-tuned sense can detect the subtle difference between the plant’s smell and the actual smell of rotting flesh. 

**Effects:** The plants in the sward smear a smelly, viscous pollen on the clothing, fur, and skin of creatures that move through them, coating the creatures. While coated in the pollen, a creature has disadvantage on WIS (Perception) checks that rely on sight and smell, as the pollen’s scent fills its nose and causes its eyes to water. Each hour the creature remains coated in the pollen or within the patch of ghastly sward, it must succeed on a DC 13 CON save or be poisoned for 1 hour as the prolonged exposure to the putrid scent nauseates the creature. 

**Resolution:** The pollen loses its potency after 8 hours. Alternatively, it can be removed by spending 5 minutes washing it off with water.

#### HORNET NEST #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
This hazard appears to be the hive of relatively mild honeybees but actually houses a swarm of easily agitated hornets. 

**Trigger:** When a creature touches the hornet nest or moves to a space within 5 feet of it, the creature suffers the effects of the hazard. The nest can be recognized as a hazard with a successful DC 13 WIS (Survival) check, provided the creature making the check can hear or see the nest. 

**Effects:** When triggered, hundreds of hornets fly out of the nest, stinging every nearby creature for up to 1 hour. On initiative count 20 (losing initiative ties) of each round, each creature within 30 feet of the nest must make a DC 13 DEX save. On a failure, a creature takes 5 (2d4) piercing damage and is poisoned until the start of its next turn. On a success, a creature takes half the damage and isn’t poisoned. 

**Resolution:** If no creature (other than the hornets themselves) is within 30 feet of the nest on initiative count 20, the hornets return to the nest, ending the effects of this hazard until triggered again. Alternatively, the nest can be attacked and destroyed (AC 10; 5 hit points; vulnerability to fire damage; immunity to poison and psychic damage), which causes the hornets to disperse. 

#### HYPERSALINE WATER #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
A hypersaline body of water contains toxic levels of salt. 

**Trigger:** When a creature is immersed in hypersaline water, it suffers the effects of the hazard. 

**Effects:** A creature swimming in hypersaline water increases any ability check DCs related to swimming by 5, except for checks to remain afloat. A creature attempting to remain afloat in hypersaline water reduces any ability check DCs related to floating by 5. A creature that can normally breathe underwater can’t breathe in hypersaline water, and magic items, spells, and other effects that magically allow a creature to breathe underwater, such as a _cloak of the manta ray_ or the _water breathing_ spell, don’t allow the creature to breathe in hypersaline water. 

**Resolution:** An affected creature must leave the area. 

#### LAVA #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
Molten rock from deep beneath the earth that erupts onto the surface is a death sentence for most creatures. 

**Trigger:** When a creature enters lava for the first time on a turn or starts its turn there, it suffers the effects of the hazard. 

**Effects:** The creature must make a DC 17 CON save, taking 55 (10d10) fire damage on a failed save, or half as much damage on a successful one. While a creature is within 30 feet of a pool of lava, it has disadvantage on checks and saves against exhaustion, and it has disadvantage on attack rolls with weapons if it is wearing medium or heavier armor or if it is wielding a two-handed weapon, as the rippling heat wears at the creature. A creature is immune to this secondary effect of lava if it is resistant or immune to fire damage. 

**Resolution:** An affected creature must leave the area. 

#### LIGHTNING STORM #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
A lightning storm is characterized by random strikes of lightning and is sometimes accompanied by gale winds or torrential precipitation. 

**Trigger:** When a creature enters the area of a lightning storm, it suffers the effects of the hazard. 

**Effects:** Every minute a creature is in the lightning storm, it must succeed on a DC 15 DEX save or take 11 (2d10) lightning damage. A creature has disadvantage on this save if it is wearing metal armor or wearing or carrying a metal weapon. 

**Resolution:** An affected creature must find shelter or leave the area. 

#### NECROTIC MOLD #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
This gray and black mold grows in graveyards, mausoleums, crypts, and other areas filled with necrotic energies and undead. 

**Trigger:** When a creature enters a space within 5 feet of a patch of necrotic mold, it suffers the effects of the hazard. A patch of necrotic mold can be recognized as such with a successful DC 17 INT (Nature or Religion) check. 

**Effects:** When triggered, the mold releases a burst of spores in a 10-foot-radius sphere centered on the triggering creature. Each creature in that sphere must make a DC 15 CON save, taking 22 (4d10) necrotic damage on a failed save, or half as much damage on a successful one. For every 20 necrotic damage it deals to creatures that aren’t Constructs or Undead, the necrotic mold grows in size, expanding by one 5-foot space.

**Resolution:** An affected creature must leave the area to avoid triggering the hazard again. Alternatively, any amount of fire damage destroys a 5-foot patch of necrotic mold. 

#### QUICKSAND #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
Quicksand is a dangerous mix of water and loose soil or sand that causes creatures in it to sink into the ground. Undisturbed quicksand typically appears to be little more than sandy ground devoid of flora. 

**Trigger:** When a creature enters quicksand for the first time on a turn or starts its turn there, it suffers the effects of the hazard. Quicksand can be recognized with a successful DC 13 INT (Nature) or WIS (Survival) check. 

**Effects:** The creature sinks 1d4 feet into the quicksand and becomes restrained. At the start of each of the creature’s turns, it sinks an additional 1d4 feet. A breathing creature completely submerged in quicksand begins suffocating. 

**Resolution:** An affected creature must leave the area. If a creature restrained by quicksand isn’t completely submerged, a creature, including the restrained creature, can take its action to free the restrained creature by succeeding on a STR check with a DC equal to 10 plus the number of feet the creature has sunk into the sand. 

>[!rules] VARIANT QUICKSAND
>Quicksand is a versatile hazard that can be adjusted to work for a variety of similar dangers. Here are a few options for using quicksand in different ways. Each of these variants creates effects in addition to quicksand’s normal effects. 
>
>**_Doom Sand._** Found most often on the Plane of Shadow or areas where the border between the Material Plane and the Plane of Shadow is thin, doom sand causes despair in those stuck within it. A creature that starts its turn restrained by doom sand must succeed on a DC 13 WIS save or become despondent until it finishes a short or long rest. A despondent creature has advantage on saves against being charmed, but it has disadvantage on ability checks and other saves. A _calm emotions_ spell suppresses the despondent effect for the spell’s duration, while a _restoration_ spell or similar magic ends the effect. 
>
>**_Dry Quicksand._** A waterless quicksand, dry quicksand is looser, making it easier for creatures to sink but also to be rescued. A creature in dry quicksand sinks 1d4 + 2 feet each turn instead. However, the DC to free a creature restrained by dry quicksand is equal to only 5 plus the number of feet the creature has sunk into the sand. 
>
>**_Freezing Slush._** Freezing slush, quicksand composed of a mixture of water and snow, appears most often on planes filled with ice and snow, but powerful or long-lasting blizzards on the Material Plane can cause pockets of freezing slush to form. A creature that starts its turn restrained in freezing slush must succeed on a DC 13 CON save or begin to freeze solid. At the end of each of its turns, it can repeat the save. On a success, the effect ends for the creature. If the creature fails this save three times, it is petrified in ice until it spends 1 minute in a warm environment or being warmed by a fire. 
>
>**_Heated Tar Pit._** Tar pits function like quicksand, but heated tar pits slowly boil those trapped within them. A creature that starts its turn in a heated tar pit takes 5 (1d10) fire damage.

#### SANDSTORM #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
A sandstorm happens when wind whips up sand in a desert or similarly sandy environment. It is often accompanied by gale winds. 

**Trigger:** When a creature enters the area of a sandstorm, it suffers the effects of the hazard. 

**Effects:** The ground in the sandstorm’s area is difficult terrain. Every minute the creature remains within the sandstorm, it must succeed on a DC 13 STR save or be buried in sand. A buried creature is restrained and unable to breathe or stand up. A creature, including the buried creature, can take its action to free the buried creature by succeeding on a DC 13 STR check.

#### SLIPPERY SURFACE #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
A slippery surface is any surface that is difficult to balance on, such as an icy walkway, a rampart covered in oil, an alley slick with alchemical slime, or a rain-soaked rooftop. 

**Trigger:** When a creature moves 5 feet or more across a slippery surface on its turn, it suffers the effects of the hazard when it stops moving. 

**Effects:** The creature must succeed on a DEX (Acrobatics) check or fall prone. The DC is equal to 10 plus 1 for every 5 feet the creature moved on its turn.  

**Resolution:** An affected creature must leave the area. At the GM’s discretion and if the environmental conditions support it, an affected area can be cleaned, dried, or otherwise cleared of the hazard with 10 minutes of work and the appropriate materials. 

#### TAINTED FONT #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
A tainted font is a water fountain, well, or sink that has been desecrated by profane power. 

**Trigger:** When a creature enters the font for the first time on a turn or starts its turn there, it suffers the effects of the hazard. A creature that drinks water in the font also suffers the effects of the hazard. A tainted font can be recognized with a successful DC 15 INT (Religion) check. 

**Effects:** The creature must succeed on a DC 15 CHA save or become cursed by the font until it finishes a long rest. When a cursed target makes an ability check, attack roll, or save, it must roll a d4 and subtract the number rolled from the ability check, attack roll, or save. 

**Resolution:** Sprinkling a vial of holy water in the tainted font suppresses the effects of the hazard for 10 minutes. A tainted font can be permanently cleansed and turned into a mundane, nonmagical font by consecrating the area with a _hallow_ spell cast on the font. A creature cursed by the font can be cursed with a _remove curse_ spell or similar magic. 

#### TORRENTIAL PRECIPITATION #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
Precipitation such as heavy rain or snowfall causes a variety of problems. It can last from minutes to days. 

**Trigger:** This hazard begins when a creature enters an area affected by torrential precipitation. 

**Effects:** Everything within the area is considered lightly obscured. A creature that starts its turn within torrential precipitation has disadvantage on WIS (Perception) checks. If the torrential precipitation is heavy rain, it extinguishes open flame. If it is heavy snowfall, the ground is difficult terrain. 

**Resolution:** An affected creature must find shelter or leave the area. 

#### URUSHI PLANTS #css/h/feature

**Trigger:** When a creature enters a patch of urushi plants, it must succeed on a DC 13 CON save or suffer the effects of the hazard. While the rash persists, the creature has disadvantage on INT and WIS checks as the rash’s itchiness distracts it. **Resolution:** The rash fades at the end of the duration. Alternatively, an anti-itch salve can be applied to the rash to end the effect early. A creature that has proficiency with herbalist tools and access to a natural forest can find the necessary plants and create the salve with 1d4 hours of work. 

#### VIOLET MOLD #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
This blue and purple mold grows in cool, damp environments, typically where there is little sunlight.

#### WEBS #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
This hazard is typically made up of the sticky webbing created by giant spiders, but it can also apply to any hazard made up of tangled rope, strings, or nets. 

**Trigger:** When a creature enters the webs for the first time on a turn or starts its turn there, it suffers the effects of the hazard.

#### WILD MIST #css/h/feature 
_Hazard_ 
This magic-infused mist appears as a blanket of iridescent fog. 

![[wild-mist.webp|right|384]] **Trigger:** When a creature casts a spell that expends a 1st-circle or higher spell slot while in the mist, it suffers the effects of the hazard. A patch of wild mists can be recognized with a successful DC 13 INT (Arcana) check. 

**Effects:** Roll a d6. On a 1–3, the spell fizzles, failing to cast, and the spell slot is lost. On a 4–5, the creature casts a completely different spell, chosen randomly from among the creature’s known or prepared spells, but the creature expends only the spell slot it expended to cast the original spell, regardless of the final spell that is cast. ## Traps
Traps are commonly encountered in dungeons and high-security locations. Traps are intentionally built to protect important objects or creatures. They are also created to keep such things contained. Most traps deal damage, but they can also be alarms or warnings. 

Disarming a trap prevents the trap’s trigger from activating the trap’s effects. However, some traps can be triggered by a failed attempt to disarm them. Such traps note that in the resolution section of the trap’s description. If a trap doesn’t specify that failing to disarm it triggers it, the trap remains set after the failure. At the GM’s discretion, a creature that successfully disarms a trap can choose to allow it to be reset (typically by repeating the disarm check) or to permanently disarm the trap, destroying it. 

As PCs gain levels, use the effect severity guidelines (see **Effect Severity** in this chapter) to scale up trap effects and keep them meaningful. PC-facing rules for detecting and disarming traps are covered in the **Exploration Encounters** section of the _Player’s Guide_.

>[!rules] TRAP ATTACK ROLLS
>Some of the traps in this section require an attack roll be made against one or more creatures in the affected area. A trap isn’t a creature and has no senses, so because of this, it ignores any effect that would hinder its attack roll by disrupting its senses. For example, a trap doesn’t have disadvantage on attack rolls against invisible creatures, but an effect that somehow hinders its attack or protects the target by some other means, such as the _shield_ spell, affects the attack roll as normal. 
>
>If there are no valid targets in the trap’s area when it makes an attack, the trap still triggers as normal but the trap doesn’t hit anything.

### Trap Types
A trap can be either mechanical or magical. Either kind can also be complex, which is noted in the trap’s description. 
#### Mechanical Traps
Mechanical traps include things that depend on a mechanism to operate, such as pits, arrow traps, falling blocks, water-filled rooms, and whirling blades. 
#### Magic Traps
Magic traps consist of magical devices that activate spell effects. Spells like _glyph of warding_ and _symbol_ function as magic traps. 
#### Complex Traps
Complex traps continue to execute on rounds after activation instead of triggering once and being done. For example, a trap that slowly floods a room is a complex trap. On the trap’s turn, the water level rises. After several rounds, the room is completely flooded. 

Complex traps can start initiative and act independently in initiative order. On each of its turns, the trap takes an action, usually making successive attacks or maintaining an effect that changes over time. Until triggered, a complex trap can be detected and disabled like a standard trap. 
### Trap Descriptions
This section features a collection of ready-to-use traps for your game, or you can use them simply as inspiration when crafting your own. If a trap can be detected before a creature stumbles into it, that information is noted in the trap’s details. 

#### COLLAPSING ROOF #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Mechanical)_ 
This trap uses a trip wire to collapse supports that are holding up an unstable section of ceiling, which is often intentionally made unstable for this purpose. This trap can also be used to represent a collection of boulders or rocks suspended above an area. 

**_Trigger:_** When a creature moves through the trap’s area or otherwise hits the trip wire, it activates the trap. A creature can notice the trap with a successful DC 10 WIS (Perception) check. 

**_Effects:_** Each creature in the trap’s area must make a DC 15 DEX save, taking 22 (4d10) bludgeoning damage on a failure, or half as much damage on a success. Once triggered, the ground in the trap’s area is covered in rubble and is difficult terrain. 

**_Resolution:_** A ceiling or roof can typically collapse only once, permanently ending the trap once triggered. The trap can be disarmed with a successful DC 15 DEX (Thieves’ Tools) check. A creature without thieves’ tools can attempt this check with disadvantage using any edged weapon or edged tool. If a creature fails a check to disarm the trap, it triggers. 

#### FALLING NET #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Mechanical)_ 
This trap uses a trip wire to release a net suspended from the ceiling. The trip wire is a few inches off the ground and stretches between two columns, trees, or similar anchor points. The net is typically hidden from view by natural covering, such as cobwebs or foliage, or by minor illusory magic. 

**Trigger:** When a creature moves through the trap’s area or otherwise hits the trip wire, it activates the trap. A creature can notice the trap with a successful DC 10 WIS (Perception) check. 

**Effects:** When the trap is triggered, the net is released, covering a 10-foot-by-10-foot area. Those in the area are trapped under the net and restrained, and those that fail a DC 10 STR save are also knocked prone. A creature, including the restrained creature, can take its action to free the restrained creature by succeeding on a DC 10 STR check. 

**Resolution:** The net has 20 hit points and AC 10, and it is immune to poison and psychic damage. If the net is reduced to 0 hit points, it is destroyed, freeing any creatures trapped by it. A net can typically fall only once, permanently ending the trap once triggered, unless the trap includes a mechanism that pulls the net back into place. The trap can be disarmed with a successful DC 15 DEX (Thieves’ Tools) check. A creature without thieves’ tools can attempt this check with disadvantage using any edged weapon or edged tool. If a creature fails a check to disarm the trap, it triggers. 

#### FIRE-BREATHING STATUE #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Magic)_ 
This trap releases a magical gout of flame from a statue. Depending on the shape and type of the statue, the fire might be emitted from its mouth, weapon, or hand, typically spreading across a doorway or over a treasure it protects. 

**Trigger:** When more than 20 pounds of weight is placed on the trap’s pressure plate, the trap activates. A creature can notice the trap with a successful DC 17 WIS (Perception) check. A spell or other effect that can sense the presence of magic, such as the _detect magic_ spell, reveals an aura of evocation magic around the statue. 

**Effects:** The statue releases a 30-foot cone of fire. Each creature in the cone must make a DC 13 DEX save, taking 22 (4d10) fire damage on a failed save, or half as much damage on a successful one. 

**Resolution:** The trap can be disarmed with a successful DC 13 INT (Arcana) check. If a creature fails a check to disarm the trap, it triggers. Wedging an iron spike or other object under the pressure plate prevents the trap from activating. Alternatively, a successful _dispel magic_ spell (DC 13) cast on the statue destroys the trap. 

#### GRAVITY ORB #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Complex Magic)_ 
This trap is an enchanted orb that pulses with arcane power. With each pulse, creatures are drawn closer to the sphere. The orb is typically placed on a pedestal or ledge over a chasm where those drawn toward it might fall to their doom. 

**Trigger:** When a creature moves to a space within 15 feet of the orb without speaking the command phrase, it activates the trap, causing the orb to begin pulsing. A creature can notice the trap with a successful DC 15 WIS (Perception) check. A spell or other effect that can sense the presence of magic, such as the _detect magic_ spell, reveals an aura of transmutation magic around the orb. 

**Effects:** Each creature within 30 feet of the orb must succeed on a DC 15 STR save or be pulled up to 15 feet toward the orb and knocked prone. On initiative count 20 (losing initiative ties) of each round, the orb pulses again, and each creature within 30 feet of the orb must make the same save. 

**Resolution:** The orb stops pulsing if no creature is within 30 feet of it on initiative count 20, ending the effects of this trap until triggered again. A creature within 5 feet of the orb can disarm it by succeeding on a DC 15 INT (Arcana) check. Alternatively, a successful _dispel magic_ spell (DC 13) cast on the orb destroys the trap. 

#### GRAVITY WELL #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Complex Magic)_ 
This trap repeatedly reverses gravity to pulp creatures caught in its area. 

**Trigger:** When a creature moves to a space within 5 feet of a magical rune painted in the center of the trap’s area. A creature can notice the rune with a successful DC 13 WIS (Perception), and a creature aware of the rune can discern the trap’s nature with a successful DC 15 INT (Arcana) check. A spell or other effect that can sense the presence of magic, such as the _detect magic_ spell, reveals an aura of transmutation magic in the trap’s area. 

**Effects:** Each creature and item that isn’t being worn or carried within 30 feet of the rune falls upward, stopping at the ceiling or 50 feet, whichever occurs first. A creature can make a DC 15 DEX save to grab onto a fixed object it can reach, thus avoiding the fall. If some solid object, such as a ceiling, is encountered in this fall, falling items and creatures strike it just as they would during a normal downward fall, taking falling damage as normal. If an item or creature reaches the top of the area without striking anything, it remains there, oscillating slightly until the gravity changes again. On initiative count 20 (losing initiative ties) of the next round, the gravity reverses, and each creature within 30 feet of the rune falls downward, taking falling damage if it strikes an object, as normal. The gravity continues to reverse, sending creatures falling up or down, on initiative count 20 (losing initiative ties) each round for 1 minute. 

**Resolution:** After 1 minute, the trap stops reversing gravity, returning gravity in the area to normal and ending the effects of the trap until triggered again. The trap can be destroyed, immediately ending its effects, by damaging the rune. Alternatively, a successful _dispel magic_ spell (DC 13) cast on the rune destroys the trap. 

#### HANGING MACE #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Mechanical)_ 
This trap uses a pressure plate to release an overhead-mounted ball and chain, which swings down to strike the tops of creatures beneath it. 

**Trigger:** When more than 20 pounds of weight is placed on the trap’s pressure plate, the trap activates. A creature can notice the trap with a successful DC 13 WIS (Perception) check. 

**Effects:** The mace drops and swings downward toward the pressure plate. Each creature within 5 feet of the pressure plate must make a DC 13 DEX save, taking 11 (2d10) bludgeoning damage on a failed save, or half as much damage on a successful one. A creature in the area that is Small or smaller doesn’t need to make a save against this trap, unless it is elevated in some way, such as riding on a mastiff. 

**Resolution:** The hanging mace can typically fall in this way only once, permanently ending the trap once triggered, unless the trap includes a mechanism that automatically pulls the mace back into place. The trap can be disarmed with a successful DC 13 DEX (Thieves’ Tools) check. Wedging an iron spike or other object under the pressure plate prevents the trap from activating. 

#### HANGING NET #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Mechanical)_ 
This trap uses a trip wire to hoist a 10-foot-by-10-foot net—and any creatures standing atop it—into the air. 

**Trigger:** When a creature moves through the trap’s area or otherwise hits the trip wire, it activates the trap. A creature can notice the trap with a successful DC 13 WIS (Perception) check. 

**Effects:** Each creature standing in the trap’s 10-foot by 10-foot area must succeed on a DC 15 DEX save or become restrained and prone, as the net closes in and hoists the creatures to dangle 10 feet in the air. A restrained creature can take its action to free itself with a successful DC 15 STR (Athletics) check to pull the net apart or with a successful DC 15 DEX (Acrobatics) check to slip through the net. 

**Resolution:** If at least two creatures succeed on the STR (Athletics) check to pull the net apart, trapped creatures have advantage on checks made to escape the net. The net has 20 hit points and AC 10, and it is immune to poison and psychic damage. If the net is reduced to below half its hit point maximum, creatures have advantage on checks made to escape the net. If the net is reduced to 0 hit points, it is destroyed, freeing any creatures trapped by it. A net can typically be hoisted in this way only once, permanently ending the trap once triggered, unless the trap includes a mechanism that automatically pulls the net back into place. The trap can be disarmed with a successful DC 13 DEX (Thieves’ Tools) check. A creature without thieves’ tools can attempt this check with disadvantage using any edged weapon or edged tool. If a creature fails a check to disarm the trap, it triggers. 

#### HELLFIRE GOUT #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Mechanical)_ 
A tube inset into a wall or statue spews a sticky alchemical jelly. This jelly is akin to alchemist’s fire, burning its victims with fire, but other alchemical jellies that deal other types of damage can also be found in these traps. 

**Trigger:** When more than 20 pounds of weight is placed on the trap’s pressure plate, the trap activates. A creature can notice the trap, typically from scorch marks on the floor or wall opposite the trap’s tube, with a successful DC 15 WIS (Perception) check. 

**Effects:** Sticky, alchemical fluid sprays out from the wall or statue in a 15-foot cone. Each creature in the cone must make a DC 15 DEX save. On a failure, a creature takes 11 (2d10) acid, cold, fire, lightning, or poison damage (the trap maker’s choice) and is coated in alchemical jelly. On a success, a creature takes half the damage and isn’t coated. A creature coated in alchemical jelly must succeed on a DC 15 CON save at the end of its next turn or take 11 (2d10) damage of the type dealt by the trap. A creature, including the coated creature, can take its action to remove the alchemical jelly, preventing the coated creature from needing to make the save at the end of its next turn. **Resolution:** The trap can be triggered up to six times before it runs out of alchemical jelly. At that point, the trap ceases to function until refilled. The trap can be disarmed with a successful DC 15 DEX (Thieves’ Tools) check. Wedging an iron spike or other object under the pressure plate prevents the trap from activating.

#### IRON MAIDEN #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Magic)_ 
An enchanted iron maiden stands upright and at the ready, poised to capture the nearest unwary creature. 

**Trigger:** When a creature moves into a space within 5 feet of the iron maiden or touches it, the trap activates. A creature can notice the arcane runes scrawled on the iron maiden with a successful DC 12 WIS (Perception) check. A successful DC 12 INT (Arcana) check recognizes the runes as a trap that temporarily animates the iron maiden. A spell or other effect that can sense the presence of magic, such as the _detect magic_ spell, reveals an aura of transmutation magic around the iron maiden. 

**Effects:** The iron maiden attacks the creature that triggered the trap or the nearest Medium or smaller creature if the triggering creature is Large or larger. The iron maiden makes a melee attack with a +8 bonus against the creature. On a hit, the target takes 11 (2d10) piercing damage and is trapped within the iron maiden. The trapped creature is blinded and restrained, it has total cover against attacks and other effects outside the iron maiden, and it takes 5 (1d10) piercing damage on initiative count 20 (losing initiative ties) of each round. The iron maiden can have only one Medium or smaller creature trapped within it at a time. A creature, including the trapped creature, can take its action to pry open the iron maiden and free the trapped creature by succeeding on a DC 15 STR check. 

**Resolution:** The iron maiden has 30 hit points and AC 15, and it is resistant to piercing and slashing damage and immune to poison and psychic damage. If the iron maiden is reduced to below half its hit point maximum, a creature trapped within it no longer takes piercing damage on initiative count 20. If the iron maiden is reduced to 0 hit points, it is destroyed, freeing any creatures trapped within it. The trap can be disarmed with a successful DC 15 INT (Arcana) check, disrupting the power of the runes. A successful _dispel magic_ spell (DC 13) cast on the iron maiden destroys the trap. Alternatively, the iron maiden can be prevented from attacking and trapping a creature by securing rope or chains around it. 

#### LIGHTNING FLOOR #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Complex Magic)_ 
![[lightning-floor.webp|right|384]] This trap is a series of connected metal plates, each typically a 5-foot-by-5-foot square, that fills a hallway or room. In most cases, these plates cover the floor of the area, but sometimes they can cover walls or ceilings. Each is a pressure plate that electrifies when pressed, or else it electrifies when a living creature gets close to it. This electrified plate then spreads its lightning randomly to other nearby plates in a connected chain. The trap is typically powered by a network of metal wires that run between the plates and has a lightning-infused magical control switch somewhere along the network. Some trapsmiths manipulate the wires so that specific plates electrify when any one plate is activated, often lighting up a floor with a specific symbol, such as the holy symbol of a deity of storms or the symbol of the trapsmith’s faction. 

**Trigger:** When more than 20 pounds of weight is placed on a metal plate, that plate activates. Alternatively, a creature that isn’t a Plant or Undead that moves to a space within 5 feet of a metal plate that is on the wall or ceiling activates the trap. A creature can notice the metal plates contain a trap with a successful DC 13 WIS (Perception) check. A successful DC 15 INT (Investigation) check while within 10 feet of the magical control switch finds it. If a creature searching for the switch has observed the trap triggering at least once, that creature has advantage on the check and doesn’t need to be within 10 feet of the switch to find it. 

**Effects:** The activated metal plate electrifies, dealing 5 (1d10) lightning damage to the creature that triggered it. Then, the lightning travels across the plates, electrifying one plate that isn’t already electrified and is touching the first plate, which then electrifies one plate that isn’t already electrified and is touching the second plate, and so on until the first plate is connected in this chain of electrified plates to the magical switch that controls the network beneath the plates. A plate remains electrified only while a creature is continuing to trigger it or while a creature is continuing to trigger the plate that electrified others in its chain. 

Each creature on a plate when it electrifies takes 5 (1d10) lightning damage, and a creature that starts its turn on an electrified plate takes 5 (1d10) lightning damage. If the metal plates in this trap are activated by proximity rather than pressure, each creature within 5 feet of a plate when it electrifies takes 5 (1d10) lightning damage, and a creature that starts its turn within 5 feet of an electrified plate takes 5 (1d10) lightning damage. 

**Resolution:** A single plate of the trap can be disarmed by disconnecting it from the network’s wires with a successful DC 15 DEX (Thieves’ Tools) check. A creature without thieves’ tools can attempt this check with disadvantage using any edged weapon or edged tool. If a creature fails a check to disarm the trap, it triggers. The trap can be turned on or off by interacting with the magical control switch. A successful _dispel magic_ spell (DC 15) cast on the control switch destroys the trap. 
#### LOG-SPEAR #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Complex Mechanical)_ 
This trap uses a trip wire to drop a pointed log (often studded with spikes or wrapped with spears) affixed to a chain or rope into an area, where it swings back and forth like a pendulum, impaling victims. The log typically swings in an arc affecting a 10-foot line (5 feet wide), but longer or wider logs exist. 

**Trigger:** When a creature moves through the trap’s area or otherwise hits the trip wire, it activates the trap. A creature can notice the trap with a successful DC 12 WIS (Perception) check. 

**Effects:** The log swings down into the area, and each creature in the area must make a DC 13 DEX save. On a failure, a creature takes 11 (2d10) piercing damage and is knocked prone. On a success, a creature takes half the damage and isn’t knocked prone. A creature that fails the save by 5 or more is also impaled by the log. An impaled creature takes 5 (1d10) piercing damage at the start of each of its turns. A creature, including the impaled creature, can take its action to free the impaled creature by succeeding on a DC 10 STR check. 

On initiative count 20 (losing initiative ties) of the next round, the log swings back through the area, and each creature in the area must make the same save. On initiative count 20 (losing initiative ties) of each round, the log swings back and forth through the area, forcing creatures in the area to make the save, for 1 minute. 

**Resolution:** The log stops swinging after 1 minute, if any number of creatures’ DEX saves result in at least 5 total failures, or if a Large or larger creature fails the DEX save. If it stops swinging because it hits a Large or larger creature, that creature takes an extra 5 (1d10) piercing damage and is impaled, even if it didn’t fail the save by 5 or more. A log-spear can typically fall only once, permanently ending the trap once the log stops swinging, unless the trap includes a mechanism that pulls the log back into place. The trap can be disarmed with a successful DC 17 DEX (Thieves’ Tools) check. A creature without thieves’ tools can attempt this check with disadvantage using any edged weapon or edged tool. If a creature fails a check to disarm the trap, it triggers. 

#### PIT TRAP #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Mechanical)_ 
One of the simplest, most common, and most effective traps is a deep hole dug in the ground or beneath the floor, hidden by a cover made from materials that blend with the surrounding terrain. This kind of trap can also be used to represent unstable flooring in a multi-level building, ship, or similar structure, which might send a creature walking across it tumbling to a floor below. Pit traps are typically 5 feet in diameter and 10 feet deep. 

**Trigger:** When more than 20 pounds of weight is placed on the pit’s cover, the trap activates. A creature can notice the absence of foot traffic over the section of floor that forms the pit’s cover with a successful DC 15 WIS (Perception) check. A creature aware of the lack of foot traffic over the cover can determine it is the cover of a pit with a successful DC 15 INT (Investigation) check or by interacting with the cover, such as lifting a corner or lightly pressing on it from a safe space. 

**Effects:** The cover collapses under the weight or swings down like a trapdoor, causing all creatures on it to fall into the pit below. Each affected creature takes falling damage based on the pit’s depth. 

**Resolution:** The cover of a pit can be removed, but the trap otherwise can’t be disarmed. At the GM’s discretion, a pit can be filled in, or its cover can be shored up to prevent it from collapsing with at least 10 minutes of work and the appropriate materials. 

>[!rules] VARIANT PITS  
>A popular trap among Humanoids, pits can be created in a variety of styles, typically by adding something unpleasant at the bottom. 
>
>**_Beast Pit._** Some pits are used as pens or feeding areas, containing the remains of partially eaten creatures and possibly the eaters themselves. A beast pit contains at least one swarm of insects, snakes, or rats. A more diabolical version of this trap features an Ooze at the bottom instead. 
>
>**_Locking Pit._** A locking pit’s cover is a spring-loaded trapdoor. After a creature falls into the pit, the cover snaps shut, trapping the creature inside. A successful DC 20 STR check is necessary to pry or smash the cover open. A creature in the pit can also attempt to disable the spring mechanism from the inside with a successful DC 15 DEX (Thieves’ Tools) check, provided the creature can see and reach the mechanism. In some cases, a lever or button outside the pit (usually hidden nearby) opens the trapdoor. 
>
>**_Spiked Pit._** Spiked pits are lined with spears, sharpened stakes, or stalagmites. In addition to a creature taking falling damage when it falls into the pit, the spikes at the bottom deal an extra 11 (2d10) piercing damage to it. Nastier versions have poison smeared on the spikes. In such cases, a creature taking piercing damage from the spikes must also make a DC 13 CON save, taking 22 (4d10) poison damage on a failed save, or half as much damage on a successful one.

#### POISON DARTS #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Mechanical)_ 
This trap uses a pressure plate that causes poison-tipped darts to shoot from spring-loaded or pressurized tubes when pressed. These tubes are typically embedded in the surrounding walls, a nearby statue, a colorful mural or fresco, or a support pillar in a room. An area might include multiple pressure plates, each tied to its own set of darts.  

**Trigger:** When more than 20 pounds of weight is placed on the trap’s pressure plate, the trap activates. A creature can notice the tiny holes where the tubes are embedded with a successful DC 15 WIS (Perception) check. A creature aware of the tiny holes can deduce the presence and location of the pressure plate or plates with a successful DC 15 INT (Investigation) check. 

**Effects:** Four darts fly out from the tubes toward the pressure plate. Each dart makes a ranged attack with a +8 bonus against a random target within 10 feet of the pressure plate. On a hit, a target hit takes 2 (1d4) piercing damage and must succeed on a DC 15 CON save, taking 11 (2d10) poison damage on a failed save, or half as much damage on a successful one. 

**Resolution:** Wedging an iron spike or other object under the pressure plate prevents the trap from activating. Stuffing the holes with cloth or wax prevents the darts contained within from launching. 

#### POISON NEEDLE #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Mechanical)_ 
A spring-loaded, poisoned needle hides within a lock. This trap is most often found in locks protecting treasure chests, secret doors, or private wardrobes. 

**Trigger:** When a creature attempts to open the lock without the proper key, the trap activates. A creature can recognize the lock is trapped with a successful DC 20 INT (Investigation) check. 

**Effects:** The needle springs 3 inches out from the lock, striking the creature that triggered the trap or the nearest creature within range. The target takes 1 piercing damage and 11 (2d10) poison damage, and it must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or be poisoned for 1 hour. 

**Resolution:** The trap can be disarmed with a successful DC 15 DEX (Thieves’ Tools) check, which removes the needle from the lock. If a creature fails a check to disarm the trap, it triggers. 

#### POP-UP BLADES #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Mechanical)_ 
This trap uses a pressure plate to cause spring-loaded blades or spikes to burst from the ground from beneath a thin layer of dust, soil, or other debris. These traps are often placed in groups to fill hallways or create patches of dangerous flooring in rooms. 

**Trigger:** When more than 20 pounds of weight is placed on the trap’s pressure plate, the trap activates. A successful DC 17 INT (Investigation) check discerns that indentations in the ground or floor indicate the presence of submerged objects.

**Effects:** Several foot-long blades or spikes erupt at varying angles from the ground around the pressure plate. Each creature within 10 feet of the pressure plate must make a DC 17 DEX save, taking 22 (4d10) piercing damage or slashing damage (depending on if the trap features spikes or blades) on a failed save, or half as much damage on a successful one. The blades or spikes remain exposed while weight remains on the pressure plate, making the area difficult terrain. A creature that moves through the blades or spikes while they are exposed must succeed on a DC 17 DEX save or take 11 (2d10) damage of the type dealt when the trap triggered. 

**Resolution:** The trap can be disarmed with a successful DC 17 DEX (Thieves’ Tools) check. Wedging an iron spike or other object under the pressure plate prevents the trap from activating.  

#### ROLLING SPHERE #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Complex Mechanical)_ 
This trap uses a pressure plate to release a large sphere of solid stone from a hidden trapdoor in the ceiling, sending the sphere rolling into creatures along its path. 

**Trigger:** When more than 20 pounds of weight is placed on the trap’s pressure plate, the trap activates. A creature can notice the hidden trapdoor and pressure plate with a successful DC 15 INT (Investigation) or WIS (Perception) check. 

**Effects:** When triggered, all creatures present must roll initiative. The sphere rolls initiative with a +8 bonus. On its turn, it moves 60 feet in a straight line. The sphere can move through creatures’ spaces, and creatures can move through its space, treating it as difficult terrain. Whenever the sphere enters a creature’s space or a creature enters its space while it’s rolling, that creature must succeed on a DC 15 DEX save or take 55 (10d10) bludgeoning damage and be knocked prone. The sphere stops when it hits a wall or similar barrier. It can’t go around corners, but smart dungeon builders incorporate gentle, curving turns into nearby passages that allow the sphere to keep moving. 

As an action, a creature within 5 feet of the sphere can attempt to slow it down by making a DC 20 STR check. On a success, the sphere’s speed is reduced by 15 feet. If the sphere’s speed is reduced to 0, it stops moving and is no longer a threat. 

**Resolution:** Wedging an iron spike or other object under the pressure plate prevents the trap from activating. Alternatively, the trapdoor can be shored up, preventing it from opening, with magic such as the _arcane lock_ spell or with 10 minutes of work and the appropriate materials. 

#### SCYTHING BLADE #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Mechanical)_ 
This trap uses a trip wire to swing a scythe in an arc down from the ceiling, where it swings back and forth like a pendulum, slicing through unsuspecting victims. The scythe typically swings in an arc affecting a 10-foot line (5 feet wide), but some of these traps feature longer scythes. 

**Trigger:** When a creature moves through the trap’s area or otherwise hits the trip wire, it activates the trap. A creature can notice the trap with a successful DC 12 WIS (Perception) check. 

**Effects:** The scythe makes one melee attack with a +8 bonus against a random creature in the line. On a hit, the target takes 16 (3d10) slashing damage. On initiative count 20 (losing initiative ties) of the next round, the scythe swings back through the area, and it repeats the attack against a random creature in the line. On initiative count 20 (losing initiative ties) of each round, the scythe swings back and forth through the area, making the attack against one random creature in the line each time, for 1 minute. 

**Resolution:** The scythe stops swinging after 1 minute. A creature can take its action to stop the scythe’s swinging by succeeding on a DC 20 STR check, but a creature that does so takes damage as if hit by the scythe. A scythe can typically fall only once, permanently ending the trap once the scythe stops swinging, unless the trap includes a mechanism that pulls the scythe back into place. The trap can be disarmed with a successful DC 17 DEX (Thieves’ Tools) check. A creature without thieves’ tools can attempt this check with disadvantage using any edged weapon or edged tool. If a creature fails a check to disarm the trap, it triggers. 

#### SEARING OIL #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Mechanical)_ 
This trap uses a trip wire to tug a pot of oil kept up high, causing it to upend over a torch before landing on whatever is beneath it. The oil typically splatters in a 15-foot-radius, 30-foot-high cylinder centered on the ground below it, but the effect’s height can vary based on where the pot is placed, such as on a castle rampart or a hidden alcove in a dungeon ceiling. 

**Trigger:** When a creature moves through the trap’s area or otherwise hits the trip wire, it activates the trap. A creature can notice the trap with a successful DC 13 WIS (Perception) check. 

**Effects:** Each creature in the trap’s area must make a DC 15 DEX save. On a failure, a creature takes 11 (2d10) fire damage and is coated in burning oil for 1 minute. On a success, a creature takes half the damage and isn’t coated. A creature coated in burning oil takes 5 (1d10) fire damage at the end of each of its turns. A creature can take its action to put out the fire, ending the effect early. Water doesn’t put out the fire. 

**Resolution:** A pot of searing oil can be upended only once, permanently ending the trap once triggered, unless the pot is refilled with oil. The trap can be disarmed with a successful DC 15 DEX (Thieves’ Tools) check. A creature without thieves’ tools can attempt this check with disadvantage using any edged weapon or edged tool. If a creature fails a check to disarm the trap, it triggers. 

#### SWEEPING SPIKES #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Mechanical)_ 
This trap consists of several branches or boards—typically 5-foot-by-10-foot or 10-foot-by-10-foot—that are studded with spikes and held taut around a corner while connected to a trip wire. The spikes are fitted to swing around the corner at a height appropriate for hitting Medium Humanoids in the torso and Small Humanoids in the head.

**Trigger:** When a creature moves through the trap’s area or otherwise hits the trip wire, it activates the trap. A creature can notice the trap with a successful DC 13 WIS (Perception) check. 

**Effects:** Each creature within the trap’s area must make a DC 15 DEX save. On a failure, a creature takes 22 (4d10) bludgeoning damage and is pushed up to 10 feet straight away from the trap and knocked prone. On a success, a creature takes half the damage and isn’t pushed or knocked prone. If the trap’s branches or boards are studded with spikes, a creature that fails the save takes 11 (2d10) bludgeoning damage and 11 (2d10) piercing damage instead of 22 (4d10) bludgeoning damage. All other aspects of the trap are the same. 

**Resolution:** The trap can be disarmed with a successful DC 15 DEX (Thieves’ Tools) check. A creature without thieves’ tools can attempt this check with disadvantage using any edged weapon or edged tool. If a creature fails a check to disarm the trap, it triggers. 

#### TYPHOON OF DUST #css/h/feature 
_Trap (Magic)_ 
A brief storm of dust, grit, and small objects assails the area. This trap typically fills a room or hallway and has a magical crystal at the center that serves as a sensor, triggering the trap when creatures draw near. 

**Trigger:** When a creature moves to a space within 5 feet of the sensor crystal, the trap activates. A creature can notice the Tiny crystal with a successful DC 17 WIS (Perception) check. A spell or other effect that can sense the presence of magic, such as the _detect magic_ spell, reveals an aura of conjuration magic in the trap’s area. 

**Effects:** Loose sand and other small particles whip into a 20-foot-radius sandstorm. The area is heavily obscured and becomes difficult terrain. Open flames are extinguished. A flying creature must land at the end of each turn spent in the area or make a DC 11 STR save. On a failure, the creature falls to the ground, taking falling damage, as normal. A creature that starts its turn in the area takes 11 (2d10) bludgeoning damage and must succeed on a DC 17 STR save or be knocked prone. 

**Resolution:** The sandstorm lasts 1 minute or until disarmed. The trap can be disarmed by prying the crystal sensor out of its housing with an appropriate tool and a successful DC 17 STR (Athletics) check. The trap can also be disarmed with a successful DC 15 INT (Arcana) check, disrupting the enchantment in the crystal. If a creature fails a check to disarm the trap, it triggers.

# Downtime Expanded

This section introduces additional downtime activity options for PCs. See the _Player’s Guide_ for the general downtime rules. 
## Crafting Magic Items
If the GM allows it, PCs can spend downtime creating magic items like those found in the _Player’s Guide_. This activity is primarily used for making permanent magic items. Rules for crafting magic potions and scrolls are covered in the **Potion Brewing** and **Scroll Making** downtime activities later in this chapter. 

Crafting a magic item always requires access to the raw magical materials needed to make the item. It also requires proficiency in the Arcana skill or proficiency with a type of tool that could reasonably be used to craft the item. For example, a GM could rule that proficiency with smithing tools could be substituted for proficiency with the Arcana skill to craft a magic sword. 

Creating a magic item also requires the crafter to infuse a portion of their own power into the item—and a will strong enough to channel this flow of power successfully. For this reason, the greatest rarity of magic item a PC can craft is restricted by the PC’s level, as shown in the **Magic Item Crafting by Level** table. A PC can craft magic items of that rarity and all lower rarities. Artifact magic items, fabled magic items, and sentient magic items can’t be crafted by PCs.

```tx
|PC LEVEL | HIGHEST CRAFTABLE RARITY|
|---|---|
|1st|Common|
|4th|Uncommon|
|7th|Rare|
|12th|Very Rare|
|17th|Legendary|
[MAGIC ITEM CRAFTING BY LEVEL #css/tx/table]
```

**_Time._** The PC’s progress toward crafting magic items is measured in weeks of downtime, with each degree of rarity taking a gradually higher number of weeks to complete, as shown on the **Weeks by Rarity** table. The GM can increase or decrease the number of weeks required to make a specific item as makes sense for the pace and story of a campaign. Successful resolution checks can dramatically shorten this time. 

```tx
|ITEM RARITY|WEEKS REQUIRED|
|---|---|
|Common|1|
|Uncommon|2|
|Rare|10|
|Very Rare|25|
|Legendary|50|
[WEEKS BY RARITY #css/tx/table]
```

Due to the complex nature of crafting magic items, multiple characters can’t work together to speed up the magic item crafting process. 

**_Cost._** Crafting magic items requires a PC to spend money on the raw materials necessary to make them. To craft a magic item, a PC must find or purchase materials equal to the cost of the magic item’s listed value (see costs for magic items in the _Player’s Guide_). 

**_Resolution._** At the end of each week spent crafting a magic item, a PC rolls an ability check with the ability modifier deemed most appropriate by the GM (use INT as the default) and whichever proficiency allowed the PC to craft the item—the Arcana skill or a relevant tool. The result of this check determines the progress made toward crafting the magic item, as shown on the **Magic Item Crafting Resolution** table. Luck can be spent 1-for-1 to increase the result of this roll, but Luck can’t be used to grant advantage on the roll and no other features can modify it. Once the full time and cost are spent, the PC successfully crafts the magic item.

```tx
|CHECK RESULT|RESOLUTION|
|---|---|
|1–10|Pay a portion of the magic item’s total cost (5%, minimum of 5 gp) to cover wasted materials or lose 1 week of progress toward crafting the item.|
|11–15|Make 1 week of progress toward crafting the item.|
|16–20|Make 2 weeks of progress toward crafting the item.|
|21–24|Make 3 weeks of progress toward crafting the item or a number of weeks of progress equal to your PB (whichever is higher).|
|25+|Halve the number of remaining weeks needed to craft your item (to a minimum of 1 week remaining).|
[MAGIC ITEM CRAFTING RESOLUTION #css/tx/table]
```

>[!rules] QUESTS FOR MAGIC MATERIALS
>If desired, you can increase the difficulty when using downtime to craft magic items (whether permanent or consumable) by requiring that a PC first complete a specific quest or similar narrative task, at the end of which they learn a recipe or acquire a rare component necessary to craft the item. If desired, such rare components might need to be harvested from monsters, using the **Monster Harvesting** variant rules included later in this chapter. 
>
>If you want to use these extra difficulties in your game, communicate your intentions to players as early as possible—ideally, as part of the house rules discussion during a session zero. If that isn’t feasible, do your best to alert the players to your intentions as soon as a player makes you aware of their desire to craft magic items. Being as specific as possible about why you’re introducing these quests and what the PCs have to do to complete them is critical to ensuring your players don’t feel unfairly punished by the additional layer of complexity.
## Potion Brewing
If the GM allows it, PCs can spend downtime creating potions like those found in the _Player’s Guide_. This activity primarily allows you to create magic items specifically from the potion magic item category (which includes salves, oils, and similar), but it can also be used to guide the creation of any consumable magic item not specifically covered by existing rules. Consumable magic items include any item that can be used once (or a few times) before it’s expended for good. The exception to this is the creation of _spell scrolls_, which are covered by the Scroll Making downtime activity later in this chapter. 

Like other forms of crafting, potion making requires access to the raw magical materials needed to make the item. It also requires proficiency with herbalist tools. Depending on the circumstances, a GM can allow a PC to brew potions with a proficiency in alchemist tools instead

Brewing a potion also requires the crafter to infuse a portion of their own power into the item—and a will strong enough to successfully channel this flow of power. For this reason, the greatest rarity of consumable magic item a PC can craft is restricted by the PC’s level, as shown in the **Consumable Crafting by Level** table. A PC can craft magic items of that rarity and all lower rarities. 

```tx
|PC LEVEL|HIGHEST CRAFTABLE RARITY|
|---|---|

|4th|Uncommon|
|7th|Rare|
|12th|Very Rare|
|17th|Legendary|
[CONSUMABLE CRAFTING BY LEVEL #css/tx/table]
```

**_Time._** The PC’s progress toward crafting a potion (or other consumable magic item) is measured in days of downtime for lower rarities and in weeks of downtime for higher rarities, as detailed in the **Potion Brewing Time** table. The GM can increase or decrease the number of days or weeks required to make a specific potion as makes sense for the pace and story of a campaign.

Due to the complex nature of brewing magical potions, multiple characters can’t work together to speed up the potion brewing process. 

**_Cost._** Crafting potions requires a PC to spend money on the raw materials necessary to make them. To craft a potion, a PC must find or purchase materials equal to the cost of the magic item’s listed value (see costs for magic items in the _Player’s Guide_). 

**_Resolution._** Once the full time and cost are spent, the PC successfully crafts the potion. 
## Scroll Making
If the GM allows it, spellcasting PCs can spend downtime creating _spell scrolls_. Also known as scroll crafting or scribing, making spell scrolls puts the power to harness and control magic into an easily usable form. Like other forms of crafting, scroll making requires certain proficiencies and access to raw crafting materials. 

A PC can create a _spell scroll_ of any spell the PC knows or can prepare. If the spell is innately known (not granted by a class feature), as if through a lineage feature, the spell can be scribed only at the lowest possible circle. In addition, if the spell is a cantrip, the resulting scroll is always cast as if the caster was 1st level.

In addition to the spell restrictions, a PC must be proficient in at least one of the following to craft a _spell scroll_: 
- [[Artist Tools]]. 
- [[013. Glossary/Skills/Arcana|Arcana]] skill (can only be used to craft spells on the [[Arcane]] or [[Wyrd]] spell source lists). 
- [[Nature]] skill (can only be used to craft spells on the [[Primordial]] spell source list). 
- [[Religion]] skill (can only be used to craft spells on the [[Divine]] spell source list). 

**_Time._** The PC’s progress toward crafting a _spell scroll_ is measured in days of downtime for lower spell circles and in weeks of downtime for higher spell circles, as detailed in the **Scroll Making Time and Cost** table. The GM can increase or decrease the number of days or weeks required to make a specific _spell scroll_ as makes sense for the pace and story of a campaign. 

```tx

|1st|1d4 days|50 gp|
|2nd|1d6 days|100 gp|
|3rd|2d6 days|200 gp|
|4th|2d12 days|350 gp|
|5th|2d4 weeks|650 gp|
|6th|2d8 weeks|1,500 gp|
|7th|4d8 weeks|2,500 gp|
|8th|6d10 weeks|5,000 gp|
|9th|4d20 weeks|10,500 gp|
```

If a PC is crafting a _spell scroll_ of a spell that can be cast at a higher spell circle, the PC can choose to make the _spell scroll_ of its circle or higher, provided the PC can cast the spell at that higher circle. The PC must still spend the appropriate time and cost for creating a scroll of a higher circle. 

Due to the complex nature of scribing scrolls, multiple characters can’t work together to speed up the scroll‑making process. 

**_Cost._** Scroll making requires a PC to spend money on the raw materials necessary to make them. To craft a _spell scroll_, a PC must find or purchase materials equal to the value listed for the circle of the desired spell, as shown in the **Scroll Making Time and Cost** table. 

**_Resolution._** Once all required time and costs are spent, the PC successfully crafts the _spell scroll_. 

# Dread 

When PCs witness terrors beyond mortal comprehension, they might be inflicted with dread. Dread goes beyond standard fear. It blasts the mind and can scar a creature indefinitely, requiring magical intervention to cure. Heroic characters aren’t psychically harmed by the horrors they face or the carnage they inflict day after day, but sometimes, the stress of adventuring can weigh heavily. If your campaign has a strong horror theme, dread can emphasize the horrific nature of threats. 
## Accruing Dread
Various magical effects, diseases, and supernatural hazards can inflict dread on an otherwise stable mind. Some powerful magic items can also wreck the psyche of creatures who use or become attuned to them. As a GM, you can use dread outside of the specific game mechanics that already utilize them to enhance the danger or horror of a situation, a story arc, or even a campaign.  

Resisting a dread-inducing effect usually requires a WIS or CHA save, but specific effects can detail how to avoid or overcome dread effects. 
## Curing Dread
A _calm emotions_ spell can suppress the effects of dread while a _restoration_ spell can rid a creature of a short-term or long-term dread effect. Depending on the source of the dread, the _remove curse_ spell might also prove effective. A _greater restoration_ spell or more powerful magic is required to rid a character of an indefinite dread effect.  
## Dread Effects
Dread effects can be short-term, long-term, or indefinite. Simple horrors typically impose short-term dread effects, which last for just a few minutes. More horrific encounters or cumulative exposure can result in long-term or indefinite dread effects. 
- A character afflicted with short-term dread is subjected to an effect from the **Short-Term Dread** table for 1d10 minutes. 
- A character afflicted with long-term dread is subjected to an effect from the **Long-Term Dread** table for 1d10 × 10 hours. 
- A character afflicted with indefinite dread gains a roleplaying flaw from the **Indefinite Dread** table that lasts until cured.

# Poisons Expanded

This section contains expanded rules and poison options for use with the basic poison rules found in the _Player’s Guide_. 
## Poison Types
Poisons come in the following four types. 

**_Contact._** Contact poison is smeared on an object and remains potent until it is touched or washed off. A creature that touches contact poison with exposed skin suffers its effects. 

**_Ingested._** A creature must swallow a full dose of ingested poison to suffer its effects. The dose can be delivered in food or a liquid. You might decide that a partial dose has a reduced effect, such as having advantage on the save or dealing half damage on a failed save. 

**_Inhaled._** These poisons are powders or gases that take effect when breathed in. Blowing powder or releasing gas subjects creatures in a 5-foot cube to its effect. The resulting cloud dissipates immediately afterward. Holding one’s breath is ineffective against inhaled poisons, as they affect nasal membranes, tear ducts, and other parts of the body.

**_Injury._** Injury poison can be applied to weapons, ammunition, traps, and objects that deal piercing or slashing damage. Once applied to a weapon or piece of ammunition, the poison remains potent for 1 minute, or until delivered through a wound or washed off. A creature that takes piercing or slashing damage from an object coated with the poison is exposed to its effects.
## Acquiring Poison
In some settings, laws prohibit the possession and use of poison, but an illicit dealer or unscrupulous apothecary might keep a hidden stash. Characters with criminal contacts might be able to acquire poison easily. Other characters might have to make extensive inquiries and pay bribes before they acquire the poison they seek.

Besides crafting, another option is to harvest it from a monster, see [[#Monster Harvesting]] rules for more details.
## Poison Descriptions
Each type of poison has its own debilitating effects.

**_Assassin’s Blood (Ingested)._** A creature subjected to this poison must make a DC 10 CON save. On a failed save, it takes 6 (1d12) poison damage and is poisoned for 24 hours. On a successful save, the creature takes half damage and isn’t poisoned. 

**_Burnt Othur Fumes (Inhaled)._** A creature subjected to this poison must succeed on a DC 13 CON save or take 10 (3d6) poison damage. Regardless of the result, it must repeat the save at the start of each of its turns. On each failed save, the creature takes 3 (1d6) poison damage. After three successful saves, the effect ends. 

**_Drow Poison (Injury)._** This poison is typically made only by the drow, and only in a place far removed from sunlight. A creature subjected to this poison must succeed on a DC 13 CON save or be poisoned for 1 hour. If the save fails by 5 or more, the creature is also unconscious while poisoned in this way. The creature wakes up if it takes damage or if another creature takes an action to shake it awake. 

**_Malice (Inhaled)._** A creature subjected to this poison must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or be poisoned for 1 hour. While poisoned in this way, the creature is also blinded. 

**_Oil of Taggit (Contact)._** A creature subjected to this poison must succeed on a DC 13 CON save or be poisoned for 24 hours. While poisoned in this way, the creature is also unconscious, but it wakes up if it takes damage.

**_Pale Tincture (Ingested)._** A creature subjected to this poison must succeed on a DC 16 CON save or take 3 (1d6) poison damage and be poisoned. The poisoned creature must repeat the save every 24 hours, taking 3 (1d6) poison damage on a failed save. Until this poison ends, the damage the poison deals can’t be healed by any means. # Rewards
![[treasure.webp]]
Ensuring PCs are rewarded for their valiant acts of heroism is one of the GM’s most important jobs. At minimum, the PCs should receive rewards at the end of each adventure or story arc. However, making handouts of experience, treasure, and other rewards something you do at the end of every game is always a great way to wind down your sessions—and give your players something to look forward to using next time! 
## Experience
Handing out experience points (or XP) is arguably the most important reward of all. 

If you are using the standard Tracking XP method to level (described in the _Player’s Guide_), you should calculate and hand out XP at the end of every game session. Rewarding XP at the end allows you to pay special attention to rewarding extra XP for fun or thrilling moments that happened in the session, in addition to the XP the PCs might have received from defeating any monsters they encountered.

If you are using the Tracking Milestones method (described in the _Player’s Guide_), you likely won’t be handing out XP every session—only when you determine the PCs have made significant progress in the story or have accomplished a major goal or objective. Alternatively, if it has been several sessions since the PCs gained a level, and your players start asking when to expect the next level—it’s probably time to grant them one! 

## Downtime
Rewarding periods of downtime is an excellent way to signal the end of a major adventure or story arc. Downtime is essentially free time for the PCs that allows them to take a breather and pursue more mundane interests between the all-action-all-the-time pacing of most gameplay. Since most downtime activities require at least one full week of downtime to make progress, handing out downtime rewards of one or more weeks is recommended, as opposed to handing out single days. For more information on how downtime works, see the _Player’s Guide_. 
## Luck
Luck is a unique fail-forward resource that the PCs can generate on their own and isn’t strictly necessary for you to hand out. However, rewarding Luck is an easy and immediate way to encourage great roleplaying or other player participation. Giving every PC 1 Luck immediately after surviving a difficult encounter is a special way to reward them during gameplay. When rewarding PCs with Luck, make sure to keep an eye out for any PCs who tend to sit at the maximum amount, since awarding Luck to a PC at their maximum can cause them to lose Luck when they have to reset their total! For more information on how Luck works, see the _Player’s Guide_.

#### Magic Items
The magic items in the _Player’s Guide_ are available as treasure rewards for PCs. The rarity of each magic item helps you determine what kind of items make suitable rewards for PCs of each level. For example, PCs of 1st–4th level should primarily receive magic items of common or uncommon rarity while 5th–10th level PCs should receive primarily rare items, and so on.  
### Awarding Treasure by Tier
It is important that your treasure awards line up with the level of your PCs, otherwise you risk making the party too powerful for their current tier of play. The guidance in this section assumes a standard party of 4 PCs, so scale up or down accordingly.   

>[!rules] CONSUMABLE MAGIC ITEMS
>Potions and other consumables are factored differently than permanent or enchanted magic items in treasure awards. Because of their limited-use nature and lower worth, potions are found more frequently than other magic items. As a general rule, expect to hand out three to five potions per PC per level, with most of these being _potions of healing_ of the rarity that best suits the level of your PCs. With any more than that, a PC’s power will eclipse any relative challenge. Any less and your PCs might be more inclined to hoard their consumables rather than use them, saving them for a rainy day or the most imposing of confrontations with their adversaries.
#### Tier 1, Levels 1-3
![[reward-magic-items.webp|right|384]]In tier 1, treasure rewards should primarily be monetary, and most magic items should be consumable. It’s often best to wait until 3rd or 4th level to hand out permanent magic items, as these can radically increase a PC’s power at lower levels. 

**_Magic Items._** By the end of 4th level, the party should find about 10 permanent common magic items and 2 uncommon magic items in this level range (3 magic items per PC). 

**_Consumables._** Consumable magic items play a large part in shoring up the fragility of PCs at this level. It’s important to make consumables findable and useable throughout these levels to ensure PCs learn to freely spend consumables without fear of them becoming too scarce to actually use. 

**_Monetary Treasure._** At this tier, copper pieces are most common. Supplement rewards of coin with common gemstones and art objects, punctuated with uncommon. 
#### Tier 2, Levels 5-10
In tier 2, monetary rewards are still important, but more magic items (both consumable and permanent) should regularly feature in treasure rewards. Note that handing out magic weapons is especially important, since many monsters faced by PCs at this tier are resistant or immune to nonmagical damage. 

**_Magic Items._** By the end of 10th level, your PCs should have received a combined total of 30 common and uncommon and 6 rare magic items over the course of the campaign (9 per PC). Expect them to have sold off or traded away most magic items of more common rarities in favor of more potent items. 

**_Consumables._** _Potions of healing_ should still frequently appear in their greater varieties; basic healing potions are largely obsolete at this tier. Rare consumables, such as high-circle _spell scrolls_, become prevalent. Very rare consumables start to appear. 

**_Monetary Treasure._** At this tier, coins should primarily be silver and gold pieces, copper pieces are phased out, and platinum pieces begin to appear. The quality of gemstones and art objects increases to uncommon punctuated with rare. 
#### Tier 3, Levels 11-16
In tier 3, monetary rewards lessen in importance, and magic items should be the focus of treasure rewards. 

**_Magic Items._** By the end of 16th level, the PCs should find around 66 magic items over the course of the campaign, with about 14 of those being at least rare or very rare (about 15 magic items per PC). Again, the actual number of magic items carried will fluctuate due to items traded or sold. It is especially important to remember that many rare or very rare items require attunement, which means PCs have to be selective of what they equip with their limited attunement slots. Take care not to give out magic items that all require attunement to ensure your PCs don’t constantly have to choose what they want to equip or sell off. 

**_Consumables._** Due to the ease of magical healing, _potions of healing_ (even more potent versions) are less important and should be replaced with more unique potions and high-circle _spell scrolls_. 

**_Monetary Treasure._** Rewards of coin should be rare in favor of more interesting treasures, but any coins handed out should exclusively be gold and platinum pieces. Legendary-rarity gems and art objects become available, and they often make more interesting rewards than lump sums of coin. 
#### Tier 4, Levels 17-20
In tier 4, magic items should be the focus of treasure rewards—and hunting for individual magic items of great power make excellent quests for PCs of this tier. 

**_Magic Items._** By the end of 20th level, the PCs should find a combined total of about 80 magic items over the course of the campaign, with about 20 of those being rare or higher in rarity (about 20 per PC). 

**_Consumables._** At this point, PCs can likely accomplish anything consumable magic items of rare rarity or less with their own spells or class features. Any consumables handed out at this tier should be very rare at minimum. 

**_Monetary Treasure._** Gold, platinum, gemstones, and art objects reach an all-time high in rarity and worth. At this stage of play, PCs likely have more money than they could ever need, and monetary rewards should be largely phased out of treasure rewards. 
### Awarding Treasure by Bundles
![[awarding-treasure-by-bundles.webp|right|384]]Utilizing treasure bundles is an easy way to determine how much treasure you should award to a party over the course of certain levels. Each treasure bundle represents the total amount of treasure a standard party of 4 PCs should receive over the course of the listed level range. 

To use a treasure bundle, first determine which level your PCs are, how many there are, and what level you expect them to be at the end of the adventure. Pick the treasure bundle table that corresponds with that PC level range. Keep in mind that these bundles assume a standard party of 4 PCs. If you have fewer or more, use the instructions in the Adjusting Treasure by Party Size sidebar to modify the number of rewards in your chosen bundle. 

Once you have picked the appropriate treasure bundle, you can use it to pre-plan all the treasure you should give out during those levels. Make all the rolls the bundle requires in advance to determine what magic items, consumables, and monetary rewards you should have ready for those levels of play. Then, when you are playing the game, keep your treasure bundle list of predetermined rewards on hand to sprinkle the predetermined treasures throughout the sessions of that story arc.

>[!rules] ADJUSTING TREASURE BY PARTY SIZE
>These treasure bundles assume you have a standard‑sized party of four PCs. If you are playing with more PCs, double one die roll for consumables and add 1 to a non-rolled line item in the treasure bundle for every PC past four. For example, if you are preparing a treasure bundle for a group of five 1st–3rd level PCs, you would make 4 rolls instead of 3 on the Common Magic Item table and roll 2d4 on the Consumable Magic Items table rather than just rolling 1d4. 
>
>If you are playing with fewer than four PCs, determine the entire treasure bundle as normal—since a smaller party may need more magic items to take on the same challenges as a full party of 4. Sometimes an additional _potion of healing_ makes all the difference between success or failure for smaller parties.
### Awarding Treasure by Monster
*Excerpt from "**D&D** Dungeon Master's Guide" book by **D&D Beyond**.*
#### Individual Treasure
Characters might find small amounts of treasure in the pockets, pouches, or personal stashes of individual monsters. Even if a monster doesn’t intentionally collect treasure, characters might find scattered coins and other monetary treasure left behind by the monster’s previous victims.

You can use the **Random Individual Treasure** table to determine how much treasure a single monster has based on its Challenge Rating (CR). The table includes the average total in parentheses, which you can use instead of rolling. To determine the total amount of treasure for a group of similar creatures, you can roll once and multiply the total by the number of creatures in the group.

```tx
|CR|Treasure|Roll|
|---|---|---|
|0–4|3d6 (10) GP|`dice: 3d6` GP|
|5–10|2d8 × 10 (90) GP|`dice: 2d8 * 10` GP|
|11–16|2d10 × 10 (110) PP|`dice: 2d10 * 10` PP|
|17+|2d8 × 100 (900) PP|`dice: 2d8 * 100` PP|
[Random Individual Treasure #css/tx/table]
```
#### Treasure Hoards
Adventurers sometimes discover large caches of treasure, the accumulated wealth of a large group of creatures or the belongings of a single powerful creature that hoards valuables. The **Random Treasure Hoard** table can help you create such a cache. When determining the contents of a hoard belonging to one monster, use the row for that monster’s Challenge Rating (CR). When the hoard belongs to a large group of monsters, use the CR of the monster that leads the group. Each row includes average results for monetary treasure, which you can use instead of rolling. To create a hoard for a monster that is particularly fond of amassing treasure (such as a dragon), you can roll twice on the table or roll once and double the total.

As a rough benchmark, aim to roll on the **Random Treasure Hoard** table about once per game session. Use other guidelines to determine which magic items are in the hoard.

```tx
|CR|Monetary Treasure|Magic Items|Roll|
|---|---|---|---|
|0–4|2d4 × 100 (500) GP|1d4 − 1|`dice: 2d4 * 100` GP and `dice: 1d4 - 1` M.I.|
|5–10|8d10 × 100 (4,400) GP|1d3|`dice: 8d10 * 100` GP and `dice: 1d3` M.I.|
|11–16|8d8 × 10,000 (36,000) GP|1d4|`dice: 8d8 * 10000` GP and `dice: 1d4` M.I.|
|17+|6d10 × 10,000 (330,000) GP|1d6|`dice: 6d10 * 10000` GP and `dice: 1d4 - 1` M.I.|
```

>[!rules] WHERE ARE THE FABLED MAGIC ITEMS?
>One of the characteristics of fabled magic items is that they grow with the PCs, improving in rarity over the course of the campaign. It is for this reason that they don’t neatly fit into a series of random magic tables that scale in rarity. Another major characteristic of fabled magic items is that each has its own story inherent to it. For these two reasons, it should be entirely in the GM’s authority whether a fabled item should be included in a campaign’s treasure bundle. 
>
>If you wish to leave it to chance, after you roll for a treasure bundle, you might roll an additional 1d100. On a result of 5 or less, replace one of the magic items you initially determined with a fabled magic item of the same type.
