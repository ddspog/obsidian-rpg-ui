Buying and selling in _Tales of the Valiant_ can take place in a variety of locations, each with its own unique catalog and specialties. Instead of only referencing the _Player's Guide_ [[05. Equipment & Magic Items#Equipment|Equipment]] section, you can use these tables to build shops that reflect their owners, what they sell, and what they are willing to buy or trade.
# Building Your Shop

When the players encounter a shop—whether they seek it out intentionally or stumble upon it along the way—use these concepts to craft the shop's catalog and to role-play the shopkeeper or employees.
## Shop Availability
### Limited Stock
These are shops that don't produce goods on demand, such as a bazaar stall or a traveling cart. Imagine a shop whose entire stock could potentially be bought out by the party, provided they have the means to do so.
### Rural Locale
This type of shop is located in rural areas, farms, or small villages. Their inventory depends on locally available resources and trade routes. Expect a modest selection focused on practicality.
### Urban Locale
An urban shop is located in a town or city, where commerce thrives and a wide range of products is available for restock. These shops tend to have more variety than rural shops, with goods arriving from multiple sources.
### Premium Locale
These shops are the best of the best: they are well-established, often famous, and known for high-quality goods or exclusive merchandise. The owner is successful and may offer rare or high-quality items.
### Faction Only
Some shops or specific items are available only to customers who belong to the same faction as the shop owner. Use this designation to mark restricted goods or services that require faction loyalty.
### Exotic
An adventurer may seek unique and rare items, and as a vendor, offer products sourced from all corners of the world. Exotic items are often specific to certain settings or regions, making them difficult to find or even identify. Shopkeepers may face challenges in understanding or valuing these exotic goods due to their rarity and unfamiliarity.
## Selling to Players
The prices that shopkeepers charge can be influenced by several factors, including the players' relationship with the shopkeeper or broader social circumstances. The owner might accept haggling if they are fond of the players or if the party has done something to earn their favor. Alternatively, prices could be higher due to difficult circumstances like war or scarcity.
### Normal Prices
If no special factors are affecting the shopkeeper, they will charge the standard prices as listed in the _Player's Guide_ [[05. Equipment & Magic Items#Equipment|Equipment]] section.
### Cheap Prices
A discount of around 25% may be offered if the shopkeeper is in a good mood, the players have done a favor, or the shop has extra stock and can afford it. Players might also barter by offering something valuable to the shopkeeper.
### Expensive Prices
Prices can be up to 50% higher if the shop is particularly fancy, located in a wealthy district, or specializes in rare or high-quality items. The shop could also be dealing with economic hardship or monopolizing its market.
### Haggling
Establish whether a shop generally uses cheap, normal, or expensive pricing. When haggling, players may be able to adjust the price of specific items—either lowering it through successful negotiation or, in some cases, raising it if they offend the shopkeeper.
## Buying from Players
Players might want to sell loot, trade items, or exchange goods for something the shopkeeper has. Whether the shop accepts loot or trades is at the GM's discretion and should depend on whether the goods are valuable to the shop.
### Selling Cap
Each shop has a daily limit on how much they can spend on buying items from players. The shopkeeper might have limited funds or need to reserve money for other expenses. This cap can change based on whether the shop is using cheap or expensive prices.
### Hard Sell
There is a point, typically around 50% of the Selling Cap, where the shopkeeper will initially refuse to buy more. Players will need to convince the shopkeeper through skill checks, quests, or other actions.
### Trade Goods
Some shops may be willing to buy trade goods such as grains, lumber, or other bulk items in addition to typical adventuring loot. Consider whether these goods align with the shop's needs or specialties.

### Gemstone Appraisal
This service is included to assist in ruling on appraisals. If the players fail their check, the jeweler might be the next best option for determining the value.
### Jewelry and Set Gems
Jewelry with stones already set (including rings, amulets, earrings, etc.) is available based on gemstone availability, at the GM's discretion. Refer to [[07. Adventuring Options#Gems|Gems]] for suggestions on gemstone values and descriptions. The "Set Gem" service allows you to enhance the value of jewelry by adding or replacing gems.

You may also need to resize the jewelry to fit specific needs or adapt it for use by other creatures. For example, resizing a ring originally made for a giant to fit a smaller character.
### Powdering
The "Powdering" service is often necessary, as most places do not keep powdered gemstones on hand. Larger locations might have a few of the less valuable gems already powdered and bagged, but generally, this service is provided on an as-needed basis. **Typically, the customer must provide the gem in addition to paying the listed fee.**
### Size
Gem Shop prices are determined by a system that correlates the size of a gem to its price. Gems are initially sold at full size, which corresponds to the default prices listed in the [[07. Adventuring Options#Gems|Gems]] section. The price can be adjusted based on the size of the gem:
- Large: Full price (1:1)
- Medium: 1/10 of the full price (1:10)
- Small: 1/100 of the full price (1:100)
- Tiny: 1/1000 of the full price (1:1000)

For example, when casting the spell **Chromatic Orb** (1st level, Evocation), it requires a diamond worth at least 50 gp. If a Large diamond is valued at 5000 gp (1:1 ratio), then dividing that by 100 gives you a Small diamond worth 50 gp, which is sufficient for the spell requirement.
