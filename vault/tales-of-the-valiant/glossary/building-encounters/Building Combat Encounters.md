## Building Combat Encounters
If you are running a pre-written adventure, combat encounters have likely already been designed for you— with groups of enemies already put together in a way appropriate for the level of the adventure and the events of the story. However, when creating your own adventures or spicing up published material, you may need to create your own combat encounters.
### Where to Start?
There are three primary ways to start building a combat encounter. First, you can choose monsters that make sense based on the story and locations present in your game. PCs exploring an old tomb are likely to run into [[Undead]] like skeletons, zombies, or ghouls. PCs hanging around the seedy part of a city might run into [[Humanoid]] threats in the form of bandits, cultists, or spies. You can often improvise such combat encounters while running the game based on the choices of the PCs and the direction the story goes. 

![[bronze-dragon-wyrmling.webp|right|384]]Another approach is to build combat encounters around the lore of a particular monster. Maybe you think it’d be cool if the PCs discovered a manticore nest or the lair of a medusa. Maybe you build a whole adventure around the swamps inhabited by a young black dragon. Reading through the monster entries in this book can give you your own ideas for adventures and encounters based on the details that you find most interesting. 

A third approach is to think about the type of combat encounter you want to run. What kind of battle fits the situation, the pacing of the game, and is likely to excite your players? Will they enjoy facing two big brutes, a band of equally matched combatants, or a dozen smaller minions? When taking this approach, use the tables later in this section to choose the type of combat encounter and the appropriate monster difficulty. Then use the [[Appendix, Creatures by Terrain|Creatures by Terrain]] tables in the Tables Appendix later in this book to see which monsters fit the environment and the encounter difficulty you’re seeking.

### Choosing the number of Monsters
Once you have an idea which monsters you want to use in your combat encounter, you must decide how many of them to include in the encounter. If you’re building your encounter based on the story and situation, ask yourself how many monsters make sense given this situation. Some monsters are typically found alone. Others are found in small or large groups. Some monsters work in pairs or surround themselves with lieutenants or lackies (see [[Monster Roles and Tactics]] later in this chapter). Before worrying about the difficulty of a potential battle, ask how many monsters fit the situation in the game’s fiction. 

If you’re designing an encounter to challenge the characters or around a particular combat encounter model (see [[Combat Encounter Models]] later in this section), you can use the tables later in this chapter to 1) determine the threshold of a challenging encounter, 2) choose the number of monsters to fit that threshold, and 3) modify the number based on what you know of the capabilities of the PCs.
