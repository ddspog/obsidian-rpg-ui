## Combat Encounter Models  
![[copper-dragon-wyrmling.webp|right|384]] Combat encounters can take many forms in your game, but playing with different combinations of foes is key to creating a campaign full of engaging fights. When you create a new combat encounter, choose one of the following models to mix things up.  
### Single Big Monster
A group of heroes defying death in the face of a colossal monster is a classic fantasy trope that is easy to replicate in a combat encounter. However, a standard single monster is always at a disadvantage when facing an entire group of PCs (see [[Determining Encounter Difficulty|Understanding Action Economy]]). A solo monster might be a solid threat when facing four or five PCs if its CR is either equal to the average level of the PCs for 1st to 4th level PCs or up to five higher than the average level of the PCs for PCs of 5th level or above. When the PCs reach 5th level, they can often neutralize a single creature with spells or class features—especially if the monster makes an unlucky roll or two. If you’re looking to challenge PCs of 5th level or above, include more than a single standard monster, or make use of especially tough legendary monsters. 
```rpg rule.side
title: Run easy Encounters
type: rules
---
It’s tempting to fall into the trap of running only challenging combat encounters. Instead, mix in easier encounters against a few weak foes. It gives the PCs a chance to shine, easily taking down foes they once might have feared. Likewise, facing a weak foe opens up entertaining opportunities for roleplaying or exploration. Shifting between easy and hard combat encounters keeps your players excited and energized to see what’s next, rather than wrung out by too many nailbiters.
```
Legendary monsters—as in, creatures with legendary actions (see [[02. Understanding Monster Statistics#Legendary Actions|Legendary Actions]])—are designed to face a whole group of PCs even when their average level is 5th or above. If you’re looking to challenge a group of PCs at 5th level or above with a single monster, consider using a legendary monster with a CR equal to the average PC level + 5. 
### Boss and Lieutenants
An effective variation of the big boss monster encounter is to have the PCs square off against a boss accompanied by a few lieutenant monsters whose capabilities complement the boss. As an example, a mage could be paired with several veteran bodyguards, or a young red dragon could be backed up by several kobolds. 

This boss and lieutenant model works well when a boss by themselves could otherwise be neutralized with a single spell or ability. Even in this situation, however, expect players to gang up on the boss first. For this model, the boss may have a CR of roughly half of the total challenging benchmark while the total CRs of the lieutenants equal the other half. 
### Double Trouble
One way to run a big monster without worrying about it getting pinned down and killed too easily is to use two big monsters. Two monsters of the same type keep the PCs’ attention split, forcing them to protect their vulnerable allies. For this model, the two big monsters might each have a CR of roughly half the total CRs of the challenging encounter benchmark. If you have six PCs instead of four or five, consider adding a third big monster to the mix, each with a CR of 1/3 the total CRs of the challenging encounter benchmark. 

### Matched set of Foes
In this model, the PCs face a group of monsters roughly equivalent in number and power to themselves—such as five lower-level PCs facing five gnolls. Battles against a single type of foe like this are easy to run and provide a good challenge for the PCs. For a challenging battle, such monsters should have a CR of roughly 1/4th the level of the PCs if they’re 4th level or below, 1/2 of the level of the PCs if they’re 5th to 10th level, and 3/4 the average PC level if they’re 11th level or above. 
### Horde of Minions
Nothing’s more fun than blasting down a horde of weaker monsters. Though each monster in the horde of minions model poses very little threat to the PCs, large numbers of them look scary when crawling over a bone-riddled hill. Such hordes can be deadly to PCs below 5th level but at 5th level and above, the PCs often have spells and abilities to deal with large groups of monsters. A horde can be challenging to PCs of 5th level and above when each member of the horde has a challenge rating 1/10 the average level of the PCs, assuming five monsters per PC. 
### Waves of Monsters
One very effective way to run memorable and challenging fights is to run waves of monsters instead of putting all the monsters out at once. Mix and match the encounter models above for each wave of a multi-wave fight. For example, a group of five 6th level PCs battling in the throne room of an underground crypt might face a horde of twenty skeletons first. Once most of the skeletons are dead, four ogre zombies bash through the walls. About halfway through this fight, the crypt’s undead mage arrives, floating up from the bones atop the throne. Such waves of combatants push PCs to their limits without overwhelming them all at once.
