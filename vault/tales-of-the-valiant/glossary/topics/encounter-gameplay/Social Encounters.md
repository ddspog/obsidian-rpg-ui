## Social Encounters
```rpg rule.content
id: summary
---
Social encounters involve interacting with creatures in ways other than direct combat, usually with a specific objective. Convincing an official to help your cause, improvising a captivating performance, and interviewing witnesses are all social encounters. This is a brief, player-facing discussion of the kinds of things you might face in an exploration encounter. 
```
### Settlements
```rpg rule.content
id: settlements
---
Fantasy worlds often have pockets of safety and civilization in the untamed lands ruled by monsters and magic. During their adventures, PCs might well visit towns, cities, or other settlements to rest and restock between quests. Since settlements mean lots of people, they are great places for social encounters and downtime (see [[06. Playing the Game#Downtime Activities|Downtime Activities]]).  
```
### Attitude
```rpg rule.content
id: attitude
---
Friendly, hostile, and neutral are the three categories used to describe a creature’s attitude toward PCs. These terms appear in class features, spells, and conditions to describe who can be affected by certain effects and how they behave. 

A [[Social Encounters#attitude|friendly]] NPC is willing to actively help PCs and generally wants to see the PCs survive and flourish. A [[Social Encounters#attitude|hostile]] NPC is unwilling to help PCs and generally wants to see the PCs fail. A [[Social Encounters#attitude|neutral]] NPC has no strong feelings towards the PCs (positive or negative) and treats them as is most beneficial or logical for the NPC in the moment. 

By default, most NPCs are neutral toward the party. PCs who routinely have positive interactions with a neutral NPC can shift their attitude to friendly. PCs who harm, threaten, or mistreat NPCs can shift their attitude to hostile. It’s easy to force a creature’s attitude toward hostile and more difficult to shift an attitude to friendly.
```
