## Modifying Monsters
![[void-dragon-wyrmling.webp|right|384]] This book contains monsters suitable for all manner of adventures and encounters, but it can’t account for every possible scenario. When you can’t find the perfect monster for your encounter, the easiest option is to find a comparable monster in this book, then use one of the following strategies to customize that monster to fit.
### Customizing Abilities and Traits
You can often get a great customization by simply adjusting the statistics or traits. Each of these approaches is described below. 
#### Altering Statistics
When you need a monster that doesn’t exist, start by finding a comparable monster stat block in this book, then make a minor adjustment appropriate to the encounter. A lot can be accomplished simply by altering how a creature moves, senses its surroundings, survives, and so on. 

Need a suitable mount for PCs exploring a volcanic dungeon? Take the giant lizard stat block and give it fire resistance. Need a kingdom of bird people? Take any [[Humanoid]] stat block and add a flying speed. Need a giant earthworm? Take the giant constrictor snake stat block and replace its swimming speed with a burrowing speed.  

You can easily change any of the statistics in this list to alter an existing stat block: 
- **Speed.** Adding a [[burrowing]], [[flying]], or [[swimming]] speed can make a monster compatible with almost any environment. 
- **Resistant/Immune/Vulnerable.** Altering any of these condition or damage interactions can instantly shift where the monster lives or how it survives. 
- **Senses.** Adding [[darkvision]] or [[tremorsense]] makes any creature better suited to underground or underwater areas. 
- **Languages.** Switching known languages allows a monster to fit into any culture that fits your game. Giving a language to a creature that typically doesn’t speak a language or giving it telepathy can add a quick magical twist to a monster. 

Most of these kinds of changes to a monster’s stat block don’t alter its challenge. However, adding or removing conditions or damage types to which a monster is resistant, immune, or vulnerable can make a monster more or less powerful, depending on the encounter and the environment. For example, making a monster vulnerable to an environmental effect that is also affecting the PCs can add an extra layer of tactics for the PCs, who might use the environment against the monster, but this also reduces how challenging the monster is for them. 
#### Altering Traits
Oftentimes, adjusting a monster to suit a specific encounter can be solved by altering its existing traits. Traits are passive features presented in monster stat blocks after ability modifiers and before actions (see **Elements of a Stat Block** in Chapter 2).  

You can always design a trait from scratch, but borrowing an existing trait from a different monster often serves just as well. Most traits have little to no impact on a creature’s CR but use your best judgment when adding traits. Adding too many traits can make a monster difficult to run in combat. A rare few traits (like Blood Frenzy or Magic Resistance) can significantly alter a monster’s power level, especially in ideal environments (for instance, if a monster with Pack Tactics has a multitude of allies, it ensures almost every [[Attack|attack]] has advantage). In general, for every 5–8 damage a trait adds to the damage a monster can deal each round, increase the monster’s CR by 1. 

The **Sample Traits** table lists useful traits that can quickly alter a monster’s capabilities. You can modify their wording to make sense for the monster you are customizing. For example, if you give a monster the False Appearance trait, you should specify what object that creature looks like when motionless, stating it appears like a statue, area rug, stalagmite, or other relevant example. Information that appears in [brackets] indicates what numbers on the monster’s stat block you should use to calculate the trait’s DC. For any trait requiring a monster’s PB, see **Proficiency Bonus** in Chapter 2.

### Adjusting CR
If you find the perfect monster to fit the adventure, but need to adjust it to be more or less powerful to fit the level of your PCs, you can adjust its statistics and CR. In general, for each CR you want to adjust a creature, either increase or reduce its HP by 10–15, the damage it deals each round by 5–8, and its AC by 1–2. For example, if you want to reduce the CR 5 bulette to a CR 3, reduce its HP to 78, each Bite attack to 12 piercing damage, and its AC to 15. 

Adjusting statistics in this way doesn’t truly reflect all the nuances that go into calculating CR, but it is a reliable quick fix for most scenarios that require a CR adjustment on the fly. Keep in mind that the bigger the gap between the current and desired CRs, the less accurate this adjustment is going to be. Higher CR creatures have access to more actions, higher circle spells, and other features that aren’t easily reflected with simple mathematical adjustments. As a general rule, use this method for adjusting a CR no more than 5 steps above or below the creature’s standard CR. If the CR gap is bigger than 5, finding an entirely different creature is your best option.
