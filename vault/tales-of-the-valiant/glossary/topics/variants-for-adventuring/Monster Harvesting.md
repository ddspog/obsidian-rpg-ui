## Monster Harvesting
`@[[monster-harvesting.webp]].banner(hero)`
When PCs take down a powerful monster, they occasionally want to know what trophies they can keep from it. These trophies might be purely decorative, like attaching feathers to a weapon or adorning a shield with a horn, but sometimes they can be more. This section provides rules for giving PCs that “something more” as treasure when they fell a creature.  
### Harvesting materials from Creatures
To harvest usable materials from a dead creature, a PC must be proficient in the Survival skill or have proficiency with trapper tools. In certain circumstances, a GM might allow PCs proficient in the Arcana skill to attempt to harvest materials from creatures with uniquely non-corporeal or magical forms. Similarly, a GM might allow PCs proficient with tinker tools to attempt to harvest materials from Constructs or creatures with similarly machine-like forms. 

PCs with a qualifying proficiency can attempt to harvest material from a slain creature by succeeding on a WIS (Survival), WIS (Trapper Tools), or other WIS ability check using the qualifying proficiency. The DC to successfully harvest from a creature is based on its CR, as detailed in the **Harvesting DCs by CR** table.
 
```rpg table.harvesting-dcs-by-cr
|CREATURE CR|HARVESTING DC|
|---|---|
|0–4|12|
|5–8|13|
|9–12|14|
|13–16|15|
|17–20|16|
|21–24|17|
|25–28|18|
|29+|19|
[HARVESTING DCS BY CR]
```

Harvesting prime material is a time-consuming process best done outside the confines of dungeons or similarly perilous locales. When time is a factor, assume collecting 1 Harvest takes approximately 1 hour—meaning a Tiny creature could be completely harvested over the course of 30 minutes, while it would take a single creature up to 8 hours to fully harvest a Gargantuan creature. 

**_Result._** Success on a check means the PC gains 1 Harvest from one material category of the PC’s choice (see  **Categories of Harvested Materials** later in this section) and the creature’s total available Harvest is reduced by 1. If a PC fails this check, they gain no Harvest during the attempt and the creature’s total available Harvest is reduced by 1. 

**_Total Harvest._** A creature can produce a number of Harvest based on its size, as detailed in the **Maximum Harvest by Creature Size** table. This is the total Harvest a creature can produce, regardless of which material categories are harvested. Tiny creatures produce less than 1 Harvest, and multiples of those creatures must be harvested to gather enough material to equal 1 Harvest. 
  
```rpg table.maximum-harvest-by-creature-size
|CREATURE SIZE|MAX HARVEST|
|---|---|
|Tiny|1/2|
|Small|1|
|Medium|2|
|Large|4|
|Huge|6|
|Gargantuan|8|
[MAXIMUM HARVEST BY CREATURE SIZE]
```

**_Skilled Harvesting._** If a PC has proficiency in both the Survival skill and trapper tools and exceeds a harvesting DC by 5 or more, the PC gains an extra 1 Harvest of a category of the PC’s choice from the creature, even if the creature normally can’t produce that much Harvest. This extra Harvest can be gained only once from a particular creature—regardless of the number of successful harvesting attempts made. 
### Using harvested materials
Harvested material can be used to craft an item, or it can be used as a material component when casting spells. Harvested material decays if it goes unused for several days. 
#### Crafting
![[using-harvested-materials.webp|right|384]]When relevant, you can use harvested materials to reduce the cost of making nonmagical items with the Crafting downtime activity (described in the _Player’s Guide_). You can use harvested material in this way only when the material is clearly relevant to the item you are attempting to craft. For example, it is reasonable that most materials from the Hide harvest category could be used to craft different kinds of armor. When you use harvested material to craft a nonmagical item featuring that material, the cost to craft the item is reduced by 25 percent for each Harvest expended. For example, if you use the Crafting downtime activity to make a suit of plate armor, you would typically need to pay 750 gp to cover the cost of the raw materials. However, if you craft the same suit of plate armor using 1 Harvest of dragon hide, you need to pay only 563 gp in raw materials. Harvested material can never reduce the cost to craft an item below 25 percent of the original cost to craft the item. To continue the former example, you must expend raw materials worth at least 187 gp—25 percent of the normal 750 gp—to craft a suit of plate armor, even if you expend 4 Harvest of dragon hide. 

```rpg rule.side
title: Harvesting Humanoids
type: rules
---
Unless expressly ruled otherwise by the GM, harvesting materials from [[Humanoid]] creatures is an expressly evil act that is rejected by all societies and cultures regardless of campaign setting. NPCs who witness (or suspect) a PC attempting such a harvest become immediately hostile toward that PC. In addition, materials harvested from Humanoids can’t be sold, purchased, or used to craft items because their use is universally rejected. 

If your table chooses to use these harvesting rules, consider setting aside time to establish whether any other humanoid-adjacent creatures in your setting are or aren’t acceptable to use with these rules. For example, you might decide that the rule against harvesting Humanoids also applies to non-Humanoids with partially human forms—like centaurs and satyrs. Similarly, some cultures in your setting might view certain creatures, like hippogriffs or pegasi, as sacred and see the harvesting of such creatures as a despicable act worth hostility or exile from their regions.
```
Note that using Harvest materials in this way doesn’t decrease the time needed to craft a particular item; you still need to expend the required time calculated with the item’s standard market value. 
#### Crafting Potions 
When relevant, you can use harvested materials to reduce the cost of making consumable magic items made with the Potion Making downtime activity (see the **Downtime Expanded** section of this chapter). You can use harvested material in this way only when the material is clearly relevant to the item you are attempting to craft. For example, it is reasonable that most materials from the Fluid harvest category could be used to brew fluid-based items like potions. 

When you use Harvest materials to craft a consumable magic item, the cost to craft the item is reduced by 25 percent. You can reduce the cost of crafting a consumable magic item in this way only once—using additional Harvest materials beyond the first has no impact on crafting cost. 

Note that using Harvest materials in this way doesn’t decrease the time needed to craft a particular item; you still need to expend the required time calculated with the item’s standard market value.  
#### Spell Components 
```rpg rule.side
type: rules
title: "Optional Rule: Decaying Harvest"
---
In most campaigns, you don’t need to spend time tracking when and if harvested materials decay. You can reasonably say that PCs skilled enough to regularly harvest creatures can properly store and preserve harvested materials as needed. However, if your campaign heavily features survival or otherwise features game elements about resource scarcity and management, you can utilize the following additional decay rule. 

**_Decaying Harvest._** Unless preserved (such as with the _gentle repose_ spell) or incorporated into a crafted item or the start of a crafted item, harvested materials decay, evaporate, or otherwise become unusable after 7 days. At the GM’s discretion, particularly rare or unique harvested materials might become unusable sooner or might last longer.
```
Some harvested materials can be used as substitutes for material spell components with a listed gp value. When using harvested material as a spell component, 1 Harvest can replace a material spell component with a gp value no higher than 10 × the creature’s CR (creatures with a CR below 1 are considered CR 1 for this calculation). The harvested material is always consumed by the spell, even if the spell normally doesn’t consume material components. At the GM’s discretion, some harvested material might not be an appropriate substitute for a material spell component. For example, 100 Harvest of wolf hide is theoretically equivalent to the value of the 1,000-gp diamond required to cast the _resurrection_ spell, but a GM can (and likely should) rule that 100 Beast hides don’t have equivalent thematic value.

#### Treasure
Raw harvested materials are valuable and can be sold to interested parties. If monster harvesting is commonplace in a game setting, a GM can rule that harvested materials can be spent or sold in the same way as treasures like gems or art objects (see the **Rewards** section of this chapter). However, in low-magic settings where spellcasters are scarce, the GM may require PCs to find a buyer (or establish a contact with one via the Carousing downtime activity) before they can sell harvested materials from uncommon or particularly magical creatures: 
- Assuming the PC has access to a buyer, a single Harvest is worth an amount of gp equal to 10 × the harvested creature’s CR. When determining the value of harvested materials from creatures with a CR below 1, use the following guidance:1 Harvest from a CR 1/8 creature has a base value of 1 gp and 25 sp. 
- 1 Harvest from a CR 1/4 creature has a base value of 2 gp and 50 sp. 
- 1 Harvest from a CR 1/2 creature has a base value of 5 gp. 
The GM has final say on the value of a particular harvested material given the setting, material, and current market conditions.
### Categories of Harvested Materials
![[categories-of-harvested-materials.webp|right|384]]Harvested materials fall into the following categories. Many creatures can produce harvested materials in almost all of these categories, and some creatures produce harvested materials in only a few of these categories. The GM determines which categories are produced by a creature.

**_Appendage._** This is any extra limb that extends from the core of the creature but doesn’t fit in any of the other categories. This category includes tentacles, antennae, and similar. Appendages can be used to craft armor, weapons, decorative adornments, or various supplementary equipment, such as a hollowed-out eyestalk being crafted into a spyglass.  

**_Bone._** This is typically part of the creature’s structural foundation, like the parts of a skeleton, or hardened protrusions, like claws, fangs, spines, or spikes. Bones can be used to craft armor, shields, or weapons. Smaller bones, such as fangs or claws, can be used as decorative adornments, while bone powder or marrow can be useful in crafting potions. 

**_Hair._** This is the creature’s hair, fur, whiskers, feathers, or other fuzzy or fibrous material that doesn’t fall into the Hide category. Hair can be used to craft clothing, dolls, thread for sewing, or similar. Hair can also be used as decorative adornments. 

**_Hide._** This is the creature’s skin, scales, or other outer layer. Hides are typically used to craft armor, blankets, clothing, or similar items. 

**_Fluid._** This is the blood, venom, or other liquid the creature produces. Fluid can be used to craft potions, oils, pastes, antitoxins, unguents, poultices, or other similar items. A poison harvested from a poisonous creature is almost always an injury poison and works as described in the creature’s stat block, using any associated save DCs, or as described in the **Poisons** section earlier in this chapter or in the Adventuring Gear section of the _Player’s Guide_. A poison harvested in this way has one use per Harvest, and failure to successfully harvest the poison subjects the harvester to the poison’s effects. 

**_Viscera._** This includes the muscle tissue, organs, and other internal body parts of the creature that don’t fit into any of the other categories. Viscera can be used to craft a wide variety of foods, potions, or even containers, such as crafting a waterproof pouch from a creature’s bladder. If the viscera are edible, 1 Harvest can provide enough nourishment to sustain two Small or Medium Humanoids for one day.
