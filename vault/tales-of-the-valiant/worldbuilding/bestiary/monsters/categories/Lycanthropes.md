````rpg stat.category
**Shapechanging Humanoids**. [[Lycanthropes]] can change their [[humanoid]] bodies into the forms of [[Beast|beasts]] or a hybrid of the two. Many variants exist, including rats, bears, boars, and wolves. While in humanoid form, they often take on personality aspects inherent in their bestial sides. While in animal form, they are larger than most others of their kind and demonstrate great cunning and intelligence.

**Cursed Origins**. Lycanthropes are humanoids suffering an ancient curse, which made the first lycanthropes prone to uncontrolled rages and transformed them into beasts, often without remembering the event. These rages were so terrifying—for the victims and the cursed—that the legends remain what most people know of lycanthropes. Newly cursed lycanthropes temporarily suffer the same rages, further supporting the longevity of such legends. Some lycanthropes embrace their terrifying reputation, and others seek to simply live out their lives in peace. A few live among other humanoids, hiding their true selves, but most live with other lycanthropes in distant settlements.

**Controlled Curse**. Most lycanthropes can control their shapechanging, except in times of extreme duress, and retain their memories and personalities while transformed. Cursed lycanthropes, those who became lycanthropes after being bitten, enter uncontrolled rages for only the first few months after being cursed. They eventually learn to control their bestial side. Natural-born lycanthropes (those with at least one lycanthrope parent) don’t enter uncontrolled rages from their lycanthropy.

**Painful Transformation**. Lycanthropy does more than just provoke the savagery of an enraged beast; the transformation itself is painful. Bones and joints crack and pop, muscles stretch and rend, and claws and snouts burst from flesh. Even natural-born lycanthropes aren’t exempt from the pain of transformation. As a result, most lycanthropes have a high tolerance for pain; however, silver overrides much of this tolerance, making it a universal weapon against dangerous lycanthropes.

When a [[Lycanthropes|lycanthrope]] bites a [[Humanoid]] or otherwise passes its saliva into a Humanoid’s wound, it can spread the curse of its lycanthropy to the Humanoid. For the first 30 days after becoming infected, the Humanoid experiences dreams and nightmares related to the [[Beast]] associated with its curse, such as bear or wolf. During this time, the curse can be cured with the remove curse spell or similar magic. After the 30 days has passed, the Humanoid undergoes its first transformation in its sleep, and it spends the evening without control of its body and acting in a way appropriate to a newly cursed member of its type of lycanthropy, as defined in the lycanthrope’s lore. Once the first transformation takes place, the creature can no longer be cured of lycanthropy, except by a [[wish]] spell or similar magic.

A newly cursed Humanoid continues transforming each night and remembers only fragments of these events. Every 7 days, it can repeat the save it made when it was first cursed. When it succeeds on this save three times, it gains full control of its transformation, retains its memories while transformed, and no longer has to make this save, becoming a true lycanthrope.
```
### Specific Lycanthropes
The most common types of lycanthropes are werebears, wereboars, wererats, weretigers, and werewolves. These descriptions apply mostly to natural-born lycanthropes. Those cursed with lycanthropy through a bite retain the personalities and ideals they had before becoming cursed.

**Werebears**. Werebears live in small, tight-knit family units in the forests, mountains, and wild places of the world. Of all the lycanthropes, werebears have the closest connection to nature and the natural world, and many werebears are kindly creatures that protect nature, tend the wounds of animals, and preserve the balance by culling overpopulations. Newly cursed werebears often become inordinately protective of nature, their homes, and their families, lashing out at the slightest perceived threats.

**Wereboars**. Wereboars live in large family groups in forests, grasslands, and foothills. They enjoy the simple pleasures in life, like tearing into a farmer’s field of vegetables or standing firm against a pack of wolves. Wereboars can anger quickly, especially when challenged. Wereboars tend to tear up fields and forest floors in search of food, annoying fey and farmers equally. They have a great fondness for ale and feasting and for taking apart hunting parties that expect to spear normal boar for sport.

**Wererats**. Wererats live in and around cities and towns. Of all the lycanthropes, they are the most intermingled with humanoids, often living as sailors, thieves, musicians, and scouts. When they are part of a family group, they reveal their true nature only when initiating an individual into the family by sharing the wererat curse. Newly cursed wererats often experience uncontrolled fits of kleptomania.

**Weretigers**. Weretigers live solitary lives in forests and grasslands. They take a mate long enough to raise their young, then all go their separate ways. The rarest and most elusive of lycanthropes, weretigers are the most bestial of their kind, spending more time in beast form than in humanoid shape. Newly cursed weretigers often hunt and kill for weeks, as their powerful hunger claws at them.

**Werewolves**. Extremely pack-oriented, hereditary werewolves live in family groups led by a pair of grandparents or great-grandparents. These groups live nomadically, hunting and gathering as they follow migrating game through forests and foothills. Lone werewolves are also quite common, especially among those who gain the curse through a bite, and often seek to create their own packs by spreading the curse. Werewolf lycanthropy is the most widespread and widely known, and to this day, the average person believes there is only one form of lycanthropy: werewolf.
````
